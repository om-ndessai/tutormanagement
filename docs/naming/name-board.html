Naming the platform · 13 October 2026

Thirty-two names to replace “Tutor Portal”

Two batches of sixteen, each with a logo sketch and a check of the US trademark register, the domain registries and, for the second batch, the App Store. The first batch were mostly real words, and nine of them turned out to be taken. The second batch was built the other way round: 123 candidates were screened first, and only the ones that came through are shown.

Decision, 13 October 2026: the platform is Aimtrue. The app's identifiers are renamed with it, and its logo sketch becomes the icon.

Four of the new names are clear everywhere I looked, and seven more have only a small namesake in another field. The logos are sketches to choose a direction from, and the checks are a screening, not legal advice.

Say plan and progress

For any subject and any age. A name that only says tutoring, or only says maths, is too small for where this is going.

Sit beside another name

Every organization keeps its own name and logo, and the platform's appears next to it. A school-sounding name competes with them.

Riverside Tutoring on Pinpoint AcademyRiverside Tutoring on Pinpath

Work as an icon

The mark has to read at 60 pixels in one colour, on a phone's home screen, next to every other app.

Be free to use

No live trademark in software or education, nobody already trading under it in learning, and a domain you can register.

The six I would choose between

Five from the new batch and Aimwell from the first. If you want to keep your pin, Pinpath is the one: it says plan and progress and nobody has registered it. If you want the least risk, Aimtrue has nothing against it anywhere I looked.

Precision, with your pin kept

Seven relatives of Pinpoint and Aimwell. Five keep the pin or the point; all seven are clear of software and education marks.

aimtrue
As an app icon

Aimtrue

Clear

Set the goal. Aim true.

Why it fits
Two plain words that say the whole method: choose a goal and keep your aim honest. It carries Pinpoint's precision and Aimwell's warmth, and “true” is a carpenter's word for straight and level.
The mark
An arrow flying level into a ring, its tip stopping exactly at the centre.
Watch out
It reads a little like a motto. Said aloud it is two words, so some people will type a space.
Trademark register
No live US mark contains AIMTRUE or AIM TRUE in any class.
Domains and use
No app of this name in the App Store, and no education use found on the web. aimtrue.com (2001) shows a placeholder; .io, .co, .org and .net are held. Not registered: aimtrue.app, aimtrue.ai, the education endings, and all eleven .com variants, including aimtrueapp.com and getaimtrue.com.

Pinpoint

Avoid

Know exactly where you are. Aim at where you are going.

Why it fits
It covers both halves of the product: an assessment pins down where a learner stands, and a plan aims at the goal. Everyone knows the word and can spell it.
The mark
Your idea, drawn: the bowl of the P is a target and a pin lands in the centre. Keep the pin short so it survives at icon size. The second version puts the pin and the target on the two i dots.
Watch out
Drop “Academy”: beside an organization's own name it reads as a second school. The word itself is busy in education: Pinpoint Learning (UK maths), Pearson Pinpoint, an older Houghton Mifflin Pinpoint suite and a Canadian tutoring service. It is also a recruiting product and an Amazon service (from memory).
Trademark register
151 live US marks contain the word. 65 are exactly PINPOINT and 35 of those are in the software and education classes. Owners include Google (journalism software), LinkedIn (games), Bose, Stryker and Cognex. The closest is Kelly's PINPOINT, filed in 1994 and still renewed, for software that trains and tests users of office software. Adding “Academy” is unlikely to help, because a descriptive word carries little weight when marks are compared. No live mark is PINPOINT ACADEMY or PINPOINT LEARNING. Abroad there are 60 more exact marks in these classes.
Domains and use
pinpoint.com, .app, .io, .co, .ai, .org, .net, .education and .academy are all registered. So are pinpointacademy.com (2009) and pinpointlearning.com (an advisory firm). Only pinpoint.school, pinpoint.study and pinpointlearn.com are not.

Whatever you have mastered is where you stand to reach the next thing. It works for algebra, Spanish or guitar, it is modest, and people already say “get a foothold in” a subject.
The mark
A flight of steps cut into solid ground, with the learner on the middle one: part of the way up and still climbing.
Watch out
It says progress more loudly than plan, so the tagline has to carry the plan.
Trademark register
No live mark is FOOTHOLD alone in software or education, in the US or abroad. Three neighbours matter. MYFOOTHOLD: two applications filed on 13 June 2026 for an app covering licensure exam preparation and continuing-education course delivery for health professionals. They are pending and have the earlier filing date. FOOTHOLD TECHNOLOGY: registered for database software for non-profits. FIND YOUR FOOTHOLD.: registered for IT training.
Domains and use
Every main ending is taken: foothold.com (1996, no site), .app, .io and .co; foothold.ai, .net and .org are parked or for sale. Start-ups took the variants in 2025 and 2026: a personal-finance app at usefoothold.com, a strategy workspace at footholdapp.com, and footholdlearning.com was registered in June 2026. Not registered: foothold.education, .academy, .school and .study, and footholdlearn.com.

The mark

It is blunt to say aloud, it sounds like the past tense of ring, and “bottom rung” is not a compliment.

RUNG and RUNG FOR WOMEN are registered to the Rung Foundation for career counselling, training and coaching. RUNG for education would sit directly beside that training mark. RUNGS is pending for online games.

Why it fits
A cairn is the pile of stones that marks a trail. Someone who walked it before you built it, each traveller adds a stone, and it tells you that you are still on the route. Plan, guide and progress in one image.

Three stacked stones with a small accent pebble on top. It is the simplest mark on this page and the best at 60 pixels.

How this was checked, and what it does not cover

  • US trademarks. The USPTO's own search, live marks only (registered or pending), for each name and its spaced spelling. Every name shown was also searched in the pseudo-mark field, which catches sound-alikes. 900 live marks were read across both batches.
  • A rate limit, handled. The USPTO limits automated lookups, and part-way through batch two it began refusing them, which made some names look falsely clean. I slowed down to stay inside the limit and re-ran every name. No result on this page rests on a failed lookup.
  • Abroad. Batch one was checked at the EU, UK, Canadian, Australian and Indian offices and at WIPO through TMview. TMview stopped answering automated lookups during batch two, so batch two has no overseas check.
  • Domains. 1,078 lookups at the registries themselves: each name on eleven endings and in eleven common .com patterns such as get-, try-, -app and -learning. “Not registered” says nothing about price: registries charge premium prices for some names.
  • Use without registration. Each registered exact domain was opened to see who is there, batch two was also searched in the App Store, and each shown name was searched on the web. Stepwell shows that this still misses things.
  • Not covered. State trademark registers, logo marks, Google Play, and a lawyer's judgment of how close is too close. A trademark attorney should clear the final one or two names before anything is filed, printed or submitted to a store.

Before you settle on one

  • Hold the domains. For whichever two or three you like, the unregistered .app, .io or .ai and one .com variant cost little to register now and can be dropped later. Names in this batch were being registered by other people within the last few days.
  • Say it aloud. Tell three parents the name over the phone and ask them to type it. Waypin, Aimtrue and Sopan are the ones to test.
  • Decide before the app is released. The platform's name and tagline are one constant, PLATFORM_BRAND, and cheap to change. The mobile app's identifier com.tutorportal.app and its tutorportal:// link scheme are fixed once the app is in a store, so the name should be chosen before the pre-production build goes out to people.

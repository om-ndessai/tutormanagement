# Points, badges, rewards and certificates in tutoring centres, tutoring software and school reward systems (as of 21 October 2026)

Scope: US focus (UK only for TutorCruncher). Classroom quiz games (Kahoot, Blooket, Prodigy), market sizes, Mathnasium's franchise numbers and the research on gamification and overjustification are already in `research_notes/Math challenge platforms market/` (`classroom_quiz_games.md`, `market_business_regulation.md`, `rating_engagement_mechanics.md` section 5) and are not repeated here. Method note: mathnasium.com and kumon.com pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so facts about individual Mathnasium and Kumon pages come from search-engine extracts of those pages, not full reads; centre pages are franchisee marketing, mostly undated, and vary by location. Pages read in full: The Christian Science Monitor (1992), The Conversation (2019), LiveSchool's Classcraft comparison, wise.live.

## 1. How the big tutoring franchises reward students (Mathnasium, Kumon, Sylvan, Huntington, Eye Level, RSM, Best in Class, Club Z, Varsity Tutors)

### Takeaway
The franchises split into two camps. Mathnasium and Sylvan run in-centre token economies: stamps, punches or tokens for pages done, mastery checks and effort, spent in a prize cabinet or store that runs from candy up to game consoles and gift cards, paid for by the centre. Kumon and RSM instead recognise achievement once a year with certificates, medals, trophies and ceremonies: Kumon for working ahead of grade level, RSM for outside contest results. Huntington's founder rejected tokens on principle (1992). For Eye Level, Best in Class, Club Z and Varsity Tutors, no everyday student reward system was documented.

### Cited Findings
**Mathnasium (prize cabinet, stamp/punch cards, set by each centre)**
- Students get a rewards card that instructors stamp each time they complete a page of math "error-free"; completed cards are traded for items from the centre's "Rewards Cabinet" — [Mathnasium Seal Beach, "Rewarding Students is Part of the Program"](https://www.mathnasium.com/sealbeach/news/rewarding-students-is-part-of-the-program); [Mathnasium Newbury Park, same title](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/newburypark/news/rewarding-students-is-part-of-the-program-1413634187)
- What earns credit: completing assigned Learning Plan pages, passing Mastery Checks, and at some centres achievements outside the centre such as a good grade on a school test or quiz. Mastery on a topic earns a spin on a rewards wheel that can add stamps — [Mathnasium Seal Beach, "Earning Rewards at Mathnasium"](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/sealbeach/news/earning-rewards-at-mathnasium); [Mathnasium El Segundo](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/elsegundo/news/how-mathnasium-el-segundo-helps-motivate-students)
- The price of a card falls as the level rises. At Littleton, a first-level card needs 24 stamps ("equal to about a dollar"), while levels 5 and up need only 10 — [Mathnasium Littleton](https://www.mathnasium.com/littleton/news/how-mathnasium-littleton-helps-motivate-students)
- Other centres use other conversions. One converts stars to points by "Mathlete level" (at level 3, 15 stars = 1 point), with rewards from 1 point (candy, slime) to 85 points (a quadcopter drone). Another counts each Prescriptive page as 1 punch and each Mastery Check as 4 punches plus a prize-wheel spin. Top prizes have included a Nintendo Switch at 350 punch cards, and cabinets are restocked to demand ("Squishmallows and Pokémon cards") — [Mathnasium Spokane South and sibling "How Mathnasium [centre] helps motivate students" pages](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/spokanesouth/news/how-mathnasium-spokane-south-helps-motivate-students); [Mathnasium Cedar Rapids](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/cedarrapids/news/how-mathnasium-cedar-rapids-helps-motivate-students)
- Prize ranges reported include "mystery bags" of candy and small toys up to an iPhone, Beats Pro headphones and Nintendo Switches; elsewhere, board games, plushies, LEGO, video games, an Xbox/PlayStation, Funko Pops and Tech Decks — [Mathnasium centre pages via search (Littleton/El Segundo set)](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/elsegundo/news/how-mathnasium-el-segundo-helps-motivate-students)
- East Wichita: stars for binder pages, passing Mastery checks and completing learning plans, spendable on snacks, toys and $10 gift cards. A student whose referred friend enrols for at least 4 months gets 50 Star Cards — [Mathnasium East Wichita](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/eastwichita/news/students-earn-rewards-mathnasium)
- Cash-like extras at some centres: a $100 gift-card drawing for 100% on a mastery check or a school test or quiz, and at one centre a drawing for a $100 bill for an A (90%+) on a math test — [Mathnasium centre pages via search](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/ahwatukee/news/student-incentive-100-for-100); Wikipedia summarises the system as work counting towards prizes plus "drawings for bigger prizes, such as gift cards" — [Wikipedia: Mathnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathnasium)
- **Digital cards and a digital cabinet.** Some centres give "punches" on a "digital rewards card" for completing a page, mastering a topic or "exemplary behavior", and students may give up a physical prize to donate their rewards to a monthly charity — [Mathnasium Short Pump](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/shortpump/news/mathnasium-students-give-back-shortpump); [Mathnasium McLean 2024](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/mclean/news/mathnasium-students-give-back-2024). Costa Mesa launched a "digital prize cabinet" (2020): students browse and order from home, "with parent permission", after checking with an instructor how many cards they can spend; the page also points parents to a student or parent LiveSchool account to check the card total by phone — [Mathnasium Costa Mesa, "New Digital Prize Cabinet"](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/costamesa/news/new-digital-prize-cabinet-98204726). No source documents an official company-wide link between Mathnasium and LiveSchool.
- Company framing: "students earn tokens for completing their daily goals, and earn punch cards for completing each page of coursework", and the rewards help kids "feel even better about the work they are doing and to be even more motivated" — [Mathnasium Glendale FAQ](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/glendale/news/faq-why-do-we-offer-rewards-amp-incentives-at-mathnasium)

### Inferences
- Mathnasium is the closest precedent for a "rewards shelf". It is a per-centre catalogue set by the owner, priced in a centre currency, with card costs scaled by level so younger or slower students are not disadvantaged, and redeemed on request with a parent's say for online orders. The differences from Aimtrue's plan: Mathnasium pays for its prizes and they are physical or cash-like (gift cards, $100 drawings, consoles). Aimtrue's plan is non-cash and written by each organization ("choose the next lesson's game", certificates), which is nearer the intangible privileges in school stores (section 3).
- What gets rewarded in the token-economy camp (Mathnasium, Sylvan) is the volume of work completed and effort, with mastery as a bonus. Sylvan explicitly decouples tokens from correctness. That matches Question Quest's split of a point per attempt plus a point for being right.
- Recognition in the Kumon and RSM camp is tiered and public (ceremonies, local press, trophies) but tied to milestones. That is a template for Aimtrue's printable certificates: named tiers, a dated milestone, handed over by the tutor.
- The token economies have drawn a long-standing objection from inside the industry (Huntington, 1992). Its modern echo is the school criticism in section 3.

### Gaps
- The Mathnasium and Kumon pages could not be read in full (HTTP 403). The figures above come from search extracts of franchisee pages and are mostly undated.
- No Reddit or parent-forum threads on Mathnasium or Sylvan prizes surfaced; the searches returned only centre pages. No independent parent survey on franchise prizes was found.
- No documented outcome data (retention, attendance) for any franchise reward system.
- Who pays inside the franchise (the centre's budget, or a franchisor-supplied catalogue) is not documented; the evidence implies each centre stocks its own cabinet.
- Varsity Tutors: no student-facing points or badges were found. Eye Level US centres: no everyday reward system documented. Kumon US centre counts and ASHR participation rates in the US were not found.

## 2. Do tutoring-management software products offer student gamification? (feature-gap matrix)

### Takeaway
None of the mainstream tutoring and lesson business-management products (TutorBird, Teachworks, TutorCruncher, Oases Online, My Music Staff) documents points, badges, streaks or a rewards store for students. What they offer on the student side is portals, homework or practice logs and tutor-written lesson or progress reports. Studio software (Pike13, Jackrabbit Class) has skill tracking that parents can see, and Jackrabbit adds a printable progress report. The only product found with gamification is Wise (in-session leaderboards, with badges claimed by listings). A music-lesson platform outside the list, TeacherZone, has full points, badges and leaderboards. A rewards store, redemption and certificates tied to tutoring records is a gap in this category.

### Cited Findings
- **TutorBird:** a student and parent portal with schedule, resources, study log, homework upload (with time taken and notes or questions), self-scheduling, payments and reviews; no gamification found — [TutorBird Student Portal](https://www.tutorbird.com/?p=5213)
- **My Music Staff** (a sibling of TutorBird; the two sites share page IDs and guides): a Practice Log with timer and uploads that teachers review and comment on, and a MeloFlow integration that logs practice time automatically; no badges or stickers found — [My Music Staff Student Portal](https://www.mymusicstaff.com/student-portal/); [Practice Log Communication](https://www.mymusicstaff.com/?p=45855)
- **TutorCruncher (UK):** lesson reports after each lesson, optional or mandatory, so "you, Tutors and Clients" can follow a student's progress, plus progress reports to clients; clients are prompted to rate tutors; no student gamification found — [TutorCruncher Help: Reports](https://help.tutorcruncher.com/en/articles/8225249-reports); [Clients & Students](https://help.tutorcruncher.com/en/articles/12960845-clients-students); [Software Advice profile](https://www.softwareadvice.com/music-school/tutorcruncher-profile)

- **Teachworks:** no documentation of student points, badges or rewards was found (its features page returned 404) — [search for Teachworks gamification returned only a different product, TeacherZone](https://help.teacherzone.com/knowledge/gamification-overview-points-and-badges)

### Inferences
- The gap is specific: business-management tutoring software stops at reports, while engagement products (Wise, TeacherZone, and the school systems in section 3) have no tutoring billing, pairing or curriculum. Franchises fill the gap by hand (paper cards, centre cabinets) or with school tools (LiveSchool). A rewards shelf tied to the tutor's own review, visible to guardians and redeemed through a tutor's "given" mark, has no direct equivalent among the tutoring products checked.
- Jackrabbit's "display at home" progress report and Pike13's parent-visible skill programs are the nearest precedents for parent-facing progress and certificates in this category.
- TeacherZone's leaderboard visibility setting (own, my teacher's students, whole school) is a ready model for an opt-in leaderboard with a narrow default.

### Gaps
- Teachworks and Oases Online were checked only through listings and search; their help centres were not read in full.
- No vendor in the list publishes adoption or outcome data for gamification. Wise's badges are claimed by listings, not shown on its own page.
- Other tutoring CRMs (Tutorful, LearnCube, Lessonspace, Bobbin, TutorOcean) were not checked.

## 3. School behaviour-points and reward-store systems (ClassDojo, Classcraft, LiveSchool, PBIS Rewards, Hero, Kickboard)

### Takeaway
School systems are the mature market for points and stores. Staff award points for expected behaviour. Points are redeemed in a school-wide or teacher store for tangible items or, often, privileges (early dismissal, a VIP party, a homework pass). Parents follow points and purchases in a free family app. Schools pay per staff member (LiveSchool $49-$99 per staff per year; Hero quoted per district), while ClassDojo is free and sells families a Plus subscription. Consolidation has been heavy: Classcraft was bought by HMH in 2023 and its behaviour side dropped, and Kickboard was bought by PowerSchool (deal announced 2021). The criticism is sustained, centred on ClassDojo: public point displays that shame, compliance over self-regulation, datafication, and behaviour records kept without consent.

### Cited Findings
**ClassDojo**
- Free to teachers and families, and every family can view their child's points. ClassDojo Plus is an optional family subscription ("prices vary—check the app") — [ClassDojo Help, Plus FAQs](https://help.classdojo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018137732). Reported prices conflict: $4.99/month or $59.99/year [2024, secondary] — [Vator, 23 Aug 2024](https://vator.tv/2024-08-23-how-does-classdojo-make-money/); others report $7.99 or $9.99/month — [ClassDojo pricing aggregator](https://subger.com/en/service/classdojo)
- Redemption: a teacher redeems a student's class points (the point bubble must be set to "combined"). Redeemed points leave the bubble, and the amount and note appear in the teacher's report but "will not be visible on parent or student reports"; schools can also run School Points — [ClassDojo Help, How to Redeem Class Points](https://help.classdojo.com/hc/en-us/articles/205647205-How-to-Redeem-Class-Points); [School Points section](https://help.classdojo.com/hc/en-us/sections/29011293863309-School-Points)
- With Plus, parents give "home points" (chores, brushing teeth), which teachers do not see, and kids redeem them for rewards that parents add or delete — [ClassDojo Help, Plus: Use Rewards to Redeem Home Points](https://help.classdojo.com/hc/en-us/articles/14784730551053-Plus-Use-Rewards-to-Redeem-Home-Points)
- Criticism: Manolev, Sullivan and Slee (Univ. of South Australia) argue that public score displays can "humiliate or shame students", that competition creates "conflict and unease", that tracking is "a way of promoting compliance and conformity" which "erodes trust", that "Controlled students get little chance to learn how to make sensible decisions about their own behaviour", and that ClassDojo "collects and retains all recorded data on students" in profiles that could "follow students through school" — [The Conversation, 30 Jan 2019](https://theconversation.com/digitally-tracking-student-behaviour-in-the-classroom-encourages-compliance-not-learning-110181)
- Manolev's PhD (2022) finds the app promotes "datafication, surveillance, and behaviourism" and "a narrow, datafied form of school discipline within which control is the underlying imperative" — [University of Adelaide repository](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/a2fbd345-df20-452c-b582-165c78dabbb1); [EducationHQ](https://educationhq.com/news/classdojo-harms-teachers-behaviour-management-approach-researcher-188825/)
- An HCI study interviewing 20 K-8 teachers notes that ClassDojo lets teachers "tokenize, quantify, and document student behavior" and situates it in work linking such tools to panoptic surveillance — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10333189)
- 2014 (New York Times, as reported in 2015): critics objected to "carrot-and-stick" digital discipline and a behaviour database built without parental permission. ClassDojo replied that a "permanent record" idea "horrifies us", began deleting data after a year and created a privacy centre — [WBEZ, 1 Apr 2015](https://www.wbez.org/note-to-self/2015/04/01/half-the-teachers-in-america-use-one-app-to-track-kids); [WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/classdojo-privacy-schools/); [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/classdojo-to-discard-student-data-after-a-year/334807/)

**Classcraft (status after acquisition)**
- HMH acquired Classcraft in May 2023 and said existing contracts would be honoured — [PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/3AS28W). It relaunched as "HMH Classcraft", a K-8 ELA and Math instructional product with "standards-aligned lessons, real-time insights and GenAI capabilities" — [HMH press release](https://www.hmhco.com/about-us/press-releases/hmh-launches-immersive-and-dynamic-learning-experience-with-hmh-classcraft); [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/how-to/what-is-classcraft-and-how-can-it-be-used-to-teach-tips-and-tricks)
- A competitor says the original "has recently been discontinued" and that the HMH version "no longer includes behavior management components" (LiveSchool blog, undated, © 2026; a vendor's claim) — [LiveSchool vs Classcraft](https://www.whyliveschool.com/blog/liveschool-vs-classcraft-comparison-guide). Earlier, IES funded an efficacy study of Classcraft's gamified classroom management — [IES award](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/initial-efficacy-study-classcraft-gamified-approach-classroom-management)

**LiveSchool**
- Official pricing: Starter $1,960/year ($49 per staff per year for 40 staff), Premium $3,960/year ($99 per staff), District custom. Plans are billed by staff count, not students, and parent access and take-home behaviour reports are included — [LiveSchool pricing](https://whyliveschool.com/pricing); [LiveSchool Help, pricing and packaging](https://help.whyliveschool.com/en/articles/10370335-liveschool-pricing-and-packaging)
- Rewards can be tangible (candy, pop sockets) or intangible privileges (early dismissal), through a school store run by administrators or teacher stores, plus "point goal" incentives (a number of points within a time window) — [LiveSchool Help, Running your rewards system](https://help.whyliveschool.com/en/articles/6313366-running-your-rewards-system). Points can also buy "access to the VIP party", and weekly recap emails go to students and parents — [LiveSchool vs Classcraft](https://www.whyliveschool.com/blog/liveschool-vs-classcraft-comparison-guide)

**PBIS Rewards**
- Students earn points for meeting behaviour expectations and redeem them in the school store for tangible and intangible rewards; staff track the store's inventory — [PBIS Rewards, What is PBIS Rewards](https://www.pbisrewards.com/blog/what-is-pbis-rewards/)
- A free Family App (linked by a school-issued QR code; several children across schools) shows points earned, items and privileges bought, events registered and, if the school uses the Advanced Referral System, discipline referrals — [App Store: PBIS Rewards Family](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1035388252); [Lewis Central parent guide](https://lchs.lewiscentral.org/uploaded/PBIS_Rewards_for_Parents_Guardians.pdf)
- Price: a directory lists "starting $120/year" (unverified; quote-based) — [SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/software/pbis/usa/). Its help documentation is now hosted under Navigate360 — [Navigate360 help](https://navigate360.helpjuice.com/en_US/Feature_Function_Overview_PBISR/how-it-works-teacher-rewards-new-pbis)
- In practice: students get tickets for meeting expectations and redeem them at a weekly store (La Vista Middle School, NE, April 2025) — [Papillion La Vista Schools](https://www.plcschools.org/our-district/district-news/news-details-page/~board/2024-25-district-news/post/la-vista-middle-schools-pbis-store-rewards-positive-behavior-4162025). The national PBIS Center publishes guidance on "Using Rewards within School-wide PBIS" — [PBIS.org](https://www.pbis.org/resource/using-rewards-within-school-wide-pbis)

**Hero (SchoolMint) and Kickboard (PowerSchool)**
- Hero: staff record behaviours or tardies "in seconds", assign points or disciplinary actions and "automatically communicate with parents"; district pricing on request — [SchoolMint Hero FAQ](https://schoolmint.com/faq-schoolmint-hero/); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/schoolmint-hero). Capterra lists "$6 per user per month" under a listing titled only "Hero" (unconfirmed as SchoolMint's) — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/128619/Hero/)
- Kickboard (New Orleans, launched 2009; SEL, PBIS, MTSS): PowerSchool agreed to acquire it in November 2021 (terms undisclosed) to fold it into its Unified Classroom, and later confirmed completion — [THE Journal, 12 Nov 2021](https://thejournal.com/articles/2021/11/12/powerschool-to-acquire-sel-provider-kickboard.aspx); [EdWeek Market Brief](https://marketbrief.edweek.org/marketplace-k-12/k-12-dealmaking-powerschool-acquires-behavioral-management-system-ets-snaps-kira-talent/)

**The psychological critique, beyond ClassDojo**
- Alfie Kohn's *Punished by Rewards* (1993) argues that grades, gold stars and incentive plans secure short-term compliance but weaken intrinsic motivation — [Blinkist summary](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/punished-by-rewards-en). (The experimental evidence on overjustification and gamified leaderboards is in `rating_engagement_mechanics.md`, section 5.)

### Inferences
- The school market shows the parts Phase 45 plans: an organization-written catalogue (LiveSchool and PBIS stores, ClassDojo Plus's parent-defined rewards), privileges as rewards rather than goods, point goals within a time window (the same shape as a streak or a badge), redemption done by a staff member, and a family app showing points and purchases.
- ClassDojo's choice to hide redemptions from parent and student reports is one option. PBIS Rewards does the opposite and shows purchases in the Family App. Aimtrue's "redemption requests a tutor marks as given" should decide explicitly which way it goes.
- The criticism is aimed mostly at public display and behaviour control. Aimtrue's private points, visible only to the student, guardians, current tutors and admins, an opt-in first-name leaderboard, and points for answering and for correctness rather than for behaviour, avoid the strongest objections. A leaderboard, even opt-in, is the part that draws the "shame and competition" critique.
- The behavioural-points vendors are consolidating into SIS and curriculum suites (HMH, PowerSchool, SchoolMint, Navigate360). A small independent tutoring organization is unlikely to adopt them, which keeps the tutoring gap open.

### Gaps
- No official HMH end-of-life date for original Classcraft was found; "discontinued" rests on a competitor's blog plus HMH's 2023-24 access statement.
- Current official ClassDojo Plus US price, and PBIS Rewards per-student pricing, are not published.
- No rigorous outcome studies of school stores were found in this pass; the evidence beyond PBIS guidance is practitioner material.
- No 2025-26 ClassDojo usage figures were captured here.

## 4. Tutor and teacher gamification (Varsity Tutors, Wyzant, Preply, Cambly, Outschool, Khan Academy, PBIS Rewards)

### Takeaway
Tutor marketplaces gamify the supply side: they want reliability, responsiveness and revenue. They do it with status badges that raise a tutor's visibility (Preply Super Tutor, Outschool Star and Popular Educator, Wyzant's yearly Top 10%/1%) and with pay that is tiered or carries bonuses (Varsity Tutors raises pay per repeat session; Cambly pays peak-hour bonuses). Tutor criticism focuses on opaque, quick-to-revoke badges and bonuses that are hard to earn. School software also gamifies staff: PBIS Rewards' Teacher Rewards gives teachers points for logging in, rewarding students and peer "spotlights", spent in a staff store. No platform found pays tutors for content-validation work as Question Quest does.

### Cited Findings
- **Preply Super Tutor:** reviewed every month on reliability (consistently showing up), lessons taught, ratings, fast replies and few cancelled or rescheduled trials; students can filter for Super Tutors, and the badge "significantly increases" trial bookings — [Preply blog](https://preply.com/en/blog/helping-learners-succeed-the-story-behind-preply-super-tutors); [Preply Help (FR)](https://help.preply.com/fr/articles/6062191-what-is-a-super-tutor); [Preply Help (IT)](https://help.preply.com/it/articles/6359318-the-super-tutor-program)
- The badge is assessed on the 1st of each month over the last 90 days, and one absence report can remove it, restored automatically if the report is rejected or forgiven. Cancellations and absences also affect search position — [Preply Help, absences and cancellations](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/16556084-how-absences-and-cancellations-affect-your-ranking-and-visibility)
- A tutor's account of losing the badge on a timing technicality and suspecting a ranking drop (Nov 2024) — [Koala Bites newsletter](https://koalabites-newsletter-624e17.beehiiv.com/p/preply-update-november-2024). Recurring topics on r/PreplyTutors include "Preply's business model sucks for teachers?" [dated snapshot] — [GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/PreplyTutors/)
- **Outschool:** a Star Educator needs ACE status (a professional-development programme), at least 100 enrolments (or 250 enrolment payments) in 6 months, a rating of 4.95 or higher, replies to 65% or more of messages within 24 hours, good standing, and a section in the next 30 days; there are also Rising Star and New on Outschool badges, and badges can be removed — [Outschool Support, educator badges](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/8475071-your-guide-to-educator-badges-on-outschool). A "Popular Educator" badge for the top 10% of sellers by 6-month revenue was announced as an experiment in August 2023; it is not confirmed as current — [Outschool, badges experiment](https://teach.outschool.com/platform-update-educator-badges-experiment)
- **Wyzant:** profiles show hours tutored, a star rating and labels such as "Top 10% tutor" or "top 1% … in 2018 … 2024" (yearly); the criteria are not public — [Wyzant tutor profile example](https://www.wyzant.com/Tutors/TX/Houston/10297948/?g=3HGMQGC)
- **Cambly:** base pay $0.17/min on Private+ ($10.20/hour) and $0.20/min on Kids; $2 when a student cancels 12 hours or less before a lesson; referral and peak-period bonuses tied to "Priority Hours" — [Cambly Support, Pay History](https://support.cambly.com/hc/en-us/articles/19971783841037-Payments-Pay-History). Tutors complain that bonuses need "many many hours" and that the rating system punishes cancelling (anecdotal) — [Trustpilot, Cambly](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/cambly.com?page=4)
- **Varsity Tutors:** postings list $18-$40/hour with "incrementally higher pay for each session with the same student, up to $40/hour" — [Mediabistro posting](https://www.mediabistro.com/jobs/2212058880-marketing-tutor); a review site says most tutors earn $15-$20 and are matched by algorithm (conflicts with the posted range) — [SideHusl](https://sidehusl.com/varsity-tutors/)
- **Khan Academy:** energy points measure effort, "not a measure of mastery or ability"; badges run in five tiers (Meteorite, Moon, Earth, Sun, Black Hole) plus Challenge Patches. These are learner-side; no teacher-side gamification was found — [Khan Academy Help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202487710-What-are-energy-points-); some top badges (Ramanujan, Millionaire) were retired in 2020 — [Khan community post](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/tr/community/posts/44414357110669-Badge)
- **PBIS Rewards Teacher Rewards:** staff earn points by spotlighting colleagues for school values, by logging activities, and automatically through "Staff Stars" (logging in, rewarding a student, reaching the daily point goal). They spend them in a separate Teacher Store on jean days, catered lunches or gift cards, or on raffles and events. Admins approve requests and see reports; the feature is included in the base price; students cannot see it — [PBIS Rewards, Teacher Rewards](https://www.pbisrewards.com/features/teacher-rewards/); [How it works](https://support.pbisrewards.com/article/how-it-works-teacher-rewards-new/); [Release notes, Sept 2021](https://support.pbisrewards.com/article/release-8-7-8-8-september-2021/)

### Inferences
- Marketplace badges are reputation signals sold to buyers, so they reward what drives revenue and punish lapses quickly. Within one organization there is no buyer to signal to. A tutor point that is the same for approving or flagging (as Question Quest gives) is closer to PBIS's "Staff Stars" (points for doing the routine task) than to Preply's or Outschool's badges, and it avoids rewarding a verdict.
- PBIS Teacher Rewards shows that schools accept a staff-side store with admin approval. That is a precedent if Phase 45 ever gives tutors something to redeem, though PBIS's gift cards would conflict with Aimtrue's non-cash rule.
- The criticism to avoid is opacity and badges that disappear without warning (Preply). Published, stable criteria (as Outschool publishes) answer it.

### Gaps
- No studies of the effects of tutor-side gamification on tutor behaviour or retention were found. The criticism found is anecdotal: Trustpilot, newsletters, subreddit snapshots.
- Varsity Tutors' internal tutor tiers and any Khan Academy teacher recognition programmes are undocumented in this pass. Wyzant's Top 10%/1% criteria are not public.

## 5. Certificates and physical rewards: how common, and what parents and students value

### Takeaway
Certificates are everywhere and cheap to produce. IXL generates personalised printable certificates automatically at milestones (questions, time, skills scored "excellent") alongside virtual prizes. Kumon pairs tiered ASHR certificates with medals and trophies at ceremonies. Jackrabbit prints a branded skills report for the fridge. The little survey evidence that exists suggests students value private praise and messages home more than tangible rewards, though one survey found adolescents slightly preferred rewards to praise overall. No survey of tutoring-centre parents on certificates was found.

### Cited Findings
- **IXL:** "As students reach new milestones, IXL creates personalized certificates": number of questions practised, time spent, and number of skills with "excellent" scores. Teachers and parents find them in a Certificates center, can get weekly summary emails, can print customisable certificates for "unique accomplishments", and can use a bulletin-board printable pack — [IXL blog, 12 Nov 2021](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/11/12/using-ixl-awards-and-certificates-to-inspire-learning/); [IXL blog for parents](https://blog.ixl.com/?p=16976)
- IXL virtual awards: a treasure-hunt game board per grade for math, and collectibles (stickers, stamps, balloon animals) for ELA, earned for skills and for "time spent, days practiced"; a prize can become the child's avatar. "IXL Rockstar" marks 10,000 questions answered individually or 100,000 by a class — [IXL Awards](https://www.ixl.com/awards); [IXL blog](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/11/12/using-ixl-awards-and-certificates-to-inspire-learning/)
- **Kumon:** tiered ASHR (Bronze to Platinum) with certificates and, at centre ceremonies, medals and trophies, including effort and "life skills" trophies — [Kumon awards page](https://www.kumon.com/resources/what-awards-can-students-achieve-in-the-kumon-program/); [Morgan Hill Times](https://morganhilltimes.com/?p=10009957); [Grimsby Telegraph](https://www.grimsbytelegraph.co.uk/news/pupils-unlocking-key-english-maths-170922)
- **Jackrabbit Class:** a Skills Progress Report carrying the studio's logo, "meant to be displayed at home" — [Jackrabbit, Track Student Skills](https://www.jackrabbitclass.com/?p=34)
- **Student preference surveys:** of 764 students in grades 5-12 in one northeastern US district, rewards were "preferred slightly more than praise" overall and "quiet praise" from teachers was ranked most preferred (2016, *Translational Issues in Psychological Science*) — [Fefer et al., papers](https://independent.academia.edu/SarahFefer/Papers). A 2025 pilot of 55 high-school students in seven states found praise more favourable than rewards, with private praise, "messages sent home to parents/guardians" and get-out-of-work passes preferred — [Contemporary School Psychology, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40688-025-00546-7); [USM Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.usm.maine.edu/usm-faculty-and-staff-scholarship/89)
- Practitioner view (not survey data): what middle schoolers prize "perhaps more than anything is positive communication to the home" — [Edutopia](https://edutopia.org/classroom-management-strategies-part-three); certificates of achievement are recommended for weekly behaviour, exam success or improvement — [School Specialty blog](https://blog.schoolspecialty.com/?p=3582)
- Students given the choice sometimes turn physical rewards into giving: some Mathnasium students donate their rewards to a monthly charity instead of taking a prize — [Mathnasium Short Pump](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/shortpump/news/mathnasium-students-give-back-shortpump)

### Inferences
- IXL is the cleanest template for Aimtrue's printable certificates: generated automatically from recorded milestones, personalised, printable at home, with a weekly email to parents. Aimtrue already holds the equivalents (answers, approvals, levels completed via `nextQuestLevel`).
- The survey evidence (private praise, messages home) supports a quiet "your child earned..." note to guardians, which the email rules would have to allow without free text. It favours that over a public leaderboard, and the evidence that tangible prizes add much beyond recognition is weak.
- Kumon's tiered, dated certificates handed over in person suggest a certificate should name a milestone and a tier rather than a raw point total.

### Gaps
- No survey of tutoring-centre parents (Mathnasium, Kumon, Sylvan) on how they value certificates against prizes was found. Mathnasium's own certificate practice is undocumented in what could be fetched.
- The 2016 study's full ranking tables were not read; only the abstract-level findings above.
- No US data on how often printable certificates are actually printed or shown at home.

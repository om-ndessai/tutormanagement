# Self-paced K-8 math practice products: how they gamify answering questions (as of 21 October 2026)

Scope note: Kahoot, Blooket, Gimkit, Quizizz, Prodigy's pricing and formats, Alcumus, Duolingo and
Brilliant leagues/streaks/XP, Live Mathletics and Elo-style ratings are already covered in

## 1. What each product rewards, how, and what is paywalled (the feature matrix)

### Takeaway
Almost every K-8 practice product pays a currency for correct answers or completed lessons and
lets the child spend it on an avatar or pet (DreamBox, Freckle, Mathletics, Matific, Reflex,
SplashLearn, Boddle, Beast Academy, Prodigy); the "learning" rewards (stars, badges, certificates,
streaks) are tied instead to proficiency or completion, and the most deliberate designs (Khan
Academy's weekly streak, Zearn's lesson badge, Reflex's Green Light, Mathletics' weekly caps)
refuse to pay for effort that does not show learning. Leaderboards, where present, are class-scoped,
weekly, and can be switched off or anonymised by the teacher; the only global boards found are
Mathletics' daily Hall of Fame and Beast Academy's XP leaderboard. Paywalls sit on cosmetics and
pets (Prodigy, Boddle), never on the questions themselves.

#### Khan Academy
- Energy points measure effort, not mastery or ability; earned from practice problems, videos and programming challenges. Badges come in five levels from most common to rarest: Meteorite, Moon, Earth, Sun, Black Hole, plus "Challenge Patches" for finishing certain units, tutorials or activities (via search) — [Khan Academy Help: What are energy points?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202487710-What-are-energy-points-)
- Avatars: some available at sign-up, others become available as energy points are earned or specific activities are done; many avatars have "evolutions" unlocked by more energy points (via search) — [Khan Academy Help: What are energy points?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/202487710-What-are-energy-points-). A fan wiki lists one avatar line's stages at 10,000 / 50,000 / 100,000 / 250,000 energy points (unofficial, unverified) — [Khan Academy fandom wiki: Starky](https://khanacademy.fandom.com/wiki/Starky)
- Mastery: each skill moves Familiar → Proficient → Mastered; each skill is worth 100 mastery points, Familiar earns 50; quizzes, unit tests and course challenges move levels up or down — [Khan Academy Help: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631). Until September 2018 the Course Challenge counted for 20% of course mastery points; that was removed (community post, 2018) — [Khan community: Mastery Mechanics Changes September 2018](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/360023249611/comments/360003245051)
- "Proficient" on a practice exercise needs every question right, or recovery from a single miss via a bonus question offered at the end (a right bonus answer erases the miss); no bonus is offered when a teacher assigns the same set to all students (via search; help-centre article, locale URL) — [Khan Academy Help (article 35916723756685)](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/articles/35916723756685)
- Streaks (launched with "levels", blog dated 7 August 2024, updated 20 March 2025): weekly; a week counts when a learner gets at least one skill to Proficient or higher. The team considered renewing streaks by watching a video or attempting an exercise and chose proficiency instead, citing (undescribed) efficacy research. Levels break a course into smaller milestones, each ending in a "level up" celebration. No outcome data reported; no streak freeze mentioned — [Khan Academy blog: New Streaks and Levels features](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=17224)
- Current rule in the "reimagined" experience: week runs Monday 12:00 AM to Sunday 11:59 PM Pacific; proficiency from exercises, unit quizzes or unit tests counts; the streak resets if no new skill reaches Proficient that week; the count shows under Achievements (via search) — [Khan Academy Help: What is a streak ... reimagined Khan Academy experience](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/46962453239437-What-is-a-streak-and-how-do-I-keep-it-going-in-the-reimagined-Khan-Academy-experience)
- "Reimagined" Khan Academy (announced 8 October 2025, updated 7 January 2026): practice "meant to feel like play, with motivations and celebrations"; Khanmigo embedded as a "learning coach" for students who get stuck; teachers get "actionable insights" instead of reports; district pilots, full district rollout for back-to-school 2026. The post names no specific points/badge/leaderboard mechanics — [Khan Academy blog: Motivation Meets Mastery](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=20367). Teacher access: July 2026 in the USA and several regions, all teachers early 2027 (via search) — [Khan Academy Help: What's changing for teachers](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/46056253778061-What-s-changing-for-teachers-in-the-reimagined-Khan-Academy-experience)

#### IXL
- SmartScore (0-100 per skill): 80 = proficiency, 90 = excellence, 100 = mastery; IXL recommends at least two on-grade-level skills to proficiency per week **[vendor]** — [IXL: The Impact of IXL in Pennsylvania](https://au.ixl.com/materials/us/research/The_Impact_of_IXL_in_Pennsylvania.pdf)
- Above 90 is the "Challenge Zone": gains for correct answers get smaller and drops after misses get larger; IXL tells students who miss a question to read the explanation; "no matter how many questions students miss along the way, they always have the chance to reach mastery" — [IXL SmartScore guide (PDF)](https://www.ixl.com/analytics/smart-score-guide.pdf); [IXL blog: Resetting SmartScores for teachers (2021-07-29)](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/07/29/resetting-smartscores-for-teachers/). A competitor's article (Boddle, 27 July 2026) quotes IXL documentation as "+1-2" for a right answer and "3-8 points" lost for a wrong one in the Challenge Zone, and says 100 takes about 28 questions minimum (competitor source; IXL figure not confirmed first-hand) — [Boddle: Does IXL punish wrong answers?](https://www.boddlelearning.com/article/ixl-punishes-wrong-answers)
- Awards (blog, 12 November 2021): in math each grade has a themed world with a personal game board; each challenge completed unlocks more prizes; hovering over a "?" shows the next challenge; in ELA students complete collections, each achievement level (e.g. mastering ten skills, answering one hundred questions) adds an item; a star appears to click and reveal the prize; awards can be put on the avatar — [IXL blog: Using IXL awards and certificates to inspire learning](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/11/12/using-ixl-awards-and-certificates-to-inspire-learning/). The current awards page describes "hundreds of virtual awards", a "treasure hunt to uncover hidden prizes" in math, and says students "always know just how close they are to winning their next prize" — [IXL awards](https://www.ixl.com/awards)
- Certificates are generated automatically for questions practised, time spent and number of skills with "excellent" scores; teachers see all students' certificates in a Certificates center and can get them by weekly email; custom certificates exist. "Rockstar" recognition at 10,000 questions per student or 100,000 per class, featured in a weekly "Rockstar Roundup" (2021) — [IXL blog 2021](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/11/12/using-ixl-awards-and-certificates-to-inspire-learning/)
- No IXL streak or leaderboard was found in these sources.

#### Beast Academy Online (Art of Problem Solving)
- Up to 3 stars per lesson: 1 = proficient and ready to move on, 3 = expert; the map shows the best attempt; each lesson has three problem sets, so a lesson can be retaken three times with new problems; later lessons stay locked until earlier ones earn stars; a monster icon marks enrichment lessons; a chapter test unlocks after at least one star on all but two non-enrichment lessons; a bonus "trophy problem" opens only after 3 stars — [Beast Academy Help (1798922)](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798922)
- Beast Bucks are earned by stars, trophies and gaining XP levels and are spent in "Beast Builder" on avatar parts; the bar shows "Available" and "Total"; parts unlock by XP level (eyes 1, mouths 5, legs 8, arms 11, heads 14, ears/horns 17, hair 20, tails 23, wings 26); parents can turn Beast Builder off (on by default) — [Beast Academy Help: Beast Builder and avatar overview](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798927-beast-builder-and-avatar-overview)
- A "Ranking" button opens a Leaderboard of students who earned the most XP "in the given time period", shown with avatar and username; the Puzzle Lab unlocks at XP Level 10 under some lock schemes — [Beast Academy Help: Navigating Beast Academy Online](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798920-navigating-beast-academy-online). How XP is earned and whether a child can hide from the leaderboard were not documented.

### Inferences
- Three reward currencies recur and are kept apart: (a) a spendable currency for effort or correct answers (coins, Beast Bucks, M Coins), (b) a learning signal tied to proficiency (stars, SmartScore, mastery levels, badges per lesson), and (c) a lifetime total (Beast Academy "Total" vs "Available", IXL Rockstar counts). Question Quest's two point streams (attempt, and correct-and-approved) fit this pattern; a spendable balance for Phase 45's rewards shelf should be separate from the lifetime total shown on a certificate.
- Caps and gates against grinding are the industry's answer to points that reward volume: Mathletics' 300-points-per-activity weekly cap, Reflex's store that opens only after accurate work, Khan's choice to renew streaks by proficiency, not by watching or attempting. Phase 44's streak should count a modest daily/weekly goal of answers, not raw volume.
- Weekly is the dominant streak cadence for school-tied products (Khan's weekly streak, Zearn's weekly three-lesson goal, Mathletics' weekly certificate, Matific's weekly StarMaster and Weekly Quest, Reflex's "3 Green Lights a week"). A tutoring cadence (one or two lessons a week) fits a weekly streak better than Duolingo's daily one.
- Leaderboards in K-8 are class-sized and weekly with teacher off/anonymise switches (Matific), or rank classes by AVERAGE per student (Mathletics); this is the template for Phase 45's opt-in leaderboard.
- Paywalled cosmetics visible in a classroom are the one mechanic that drew a regulatory complaint (Prodigy). An organization-written rewards shelf should never sell items or show who paid.

## 2. Flow and feedback: one question at a time, immediate vs delayed feedback, what follows a wrong answer, and feedback-free designs

### Takeaway
Every practice product found gives immediate right/wrong feedback, and most follow a miss with a
second try, a hint, a broken-down "Boost" or a worked explanation; withheld feedback appears only in
assessments (IXL's diagnostic shows right/wrong but never the correct answer; i-Ready fixes an
answer once submitted). No K-8 practice product was found that is deliberately feedback-free in
the way Question Quest is ("Saved"), so Aimtrue's design is closest to a diagnostic, not to
practice. Research on feedback timing is mixed: delayed feedback is not worse in a large
multi-class experiment, but learners prefer immediate feedback.

### Cited Findings
- **Beast Academy:** immediate feedback on lesson problems except games; two attempts per problem; a hint after the first wrong try; the correct solution after the second; parents can see every solution in Parent Reports — [Beast Academy Help: How to find solutions](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1890360-how-to-find-solutions-to-beast-academy-online-problems)
- **Zearn:** a wrong answer in Tower of Power triggers a "Boost" that breaks the question into smaller steps with more supportive manipulatives, then a fresh problem; after several failed attempts the teacher gets a "Tower Alert" (via search) — [Zearn Help: Tower of Power](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236239188). A Trustpilot reviewer says a slip sends you "back to the very beginning of the sequence" (one user, conflicts with Zearn's description) — [Trustpilot: Zearn](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/zearn.org)
- **IXL practice:** immediate feedback after each answer "in the form of SmartScore changes and written explanations" (via search) — [IXL research: SmartScore](https://www.ixl.com/research/smartscore); students who miss should "read the explanation" — [IXL SmartScore guide](https://www.ixl.com/analytics/smart-score-guide.pdf)
- **IXL Real-Time Diagnostic:** students learn whether they were right when they submit, but "won't be able to see the correct answer to wrong questions in the arena"; an "I don't know this yet" button lets them move on — [IXL Teacher Plan: Diagnostic](https://www.ixl.com/materials/us/i_guides/Teacher_Plan_Diagnostic)
- **i-Ready Diagnostic:** clicking Done submits and fixes the answer; arrows move only within reading passages, not back to earlier questions; parents are advised to tell children it is fine to get questions wrong (district guides; whether right/wrong is shown was not stated) — [Edmonds SD: i-Ready script guidance](https://www.edmonds.wednet.edu/our-district/departments/student-learning-assessment-curriculum-instruction/assessment/i-ready/script-guidance-for-administering-at-home); [Family Guide to i-Ready (2021)](https://www.ingenuityproject.org/files/2021/12/Family-Guide-to-i-Ready-2021.pdf)
- **Khan Academy:** a single miss can be recovered by a bonus question at the end of an exercise (via search) — [Khan Academy Help (35916723756685)](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/articles/35916723756685); learners report that using a hint marks the question wrong (user posts, not official) — [Khan community: Hints Are Useless](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/da/community/posts/12621735068813-Hints-Are-Useless); unit tests give immediate feedback, and a learner asked for a setting to REMOVE it so learners would rush less; after a unit test learners see skills improved or dropped but not which questions they missed (community posts) — [Khan community: Unit Tests | Unnecessary Feedback](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/vi/community/posts/4408853955725/comments/4408927600013); [Khan community: Khan Academy Unit Tests](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/7577587971725-Khan-Academy-Unit-Tests)
- **XtraMath:** a third-party review says a correct, in-time answer earns a smiley (a green check within the first 10 seconds) and at 10 seconds the correct answer appears and must be copied to move on (third-party) — [Outils TICE: XtraMath](https://outilstice.com/en/?p=5661)
- **Prodigy:** a wrong answer means the move fails; Common Sense Media found "limited feedback on wrong answers" and hints "inadequate for others" — [Prodigy parent's guide](https://prodigygame.com/blog/parents-guide-to-prodigy); [Common Sense Media: Prodigy](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/prodigy-kids-math-game)
- **Boddle:** a missed problem leads back into practice, sometimes with a quick re-teach, without losing points (company claim) — [Boddle blog (2026-07-27)](https://www.boddlelearning.com/article/ixl-punishes-wrong-answers)
- **Synthesis Tutor:** "Mistakes are expected", with immediate tailored guidance — [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)
- **Learner reaction to penalising feedback (IXL):** Trustpilot reviewers describe a typo dropping SmartScore 10-12 points and children crying as the score fell; a Change.org petition calls the system glitchy (anecdotal, self-selected) — [Trustpilot: IXL](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.ixl.co.uk?page=3); [Change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/the-use-of-ixl-in-the-public-education-system); IXL advises teachers NOT to reset SmartScores when students struggle — [IXL blog: Resetting SmartScores (2021)](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/07/29/resetting-smartscores-for-teachers/)
- **Feedback-timing research (not product-specific):** a 2015 *Review of Educational Research* meta-analysis of feedback in computer-based environments found effect sizes "negatively affected by delayed feedback timing" and smaller in primary and high school — [Van der Kleij et al. 2015 (ACU research bank)](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/86y84/effects-of-feedback-in-a-computer-based-learning-environment-on-students-learning-outcomes-a-meta-analysis). ManyClasses 1 (2021) randomised feedback timing in 38 college classes: overall effect 0.002, no generalisable advantage for immediate feedback, possible modest advantages for delayed feedback in some class types — [ManyClasses 1, AMPPS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/ampps/25152459211027575/). Mullet et al. (2014, engineering course): delayed-feedback students did better on exams, yet students preferred and believed in immediate feedback (via search summary) — [Learning Scientists blog](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2023/3/2)

### Inferences
- Question Quest's "Saved" (no right/wrong) has no direct precedent in K-8 practice; its nearest relatives are adaptive diagnostics (IXL's arena, i-Ready), where the product tells families up front that getting questions wrong is expected. Phase 44 copy should borrow that framing ("you are helping check these questions"), because children used to IXL/Khan/Beast Academy expect a tick or a cross.
- The evidence does not say withheld feedback harms learning (ManyClasses), but it does say learners prefer immediate feedback (Mullet). The cost of "Saved" is motivational more than instructional, which argues for a delayed reveal: show the child their results once the tutor has reviewed (a "results are in" moment), which also gives Phase 44 a natural notification.
- No-going-back is the norm in adaptive assessment (i-Ready fixes an answer on Done); in practice products the miss is followed by help instead. Aimtrue cannot give help without a confirmed key, so the tutor review is the help.
- IXL's experience shows that a visible score that FALLS on a wrong answer is the mechanic children complain about most; Question Quest's never-decreasing points avoid it and should stay that way.

### Gaps
- Whether IXL practice and Khan exercises formally prevent going back to a previous question was not documented in the sources read (it appears implicit in their one-at-a-time flow).
- No primary source found for XtraMath's per-answer feedback (only a third-party review) or for whether i-Ready shows right/wrong.
- No K-8 product found that is deliberately feedback-free in PRACTICE, so learner reactions to that exact design could not be documented; no study with children aged 8-14 on delayed vs immediate feedback in math practice apps was retrieved.

## 3. Error reporting: reporting a wrong question or answer key, rewards for reporting, published volumes

### Takeaway
Khan Academy (and Duolingo) let a learner report "the answer is wrong" from the question itself;
Duolingo is the only product found that publishes volumes (about 200,000 reports a day, 72% "my
answer should have been accepted", roughly 10-15% valid) and it triages reports by a machine-learned
quality score. No product was found that rewards a learner or teacher for reporting, or that tells
the reporter when a fix lands. IXL's nearest feature is a teacher report of the questions students
missed, not a report button.

### Cited Findings
- **Khan Academy:** from the exercise, "Report a problem" → choose "The answer is wrong" → add a description → submit; reports go to the content team, who "can review and fix the problem if needed" (staff replies in community posts) — [Khan community: a answer is wrong](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/7864056638477-a-answer-is-wrong); [Khan community: My answer is right, and it keeps showing that it is wrong](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/es/community/posts/360009213352/comments/360001047251)
- **Duolingo (all courses; published 29 March 2022):** a "Report" flag appears after an answer is checked, with error types that depend on the exercise and whether the answer was graded right or wrong; around 200,000 reports a day; 72% are "My answer should have been accepted", 10% audio, 5% hints; about 15% of "should have been accepted" reports are judged correct and added; a "Report Quality Estimation Tool" scores each report (the reporter, how many others reported the same answer, distance from known answers) so better reports are reviewed first; nothing says reporters are notified or rewarded — [Duolingo blog: How user reports improve course content](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-user-reports-improve-course-content); a related Duolingo post puts the valid share at about 10% (conflicting figure) — [Duolingo blog: How machine learning helps prioritize course improvements](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-machine-learning-helps-duolingo-prioritize-course-improvements)
- **IXL:** no official report-a-problem documentation was found; the "Trouble Spots" report flags a trouble spot after three misses within an item type and shows teachers the actual questions missed (2020) — [IXL blog: 3 popular teacher reports](https://blog.ixl.com/2020/11/04/3-popular-teacher-reports-on-ixl/)
- **Other (non-US or smaller) practice tools with documented flags:** Primary Leap has a "Flag Activity" button on every question page with a free-text box; Magma Maths lets teachers flag a specific student answer from a heatmap — [Primary Leap help](https://help.primaryleap.co.uk/knowledgebase/if-i-think-an-activity-or-answer-is-incorrect-how-do-i-report-it2); [Magma Maths: Report an error](https://intercom.help/magma-maths/en/articles/13890395-report-an-error)
- **Teacher signal of a bad question (not a report):** Zearn's Tower Alerts fire when a student keeps failing, which surfaces a problem item to the teacher indirectly (via search) — [Zearn Help: Tower of Power](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236239188)

### Inferences
- Question Quest inverts the industry pattern: elsewhere a learner reports an error and staff fix it later; in Aimtrue the tutor's review IS the error check, and it is rewarded (one point per review). No competitor rewards reporting, so this is a differentiator worth keeping, with Mathletics-style caps or Duolingo-style quality weighting if review volume is ever gamed.
- Duolingo's 10-15% valid rate is a warning for any child-facing "this question is wrong" button: most reports will be the child's own mistake. If Phase 44 adds a student flag, route it to the tutor's review rather than straight to the console, and never reward the child per flag.
- Telling the reporter what happened (which no competitor documents) is an open opportunity: "a question you answered was approved" is a natural Phase 44 notification and a reason to come back.

### Gaps
- No published report volume, fix rate or time-to-fix for Khan Academy, IXL, Beast Academy, Prodigy, Zearn or DreamBox.
- Whether IXL, Beast Academy, Prodigy, Zearn or DreamBox has an in-question report button could not be confirmed from official pages.
- No product found that notifies or rewards a learner whose report led to a fix.

Tablet support is standard (DreamBox has had an iPad app since 2013 with touch-optimised
manipulatives; Synthesis Tutor ships iPad and Android-tablet apps with read-aloud for under-7s),
but no product was found that publishes a rationale for a full-screen, distraction-free answering
mode.

- Question Quest's full-screen, one-question view is closer to Beast Academy/IXL/Khan practice than to Prodigy/Boddle game worlds; the published criticism of game worlds (time between questions, distraction) supports keeping rewards OUTSIDE the answering screen (a celebration after a set or on Home), not inside it.

### Gaps
- No vendor rationale for full-screen practice was found; Beast Academy's own app status could not be confirmed (search returned unrelated "Beast" apps); app store screenshots for 2025-2026 were not reviewed.

## 5. Published data on which mechanics moved engagement or learning

### Takeaway
Almost all evidence is about USAGE (minutes, lessons, skills) predicting test gains, and almost all
of it is vendor-run or vendor-partnered; the few causal tests of a single MECHANIC are Khan
Academy's 2013 growth-mindset message experiment and Zearn's 2025 PNAS megastudy of teacher nudges,
whose effects were small. No study isolating badges, coins, avatars or streaks in a K-8 math product
was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Khan Academy [vendor]:** about 350,000 students in grades 3-8 (2022-23); those averaging 30+ minutes a week (18+ hours a year) gained about 20% more than expected on MAP Growth; only about 9% reached that dosage; 9-18 hours a year → about 7% more than expected; correlational with controls — [Khan Academy blog: Efficacy Results, November 2024](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18194); [Khan Academy 2023-24 annual report: efficacy](https://2023-2024.annualreport.khanacademy.org/efficacy-results)
- **Khan Academy growth-mindset experiment (causal, mechanic-level):** January-February 2013, over 250,000 visitors randomly shown growth-mindset messages, standard encouragement or nothing above fraction problems (PERTS at Stanford with Khan Academy); growth-mindset messages increased the rate of successfully solving problems "even months after" — [Yeager et al., R&D agenda (2013, PDF)](https://aip.brightspotcdn.com/06/c1/781dd466457ca2098c324e659e78/yeager-et-al-r-and-d-agenda-6-10-13.pdf); [EdSurge 2014](https://www.edsurge.com/news/2014-07-24-growing-a-mindset-with-the-right-words)
- **Zearn teacher megastudy (PNAS 2025; Duckworth, Milkman et al.):** 140,461 teachers with 2,992,027 students randomised to 15 email nudges (planning prompts, tips, goals, "digital swag", celebrity endorsements; teachers also earned raffle tickets per opened email); best nudge (log in for an updated weekly report on your students) +5.06% student lessons completed (3.30% after winner's-curse adjustment); class-data emails +2.26% lasting 8 weeks; any nudge +1.89% average; the effect was "at least 30 times smaller" than researchers and Zearn expected — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/a-simple-way-to-boost-math-progress-in-elementary-school/); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/nudging-teachers-in-a-large-field-study-marginally-boosted-student-math-performance); [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/megastudy-underscores-the-difficulty-in-motivating-math-teachers-to-change-behaviors/)
- **Zearn RCT (RAND, 2022-2024):** 10,000 students in grades 3-5, 64 schools, large urban Texas district: +0.11 SD on MAP Growth (+0.13 for students not proficient at baseline), STAAR positive but not significant; usage differed greatly between years; "Strong" on Evidence for ESSA — [Zearn press release [vendor-published]](https://about.zearn.org/press-releases/evidence-for-essa); [Evidence for ESSA: Zearn](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/?p=1310). Louisiana (JHU CRRE, quasi-experimental): +0.20 SD on LEAP with implementation support — [Zearn: New evidence [vendor]](https://about.zearn.org/insights/zearn-math-effectiveness). Colorado: a CU Boulder professor called the state study's gain "low to modest" and confounded by the return to in-person school — [KUNC, 2025-02-13](https://www.kunc.org/news/2025-02-13/did-zearn-boost-colorado-math-scores-a-study-says-yes-but-an-expert-says-questions-remain)
- **IXL [vendor-commissioned/run]:** JHU CRRE RCT, spring 2023, Holland Public Schools (Michigan): IXL Math students outperformed controls by about 10 points on Star Math — [eSchool News, 2024-01-01](https://www.eschoolnews.com/newsline/2024/01/01/ixl-math-meets-every-student-succeeds-act-essa-tier-1-requirements-in-randomized-control-trial-conducted-by-johns-hopkins-university/); 2022 study of 1,746 grade 3-5 students over 12 weeks: groups meeting "two skills proficient a week" scored higher on MAP — [IXL blog: Two IXL skills a week](https://blog.ixl.com/2022/10/14/the-research-is-in/); Pennsylvania cohorts +2.6 percentage points proficiency (internal, pre/post) — [IXL: The Impact of IXL in Pennsylvania](https://au.ixl.com/materials/us/research/The_Impact_of_IXL_in_Pennsylvania.pdf)
- **DreamBox (Harvard CEPR, June 2016):** nearly 3,000 grade 3-5 students (Rocketship, Howard County); DreamBox recommended 5-8 lessons and 60-90 minutes a week, but students averaged 2.9-4.0 lessons and 35-44 minutes; more time was associated with more progress; researchers could not establish causation; Discovery Education's later "+7.5 points at 60 minutes" is a linear extrapolation **[vendor summary]** — [Harvard CEPR technical appendix](https://cepr.harvard.edu/resource/read-technical-appendix); [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/customized-math-lessons-help-students-learn-research-says/); [Discovery Education: Harvard study summary (PDF)](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/eff-17.5.24-Harvard_Study.pdf)
- **Prodigy [vendor]:** an ESSA Tier 3 (lowest tier) analysis commissioned by Prodigy; 2025 state reports (PA, FL, TX) are internal, select "treatment" schools by usage thresholds and compare to state averages; Iowa grades 5-6: each additional hour answering questions associated with +0.73 ISASP scale points (correlational) — [Prodigy research](https://www.prodigygame.com/research); [Prodigy Pennsylvania efficacy white paper](https://marketing.prodigygame.com/Pennsylvania-Efficacy-Report-White-Paper.pdf); [Prodigy blog: Council Bluffs](https://www.prodigygame.com/blog/council-bluffs)
- **Beast Academy [vendor-partnered]:** Utah Education Policy Center with AoPS, 2024-25, nearly 1,000 high-ability students in 12 Davis School District schools; more minutes, units and days associated with higher end-of-year scores; "very few students logged time without completing content"; associational — [AoPS blog: New study shows Beast Academy Online helps high-ability students](https://artofproblemsolving.com/blog/articles/new-study-shows-beast-academy-online-helps-high-ability-students-excel-in-mathematics)
- **Boddle [vendor-published]:** 2024-25 ESSA Level III study by Instructure researchers, 83 third graders in Alabama; about 59 sessions a year at 81% accuracy; accuracy correlated with end-of-year i-Ready; regular users reported less anxiety learning new concepts — [Boddle: The Boddle Confidence Effect](https://www.boddlelearning.com/article/boddle-essa-study-math-confidence)
- **Reflex [vendor]:** ExploreLearning publishes a Green Light vs NWEA MAP analysis (page refused fetch; figures not retrieved) — [Reflex: Green Light and NWEA MAP](https://reflex.explorelearning.com/resources/insights/reflex-green-light-nwea-map-achievement)
- **Classroom reports on game economies:** Common Sense teacher reviews of Prodigy note some students "more wrapped up in the game feature" than the math, and burn-out by year's end; others say students "beg to play" (self-selected teacher reviews; quotes not matched to individual review pages) — [Common Sense Education: Prodigy teacher reviews](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/prodigy-math/teacher-reviews/4105481); [another Prodigy teacher review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/prodigy-math/teacher-reviews/5033663)

### Inferences
- The industry's own "what works" claim is a weekly dosage target (Khan 30 min/week, IXL two skills/week, Zearn three lessons/week, Reflex three Green Lights/week, DreamBox 5-8 lessons/week), and every study shows most students fall short of it. A weekly answers goal is therefore the most evidence-aligned Phase 44 metric, and the gap between goal and reality (9% of Khan students at dosage; DreamBox students at half the recommendation) says the goal should be small.
- The only large causal mechanic test with adults (Zearn megastudy) found that showing teachers their own students' data beat swag, raffles and celebrity endorsements; for Aimtrue's tutor side, a "your students answered N questions this week, M await your review" nudge is better supported than tutor-facing badges.
- Growth-mindset framing is the one child-facing message with randomised evidence at scale (Khan, 2013); Question Quest's "Saved" screen is a natural place for it.
- None of the vendor studies isolates a reward mechanic, so Aimtrue cannot borrow an effect size for badges, coins or streaks; Phase 44 should be measured (answers per week, return rate) rather than justified by competitor claims.

### Gaps
- No study isolating badges, avatars, coins or streaks in a K-8 math practice product was found; Khan Academy's streak/levels post cites efficacy research without describing it.
- Paunesku et al.'s exact effect sizes (problems attempted/solved) were not retrieved.
- Funding of the JHU CRRE IXL trial and the Harvard CEPR DreamBox study was not confirmed (both are commonly vendor-commissioned; treat as such until checked).
- Reflex, Mathletics, Matific, SplashLearn and Freckle efficacy figures were not retrieved.

## 6. Level progression: automatic vs adult-set, and how "level complete" is celebrated

### Takeaway
Within a level, progression is almost always automatic and gated by demonstrated proficiency
(stars, all-correct towers, consecutive Green Lights, fact fluency); the choice of level or grade is
adult-set in curriculum products (Beast Academy parents, Zearn teachers, IXL/Mathletics teachers) and
algorithmic in adaptive ones (DreamBox, Prodigy, Freckle, Synthesis). Completion is celebrated with
small in-app moments (Khan's "level up", Zearn's badge calendar) and with PRINTABLE certificates
for bigger milestones (Zearn missions, XtraMath operations, IXL milestones, Mathletics tiers, Matific).

### Cited Findings
- **Khan Academy:** levels break a course into milestones; finishing one triggers a "level up" celebration; the Course Challenge lets a learner with prior knowledge accelerate — [Khan Academy blog: Streaks and Levels](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=17224); [Khan community: Mastery Mechanics Changes 2018](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/360023249611/comments/360003245051)
- **Beast Academy:** a parent can change a student's course level at any time; lessons unlock by earning stars, chapter tests after stars on all but two non-enrichment lessons; parents can manually lock and unlock content and choose lock schemes (Sequential, All Chapters) — [Beast Academy Help: Navigating BA Online](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798920-navigating-beast-academy-online); [Beast Academy Help (1798922)](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798922); [Beast Academy Help: Manually lock and unlock content](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1861857-how-to-manually-lock-and-unlock-content). Common Sense Education warns the program "lacks an adaptive re-teaching component" and eager students may jump ahead — [Common Sense Education: Beast Academy](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/beast-academy)
- **Zearn:** completing a lesson's Tower of Power earns a badge and advances the student automatically to the next lesson; teachers can change a student's assignment; Mission completion certificates mark larger units (via search) — [Zearn Help (236238988)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236238988); [Zearn Help: Change your student's assignment](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/115005912007); [Zearn Help: Mission completion certificates](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007508514-Mission-Completion-Certificates)
- **XtraMath:** a 2-3 day placement quiz sets a baseline; harder facts are introduced after the student consistently shows fluency with a set of 10; a certificate when an operation is fully mastered — [XtraMath: What do students do](https://home.xtramath.org/support/what-do-students-do-in-xtramath)
- **Reflex:** new games unlock after a run of consecutive Green Light days, shown on a sign counting the days remaining — [Reflex: unlock games](https://reflex.explorelearning.com/resources/insights/unlock-reflex-games)
- **Prodigy:** questions follow an adaptive algorithm and the classroom's current work — [Prodigy parent's guide](https://prodigygame.com/blog/parents-guide-to-prodigy); one Trustpilot reviewer says a membership was needed to override the grade the child was placed in (single anecdote) — [Trustpilot: Prodigy](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/prodigygame.com)
- **Freckle:** diagnostic pre-test, then adaptive difficulty; coins for completing levels (school documents) — [School on Wheels: Freckle](https://schoolonwheels.org/tutor-resources/academic/academic-program-freckle/)
- **Mathletics:** weekly Bronze certificate at 1,000 points, Silver after 5 Bronze, Gold after 4 Silver — [Mathletics support: Certificates and Points](https://support.mathletics.com/support/solutions/articles/265818-certificates-and-points)
- **IXL:** automatic certificates for questions practised, time and skills at "excellent"; Rockstar at 10,000 questions — [IXL blog 2021](https://blog.ixl.com/2021/11/12/using-ixl-awards-and-certificates-to-inspire-learning/)
- **Synthesis Tutor:** adapts and moves on after demonstrated mastery; no placement test described — [Synthesis Tutor](https://www.synthesis.com/tutor)
- **Alpha School (secondary):** software allows progress to a new level only after mastery of the previous one; 80% vs 90% thresholds conflict across sources — [Austin Scholar #177](https://austinscholar.substack.com/p/austin-scholar-177-the-alpha-app); [Zvi: On Alpha School](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/on-alpha-school)

### Inferences
- Question Quest's automatic move to the next level when one "runs out" is unusual: competitors gate on PROFICIENCY (stars, all-correct, fluency), not exhaustion. Because Quest withholds marking, it cannot gate on proficiency at answer time; once reviews come in, a level-complete moment could still be keyed to correctness (e.g. a certificate when a level is finished AND a share of its answers are approved and right).
- The tutor-set starting level (`quest_level_id`) matches the curriculum-product norm (Beast Academy parent, Zearn teacher); keeping the adult in charge of placement is consistent with the industry.
- Phase 45's certificates have strong precedent: XtraMath (per operation), Zearn (per mission), IXL (milestones, weekly email to the teacher), Mathletics (tiered). The weekly email of new certificates to the adult (IXL) is a cheap Phase 44/45 hook for parents.
- The celebration should be at the level boundary (Khan "level up"), not per answer, which suits a no-feedback flow.

### Gaps
- DreamBox's, SplashLearn's and Boddle's rules for moving a child to the next grade (automatic or teacher-set) were not documented in what was read.
- How Khan's reimagined experience defines a "level" and its celebration in 2026 is not documented beyond the 2024 post.
- No source described what a "level complete" screen looks like in IXL, Prodigy or Matific.

# Parent dashboards: what a parent's signed-in home leads with, and what brings parents back

Researched 2026-11-03. Most product facts come from vendors' own help centres and blogs (primary for "what the screen shows", but marketing for any outcome claim). Older facts are dated inline. App-store screenshots were not reviewed.

## 1. What does the parent see first on each platform, and what do they do on a visit?

### Takeaway
Two kinds of parent home show up. **Tutoring and class businesses** (Outschool, Mathnasium, Kumon, Wyzant, Varsity Tutors) lead with **what is coming up next** (upcoming classes, schedule), then messages, then lesson history and billing. **Self-paced practice products** (Beast Academy, Prodigy, Khan Academy, SplashLearn, IXL) lead with a **recent-window snapshot**: effort (minutes, problems) plus mastery (stars or skills), with a drill-down. The parent acts by assigning work, setting a goal with a reward, or cheering. Only Beast Academy, among those checked, puts a "struggling" alert on the summary.

### Cited Findings

**Self-paced practice products**
- **Beast Academy Online:** Parent Reports opens on "a snapshot of the past two weeks of activity" and the most recent lessons played, with two buttons, **Full Report** and **Manage Account** — [Beast Academy Help: Parent reporting overview](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1793720-parent-reporting-overview)
  - Stars are the primary indicator (1 = proficient, 2 = advanced, 3 = expert, up to 3 per lesson). Trophies unlock after 3 stars.
  - Problems answered correctly are labelled as a measure of **effort**. Time counts only during lessons, Guide reading or videos, and excessive idle time is excluded.
  - An Activity calendar shows minutes played per day.
  - A red exclamation point marks a student who is struggling or quit a test, with a suggestion to review the instructions, missed solutions and linked videos.
  - A lesson drill-down shows each problem, the student's response and the solution.
- **Beast Academy (continued):** the parent account keeps showing progress after the subscription expires. The help centre mentions weekly progress update emails to parents; their contents were not found — [Beast Academy Help: student progress and activity overview](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798926-student-progress-and-activity-overview)
- **Prodigy, monthly Report Card (2020 post):**
  - It sits under "Performance", is available at the start of each month, and the parent is emailed when it is ready.
  - It shows skills practised and mastered per topic and the strongest and weakest areas, and compares with previous months.
  - The 2020 post called it "free for all parents" — [Prodigy blog, 2020-01-21](https://prodigygame.com/blog/learning-progress-support)
- **Prodigy, conflict on whether the Report Card is paid:** later membership material lists monthly report cards, "Cheer Them On" and Practice Sheets as **membership** features. Premium parent features apply only to the child who holds the membership — [Prodigy blog, 2024-12-01](https://prodigygame.com/blog/how-to-use-your-premium-membership). This conflicts with the 2020 post; the feature has likely moved behind the paywall.
- **Prodigy Parent App, Goals:**

  - "Once the goal is complete, the reward will automatically be sent to your child's account" (an in-game reward, in the in-game mailbox). Parents can also "Send a cheer" — [Prodigy Goals & Rewards, updated 2025-08-16](https://prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/goals-rewards-tool)
  - Goals can require up to 120 correct answers. Core gets basic goals with pre-chosen rewards; Plus gets "Premium Goals & Rewards" — [Prodigy membership pages](https://prodigygame.com/blog/choosing-prodigy-membership) (vendor; seen via search summary)

A 2026 review claims "two principal reports" now exist; this is unverified — [Educators Technology, 2026-09](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2026/09/khan-academy-kids-review.html)

**Duolingo**

### Inferences
- A **tutoring** parent comes to ask "when is the next lesson, and has anything changed?" Every tutoring or class product leads with the upcoming schedule, keeps messages one tap away, and puts billing on a secondary tab.
- A **practice** parent comes to ask "is my child doing it, and getting it?" Products answer with a short recent window (Beast Academy: two weeks; Prodigy: one month; Kumon: daily, with a six-month trend) that separates effort from mastery.
- The parent's verbs, by how many products offer them:
  1. view progress (all)
  2. message the tutor or teacher
  3. manage the schedule
  4. assign or set a goal (Khan, Prodigy, SplashLearn)
  5. cheer (Prodigy, ClassDojo)
  6. pay or see invoices
- For Aimtrue, a **tutoring-space parent's home** could lead with:
  1. the next lesson, with any cancellation or change
  2. the latest write-up or homework
  3. the balance
- A **Quest-only parent's home** could show:
  - a this-week card per child: days played against the 3-days streak target, questions answered, level and badges
  - one alert: struggling, or an unreviewed Assessment
  - one action: set a goal, play together, or cheer
- Beast Academy's red "struggling or quit" flag is a cheap, transferable way to surface the one thing that needs a parent.

### Gaps
- No primary source on whether IXL family accounts get an automatic weekly email (IXL material covers teacher-sent reports only).
- Duolingo ABC parent view: nothing reliable found.
- Khan Academy Kids current parent reporting: unverified.
- Khan's Parent Quick Start Guide returned 403, so its exact above-the-fold layout is unconfirmed.
- The consumer (non-school) Varsity Tutors parent dashboard is not documented in public help pages found.
- The contents of the Beast Academy and Khan weekly emails were not found.

## 2. How do they handle several children?

### Takeaway
There are three patterns:
- an all-children overview with one avatar or row per child, then drill-down (Beast Academy; Outschool's home and Schedule tab)
- a profile switcher (Prodigy top-left, Outschool avatar dropdown, Khan's list then per child)
- per-child paywalls (Prodigy)

Overview-first answers "what's happening across my kids" with no taps.

### Cited Findings
- **Beast Academy:** an avatar per student, and "The leftmost icon shows a snapshot for all of your students" — [Beast Academy Help](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1793720-parent-reporting-overview)
- **Outschool:**
  - The homepage and the Schedule tab cover all learners — [Outschool parent guide](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/2086860-how-to-use-outschool-as-a-parent); [Outschool mobile](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/11944008-outschool-mobile-app-support)
  - Each learner has a "learner space" reached by a switcher under the avatar. Returning to parent mode requires solving a multiplication problem by default — [Outschool learner spaces](https://support.outschool.com/en/articles/4715673-learner-spaces)
- **Prodigy:**
  - The parent picks the child's profile in the top-left — [Prodigy Goals, 2025](https://prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/goals-rewards-tool)
  - "Each Premium Membership purchase applies to one child only" — [Prodigy, 2024-12-01](https://prodigygame.com/blog/how-to-use-your-premium-membership)
- **Khan Academy:**
  - Children are listed; a person icon opens a child's Learner Home, the logo returns to the parent view, and "Add a child" adds more — [Khan Support](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/tr/articles/204795300-How-do-I-view-my-child-s-profile)
  - Khanmigo for Parents is priced per family for up to 10 learners — [Khan blog 2025](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=19766)
- **SplashLearn:** has a help article, "How can parents switch child profiles", which points to a per-child switcher (the article returned 403) — [SplashLearn help](https://support.splashlearn.com/hc/en-us/articles/32872622832274-How-can-parents-switch-child-profiles)
- **Brightwheel:** reports are searchable by date and child — [Brightwheel](https://mybrightwheel.com/solutions/family/)
- **Duolingo Family Plan:** separate accounts for each member — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/plus-family-plan). Whether members see each other's progress is unverified.

### Inferences
- A combined home with **one card per child** suits Aimtrue's mixed families (one tutored child, one practising child), with a switcher only for drill-down.
- Outschool's maths-problem gate is a cheap child-lock for a shared device.
- Per-child permissions or pricing (Prodigy) produce uneven homes for siblings; avoid that.

### Gaps
- No data comparing switcher and combined layouts on engagement.
- No layout found for several children in ClassDojo, Kumon or Mathnasium.

## 3. Weekly reports, digests and nudges: do they bring parents back? Numbers

### Takeaway
The evidence is for pushed, short, specific messages, not for dashboards:
- **Results:** automated alerts cut course failures by about 27-40% (depending on the version cited), and one-sentence weekly teacher messages cut credit failure by 41%.
- **Design that works:**
  - improvement-framed messages beat praise-only ones
  - encouragement and context lower opt-outs, while volume and complexity raise them
  - opt-out enrolment keeps about 89% subscribed
- **Where the effects are weaker:** middle school.

No vendor publishes return-visit or open rates for weekly parent emails.

### Cited Findings
- **Bergman & Chan** (Kanawha County, WV; 22 schools; 1,137 students; texts about missed assignments, grades and absences):
  - Course failures fell from 0.97 to 0.71 per student (−27%) and attendance rose 12%.
  - There was no effect on test scores.
  - Over 32,000 texts cost about $63 — [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/print/pdf/node/2353)
  - The working paper says −38% — [CESifo WP 6493](https://www.cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp6493.pdf); NPR quoted about 40% — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/517757199). The published version is in JHR 56(1) — [JHR](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/56/1/125.full.pdf). The figures differ by version.
  - Effects were concentrated in high school and among low-GPA students; for middle schoolers it "didn't make much difference" — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/517757199)
- **Kraft & Rogers 2015** (weekly one-sentence teacher messages, summer credit recovery):
  - The share failing to earn credit fell from 15.8% to 9.3% (−41%), mainly by preventing drop-outs.
  - "What to improve" messages beat "doing well" messages — [HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/underutilized-potential-teacher-parent-communication-evidence-field-experiment-0); [Boston Fed, 2016](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/communities-and-banking/2016/spring/the-underutilized-potential-of-teacher-parent-communication.aspx)
- **READY4K / Tips by Text** (York, Loeb & Doss; SFUSD; 1,031 parents):
  - Parent involvement rose 0.15-0.29 SD and child literacy about 0.11 SD in the published version; earlier versions reported about 0.21 SD — [CEPA WP](https://cepa.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/One%20Step%20CEPA%20Working%20Paper%206%201%2017.pdf); [NBER w20659](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20659.pdf)
  - A vendor summary reports +0.31 for the lower half of baseline skill — [vendor summary](https://solanolibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Ready4K_Research_Summary.pdf)
- **Fricke, Kalogrides & Loeb 2018, "It's too annoying":**
  - "A high quantity of texts and more complex texts lead recipients to opt out at greater rates."
  - Context plus encouragement lowers opt-out — [Tips by Text](https://tipsbytext.stanford.edu/research/its-too-annoying-who-drops-out-educational-text-messaging-programs-and-why)
- **Default opt-out enrolment** (Talk to Your Baby RCT): **88.7%** stayed enrolled over 26 weeks; few enrolled when it was opt-in — [Duke, 2020-10-06](https://childandfamilypolicy.duke.edu/blog/news/a-simple-enrollment-change-yields-big-dividends-in-childrens-early-learning-program-2/); [JCFS article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10826-020-01838-7)
- **Product emails exist, with no outcome data:**
  - Prodigy emails when the monthly Report Card is ready — [Prodigy 2020](https://prodigygame.com/blog/learning-progress-support)
  - Beast Academy sends weekly emails — [Beast Academy Help](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798926-student-progress-and-activity-overview)
  - Khan sends a weekly Parent Report — [Khan community](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/fr/community/posts/4404538388237-Update-Temporary-change-to-weekly-Teacher-and-Parent-Report-email-delivery)
  - Some Mathnasium centers send monthly reports — [Mathnasium Littleton](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/littleton/news/how-do-you-know-what-your-child-is-doing-at-mathnasium-of-littleton)
- **Open rates (higher education and vendor figures only):**
  - A university family-communications program averaged a 49% open rate and a 9% click rate — [CampusESP/UH](https://ssl.uh.edu/dsa/events/uhslc/2023/_files/utilizing-technology-through-engagement.pdf)
  - Education open-rate benchmarks are claimed at 25-30% (2021) — [Benchmark](https://ui.benchmarkemail.com/resources/manuals/education/industry-open-rates)
- **ClassDojo:** no independent figure for the share of parents active.
  - Vendor case studies claim 94-97% of district families connected — [Pittsylvania (vendor)](https://www.casestudies.com/company/classdojo/case-study/pittsylvania-county-schools-connects-97-of-families-with-classdojo)
  - "89% of teachers…" — [ClassDojo one-pager 2025-08](https://static.classdojo.com/img/2025/08/classdojo-1pager-v3.pdf)

### Inferences
- A weekly parent digest that is **on by default with an easy opt-out**, **few and simple**, and **improvement-framed with encouragement**, with one action per child. For example:
  - "Maya played 2 of 3 days; one more keeps her streak."
  - "Arjun's Assessment was reviewed: 2 things to practise."
- The effects are weak for middle school, so for grades 1-8 the digest is better aimed at the parent's role in driving practice than at monitoring.
- Aimtrue's Phase 32 email rule (no notes, money or scores) fits: say what happened and what to do, and link in for detail.
- Only production sends email and self-serve spaces send none, so an in-app "this week" card may matter more in practice.

### Gaps
- No published numbers tying weekly emails to return visits, opens or retention for IXL, Prodigy, Khan, SplashLearn, Beast Academy or ClassDojo.
- Bergman's portal-usage figures were not retrieved.
- No push-notification (as opposed to SMS or email) parent studies were found.

## 4. What parents value, and what irritates them

### Takeaway
- **What parents value:** an honest picture of where their child stands (nearly 9 in 10 think their child is at grade level; about half are not), concrete next steps, and encouragement.
- **What irritates them:** volume (10-15 school apps, continuous feeds), complexity, and screen-time guilt.
- **Design implication:** a calm, summarised, actionable home rather than a data dump or a feed.

### Cited Findings
- **Gallup and Learning Heroes** (about 2,000 K-12 public-school parents, released November 2023) — [The 74, 2024-01-08](https://www.the74million.org/article/when-getting-good-grades-and-working-at-grade-level-are-not-the-same-thing/)
  - 88% (reading) and 89% (math) believe their child is at grade level; about half of students were below at the start of 2022-23.
  - 64% rely on report cards, and 79% report mostly B's or better.
  - 97% of parents who knew their child was below grade level worried about math, against 22% of those who believed the child was at or above it.
- **Earlier Learning Heroes waves:** the 2018 wave also found "nearly 9 in 10" — [Learning Heroes 2018](https://www.HCMstrategists.com/s/LH_ParentsReport2017.pdf). City figures in 2023 were 84-92%, against 8th-grade proficiency of 15-34% — [The 74, St Louis](https://www.the74million.org/article/bridging-the-parent-perception-child-performance-gap-in-st-louis-schools/)
- **Information overload:**
  - Parents juggle 10-15 apps (Cornerstone/Edsby, Dec 2024 to Jan 2025; small sample of 125 parents; a vendor) — [Edsby](https://www.edsby.com/new-research-report-unveils-educational-app-overload-in-k-12-schools/pressreleases/); [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/using-too-many-school-apps-may-burden-parents-teachers)
  - "I just block it out because I can't keep up" — [Boston Globe, 2026-10-05](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/05/lifestyle/parents-schools-edtech-apps-platforms-passwords/)
  - Australia, February 2026: 78% report communication issues and 26% feel overwhelmed by platforms — [McCrindle](https://mccrindle.com.au/media-releases/parents-demand-better-school-communication/)
  - Parents receive about 300 notifications a day (general phone use, a small study), linked to stress — [Michigan Medicine](https://michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/parents-who-frequently-pick-phones-due-device-notifications-may-be-more-stressed)
  - About a third find school email more stressful than their work inbox (year unconfirmed) — [Motherly](https://mother.ly/?p=238954)
- **Message design and opt-out:** volume and complexity drive opt-out; encouragement reduces it — [Fricke et al. 2018](https://tipsbytext.stanford.edu/research/its-too-annoying-who-drops-out-educational-text-messaging-programs-and-why)
- **Brightwheel:** reviewers complain about the undifferentiated feed — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/144060/brightwheel/reviews/?page=72)
- **Screen-time guilt (vendor surveys):**
  - About 74% feel guilty (Lingokids, April 2025, n=1,000) — [Lingokids](https://lingokids.com/press/lingokids-sparks-conversation-around-screen-time-parent-guilt-experienced-by-more-than-74-of-u-s-parents)
  - "84%… feel guilty… yet 98% allow screentime" — [Lingokids](https://lingokids.com/press/the-screen-guilt-paradox)
- **Pew** (May 2025, 3,054 parents of children 12 and under): screens are seen as useful for learning and entertainment, alongside concerns — [Pew topline 2025-10-08](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/10/PI_2025.10.08_Parents-Kids-Screens_TOPLINE.pdf)
- **Beast Academy:** labels problems correct as "effort", separate from mastery stars, and excludes idle time — [Beast Academy Help](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1793720-parent-reporting-overview)

### Inferences
- Answer "where does my child stand against their grade" plainly (Aimtrue has NC-standard and BA levels), plus a next step. Effort alone can reinforce the false "on track" belief.
- Avoid guilt: report minutes against a modest target (the 3-days-a-week streak), and frame a shortfall as one improvement step.
- One summarised home is the opposite of the Brightwheel/ClassDojo feed model.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 independent survey ranking which dashboard elements parents value.
- The Learning Heroes 2023 PDF could not be text-extracted.
- The Common Sense Media 2025 census was seen only via a secondary source (about 75% think screens can support learning — [Substack](https://thatdigitaldad.substack.com/p/what-the-common-sense-media-census)).
- NN/g and Baymard dashboard material was not covered.

## 5. Parent + child co-play

### Takeaway
Joint media engagement research supports parents talking and playing alongside children; questions during co-play lead to richer, more prosocial exchanges. The friction is that young children often resist parent involvement. In products, co-play mostly takes the form of co-operative family goals (Duolingo Family Quest, from an unofficial source) and parent-set goals with rewards and cheers (Prodigy). No product publishes an engagement uplift from co-play.

### Cited Findings
- **Joint media engagement:**
  - Sesame Street research found that children whose parents talked about the show learned more; "joint media engagement" extends this to digital media — [Cooney Center: The New Coviewing](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/publication/the-new-coviewing-designing-for-learning-through-joint-media-engagement/)
  - Its guidance includes "support co-creation", "encourage shared positioning" and "support different roles for partners" — [Cooney initiative](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/initiative/the-new-coviewing-initiative-investigating-and-designing-for-joint-media-engagement/)
- **Co-play studies:** parents' questions during co-play "often lead to prosocial encounters" — [Cooney: Playing Well Together, 2025-05](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/?p=25906)
- **Friction:** "young children often resist parent involvement, causing conflict and discouraging co-play" — [Cooney blog](https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/?p=22597)
- **Duolingo Family Quest (unofficial):** co-operative, up to six members, about 105 lessons (against about 50 for a Friends Quest), a four-day window, nudges and gifts — [Duoplanet](https://newsletter.duoplanet.com/posts/duolingo-family-quest)
- **Prodigy:** goals with automatic in-game rewards, plus "Send a cheer" — [Prodigy 2025](https://prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/goals-rewards-tool)
- **ClassDojo:** parents add encouragement on portfolio items — [ClassDojo](https://www.classdojo.com/portfolios/)
- **READY4K:** gains came from parents doing short activities with the child; parent involvement rose 0.15-0.29 SD — [CEPA WP](https://cepa.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/One%20Step%20CEPA%20Working%20Paper%206%201%2017.pdf)

### Inferences
- A **"Play with <child>"** action on the parent's home, using existing Quest Games with the parent as host and player on the child's team (co-operative, a helper role), fits the joint-media-engagement guidance better than parent-against-child play.
- A **co-operative family weekly target** (everyone's days count) could be derived from `activity_days` like the existing streak, with nothing stored.
- A **one-tap cheer** suits parents who will not co-play.
- Co-play should be offered, not pushed: no guilt nudges.

### Gaps
- No published metric shows co-play or family-quest features raising retention.
- There is no official Duolingo documentation of Family Quest.
- No study compares competitive and co-operative parent-child math play for grades 1-8.

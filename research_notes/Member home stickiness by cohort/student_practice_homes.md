# Learner (student) signed-in home screens of leading kid/teen math and practice apps: stickiness patterns

> Intended path: /Users/ndessai/projects/tutormanagement/research_notes/Member home stickiness by cohort/student_practice_homes.md. Plan mode blocked that write, so the notes are saved here. Researched 2026-11-03. Every item gives its source's date; anything before 2025 is marked [older]. Several help-center pages (Khan Academy, Zearn) returned 403 to the fetcher. Where so, the finding comes from the search engine's snippet of that page and is marked "(snippet)".

## 1. What does each app's signed-in learner home look like, and what is its single primary action?

### Takeaway
Every leading product gives the learner one dominant "do the next thing" control above the fold: Duolingo's current node on the path, Khan Academy Kids' big green play button, Zearn's "Next Up", Khan Academy's Daily Mission, IXL's "Next up" recommendations. Status (streak, points, gems) sits in a thin top bar. Browsing (a library, a course switcher, all skills) is secondary and placed in a corner or a tab. Where a teacher assigns work, that work *is* the primary action, as Khan Academy, Zearn, Mathletics and SplashLearn all do.

### Cited Findings
**Duolingo (including Math, Music and Chess as courses in the same app)**
- The home screen became a single "path" for every learner on 2022-11-01 [older]. The stated reason: learners "weren't sure they were using Duolingo the 'correct' or 'best' way," so the team wanted "a clear path to follow." — [Duolingo blog, "New Duolingo home screen design", 2022-05-06](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design)
- How the path is built [older]:
  - Each circle on the path is one level.
  - Lessons mix new and review material, and are grouped into small units.
  - Practice and Stories were moved INTO the path.
  - Tips moved to a per-unit guidebook in the unit's top-right corner.
  - Quests moved to their own bottom tab (chest icon).
  - Completed levels turn gold.
  - A floating arrow at bottom-right returns you to your current spot after you scroll away.
  — [Duolingo blog, 2022-05-06](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design)
- The streak sits "prominently in the top bar as a flame icon" on the home screen [older]. — [Duolingo blog (Kai Herng Loh), 2017-05-10](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)
- Math, Music and Chess are courses inside the same app. You switch by tapping the current course's flag at the top of the path, and a "Course" option offers Math, Music or Chess. So the home is always ONE course's path, with a switcher in the top bar. — [Duolingo blog, "Add new course"](https://blog.duolingo.com/add-new-course/)
- Critique: one single path makes long courses feel endless (for example, 200 units in French) and takes away learner choice [older]. — [UX Collective, "Down the wrong path"](https://uxdesign.cc/down-the-wrong-path-the-disaster-of-the-latest-duolingo-ui-update-a4cdd1e6ea1c)
- Scale today: 50.5M daily active users (DAU) in Q3 2025, up 36% year over year. — [Duolingo Q3 2025 shareholder letter (SEC 8-K)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828025049514/q3fy25duolingo9-30x25share.htm)

**Khan Academy (students, "Khan Academy Reimagined", 2025-26)**
- The student dashboard is described as the student's "home base": "where you can find assigned work, track your progress, view your Gems." Overdue assignments "appear in your daily and weekly mission so you can catch up," and the full list is behind "View all assignments" (snippet). — [Khan Academy Help, "How do I use the student dashboard"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/46935588089997-How-do-I-use-the-student-dashboard-in-Khan-Academy)
- Missions (snippet) — [Khan Academy Help, "What's changing for students on the reimagined Khan Academy"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/articles/46056261189773):
  - The Daily Mission "sits at the top of the Learning Queue" and holds work due today, work due tomorrow and overdue work.
  - Weekly Missions hold later work in this week and next. They "automatically show to the student once they complete all the tasks in their Daily Mission."
  - Both are built automatically from assigned work, and both count toward Unit Missions.
- The earlier "Learner Home", the first page after login, showed classes, profile, independent courses, and streak and level progress. Assignments had Active and Past tabs, and the left navigation separated independently chosen courses from work assigned by a teacher or coach (snippet) [older]. — [Khan Academy Help, "What is my Learner Home page"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360030629852-What-is-my-Learner-Home-page-and-what-can-I-do-there)
- In one week, learners "clicked into their personalized 'Next Up' content more than 16 million times," and about 500,000 students kept their streaks alive. — [Khan Academy blog, "Keeping your streak alive", 2025-04-21](https://blog.khanacademy.org/keeping-your-streak-alive-insights-tips-from-the-last-6-months/)

**Khan Academy Kids (ages 2-8)**
- The home is one big green play button in the center, which opens the personalized Learning Path. The Library (books, videos, activities, coloring) is a purple book icon in the top right. The path "automatically adjusts to each child's level." — [Common Sense Education, Khan Academy Kids](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/khan-academy-kids); [CSU Thinkspace review](https://thinkspace.csu.edu.au/thelibrarykingdom/?p=185)

**Zearn (grades K-8, classroom)**
- On login, students land on their Student Feed. Its "Next Up" section is where they work on the Independent Digital Lesson currently assigned to them. A lesson tracker shows the exact lesson and the activity within it. Foundational lessons from the Math Library are also reached through the feed (snippet). — [Zearn Help, "Explore the Student Feed"](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/360058908394); [Zearn Help, "Assign an Independent Digital Lesson"](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/4403429503639-Assign-an-Independent-Digital-Lesson)

**IXL (student view)**
- Students reach a "visual wall of recommended skills" from Learning > Recommendations. It is based on practice history and "updated every time students work on IXL." Recommendations are grouped as "Try something new", "Next up", "Keep at it", "Work it out" and "Go for gold", and can be filtered by type. Teachers are told to use the wall for early finishers, bell work or when there is a substitute. — [IXL for Personalized Practice (guide PDF)](https://ca.ixl.com/materials/i_guides/IXL_for_Personalized_Practice.pdf); [IXL Recommendation Types (PDF)](https://www.ixl.com/materials/IXL_Recommendation_Types.pdf)

**Mathletics**
- The student's weekly points total sits at the top of the console's Home and resets every week, Monday to Sunday. Teacher-set tasks live in a "To-do" list. — [Mathletics Knowledge Base, "Get familiar with the student environment"](https://knowledgebase.mathletics.com/2-get-familiar-with-the-student-environment); [Mathletics, "How can students earn points"](https://knowledgebase.mathletics.com/rewards-and-recognition/how-can-students-earn-points-in-mathletics)

**SplashLearn (grades PreK-5)**
- The student dashboard is "a personalized plan split into daily milestones" with practice goals and reminders. Work assigned by a teacher sits under a "From Your Teacher" tab, and teachers can bypass the recommended path. — [SplashLearn student dashboard page](https://www.splashlearn.com/student-dashboard); [SplashLearn Support, Math Facts](https://support.splashlearn.com/hc/en-us/articles/12274860294418-Math-Facts-on-SplashLearn); [Educators Technology](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2022/01/splashlearn-free-math-games-for.html)

**DreamBox (Discovery Education)**
- K-2 and grades 3-8 get different environments:
  - K-2 students reach their home view through their avatar in the top-left.
  - Grades 3-8 reach it through a tab on the right edge.
  - Both places show the student's weekly lesson count.
  - Re-picking a lesson within 14 days resumes it where it was left [older, 2023].
  — [Discovery Education, Getting Started FAQs 2023 (PDF)](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/Getting%20Started%20FAQs%202023.pdf?version=0); [DreamBox K-5 Teacher Guide (PDF)](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-K-5-Math.pdf)

**Prodigy Math (game, grades 1-8)**
- Students "complete daily and seasonal quests to earn epic items and rewards," and win battles on the map by answering math questions. — [Prodigy Math product page](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/prodigy-math/)
- Parents set Goals: a number of questions to answer correctly. Paid tiers multiply the rewards for meeting daily learning goals: "Ultra Members get double the Wishcoins for completing daily learning goals" (Plus members are quoted as 3x). — [Prodigy blog, Goals & Rewards, 2025-08-16](https://prodigygame.com/blog/goals-rewards-tool); [Prodigy blog, "Is a Prodigy Membership worth it?", 2025-08-14](https://prodigygame.com/blog/is-prodigy-membership-worth-it)

**Brilliant (teens and adults)**
- The current streak count "can be found on your home page." An iOS widget shows streak status and prompts to practice through the day. Terminology changed: "courses are now topics, levels are units, and lessons are concepts." — [Brilliant Help, "What is a streak?", updated 2026-10-07](https://brilliant.org/help/using-brilliant/what-is-a-streak)

**Quizlet (13+)**
- The mobile home is now a personalized feed of "Study Bites, Progress Cards [jump back into recent study], and Content Recommendations." It is "optimized for U.S. college students but … available globally for learners ages 13+." — [Quizlet Help, "Navigating your home feed on mobile devices"](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-au/articles/38999971996301-Navigating-your-home-feed-on-mobile-devices)

**Beast Academy Online** — see Q2 for its structure. I found no public description of its student landing screen; the help article sits behind a login.

### Inferences
- The common shape of the screen, top to bottom:
  1. A status strip (streak, points or gems, course or level switcher).
  2. ONE large "next" control: a node on a path, a play button, Next Up, or the Daily Mission.
  3. Secondary routes such as a library, all skills or recommendations, placed lower or behind a tab.
- No product leads the home with a grid of equal-weight choices. Even IXL, which has a "wall", splits it into labelled groups led by "Next up".
- For Aimtrue:
  - **Students with a tutor:** the tutor's assigned Assessment is the primary card, as Khan's Daily Mission and Zearn's Next Up are.
  - **Self-directed learners:** the primary card is "Continue" (the last tile or level) or today's Daily set.
  - **Tiles:** these are Aimtrue's equivalent of IXL's recommendation wall, a secondary browse area.
- Khan Academy's reveal pattern (Weekly appears only after the Daily is done) is a cheap way to keep a single primary action while still offering more.

### Gaps
- No primary description of Prodigy's post-login world map was found. The help center was not surfaced; the only claims about daily login rewards came from low-quality sites and were dropped.
- I found no Beast Academy Online public page on the student landing screen. The "Navigating Beast Academy Online" help article requires a login.
- Brilliant: no official post on its 2025-26 home layout beyond the help center's mention of the home page and streak.
- Duolingo: the current (2026) home's exact above-the-fold layout is not documented in a primary source beyond the 2022 post and the 2017 note on the top-bar flame. These come from my own knowledge, NOT verified this session:
  - the bouncing "START" bubble on the current node;
  - the unit banner with a guidebook button;
  - the top bar with course flag, streak, gems and hearts or energy.

## 2. How are levels, units, worlds and skills shown as tiles or paths? Is color coded per level or unit, and how accessible is it?

### Takeaway
Two models dominate:
- **The linear path** (Duolingo, Khan Academy Kids, Zearn's lesson tracker, DreamBox): one next node, with completed, current and locked states.
- **The labelled wall or grid** (IXL's recommendation categories, Beast Academy's 12 chapters a level, each lesson rated 0-3 stars).

Progress is shown with completion states (gold, stars, trophies) more than with hue. Where shading carries meaning, it is a single-hue intensity ramp (Beast Academy's parent report: darker blue means stronger). There are no documented examples of rainbow-per-grade coding. Accessibility rules require that color never be the only signal.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo:
  - Completed levels turn gold and can be revisited.
  - "Legendary" now applies to a whole unit.
  - Units are small groups of lessons that mix new and review content.
  - Each unit has a guidebook.
  [older] — [Duolingo blog, 2022-05-06](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design)
- Duolingo's OLD home was a tree of "colorful bubbles in rows of one to three", each skill a circle in its own color with an icon. The 2022 path replaced it with "units of pebbles in a single line winding down the screen" [older]. — [Duolingo blog, 2022-05-06](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design) (as summarized in search; the fetched page confirmed the path and units but not the color wording)
- Beast Academy Online:
  - Each level has 12 chapters, and each chapter has 16-17 lessons of 5-12 problems.
  - A lesson earns 0-3 stars (Needs Practice, Proficient, Advanced, Expert), and at least one star is needed to move on.
  - Trophies are shown beside stars.
  - In parent reports, "the darker the blue, the stronger the performance."
  — [Beast Academy Help, Student progress and activity overview](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1798926-student-progress-and-activity-overview); [Beast Academy Help, Parent reporting overview](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1793720-parent-reporting-overview)
- Beast Academy lock schemes:
  - Sequential: "no parent involvement" needed.
  - All Chapters: "supervision recommended".
  - Free Play: "close guidance recommended".
  - The Puzzle Lab unlocks at XP Level 10.
  — [Beast Academy Help, How to update your student's lock scheme](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1792596-how-to-update-your-student-s-lock-scheme); [Beast Academy Help, manually lock/unlock content](https://help.beastacademy.com/a/1861857-how-to-manually-lock-and-unlock-content)
- IXL groups recommended skills by intent ("Try something new", "Next up", "Keep at it", "Work it out", "Go for gold"). Groups are labelled categories that can be filtered, not just colors. — [IXL for Personalized Practice](https://ca.ixl.com/materials/i_guides/IXL_for_Personalized_Practice.pdf)
- Brilliant renamed its hierarchy to topics > units > concepts (help article updated 2026-10-07). — [Brilliant Help, "What is a streak?"](https://brilliant.org/help/using-brilliant/what-is-a-streak)
- Brilliant's leagues are named after chemical elements, rising from Hydrogen to Einsteinium. Named tiers, not colors alone. — [Brilliant (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliant_(website))
- WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Color, Level A): color must not be "the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element." It was not re-fetched this session; this is the standard's text. — [W3C, Understanding SC 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/use-of-color.html)

### Inferences
- If Aimtrue colors tiles by grade or curriculum, each tile should also carry a text code and an icon or shape, so the color is redundant.
  - The codes already exist: `G4`, `BA3`, `NC.3.OA.1`.
  - This meets SC 1.4.1.
  - It mirrors Beast Academy, which pairs its blue ramp with star counts and words, and Brilliant, whose tiers have names.
- A single-hue intensity (or stars, or a fill) reads better for "how far along" than a rainbow does. Hue suits identity ("this is Beast Academy, this is NC"), not progress.
- Prefer a "current" state (enlarged, pulsing, or labelled "Start") over relying on color for "locked vs. unlocked". Duolingo's floating return-to-current arrow shows how important it is never to lose the current node.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025-26 source documents Duolingo's per-unit banner colors or the exact locked/unlocked styling. My own knowledge is that unit banners cycle colors and locked nodes are grey; that is unverified here.
- Found no published A/B result on color-coded tiles vs. neutral tiles for kids' learning apps.
- Prodigy's world/zone theming and SplashLearn's map colors: no primary source found.

## 3. Daily goals, streaks (daily vs weekly, freezes), XP, leagues, continue cards, daily challenges: what drives return visits, with numbers?

### Takeaway
Streaks are the best-evidenced return driver: Duolingo's data, plus peer-reviewed work showing intact streaks raise later engagement and broken ones depress it. Three details matter:
- the bar to keep a streak should be low (one lesson; Brilliant asks for 3 problems; Khan for one practice problem);
- breaks should be protectable or repairable (freezes, weekend amulets, Brilliant's Streak Charges);
- the commitment should be explicit.

Leagues add time-on-task. Teacher- or parent-driven products use WEEKLY cycles instead (Khan's weekly streak and missions, Mathletics' weekly 1,000-point certificate, DreamBox's weekly lesson count), which fits a 3-days-a-week target.

### Cited Findings
**Duolingo streak and retention numbers**
- From the 2020 change that separated the daily goal from the streak, so one lesson extends the streak (A/B test) [older] — [Duolingo blog, "Improving the streak" (Anton Yu), 2020-11-19](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak):

  | Measure | Change |
  | --- | --- |
  | Day-14 retention | +3.3% relative |
  | Daily active learners | +1% |
  | Daily learners on a streak, within 20 days | +10.5% |
  | New learners on a streak | +19% |
  | Learners on a 7+ day streak | +"over 40%" |

- Same source [older]:
  - Learners on a 7-day streak are 2.4x more likely to return the next day.
  - A year later, "just over half" of daily learners had a 7+ day streak, up from about a third.
  - Trade-off: fewer learners met their daily goals, but more built a habit and total learning time rose.
- Streak Wager (bet gems on 7 more days, double back if kept) [older]: significant lifts at Day 1, Day 7 and Day 14, with **Day-7 retention +14%**. — [Duolingo blog, 2017-05-10](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)
- Weekend Amulet (protects the streak over a weekend) [older]: learners offered it were **4% more likely to return a week later and 5% less likely to lose their streak**. It was introduced because DAU "drop by as much as 5-10%" on weekends. — [Duolingo blog, 2017-05-10](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)
- Jorge Mazal (former CPO), in a first-person essay rather than a paper [older]: [Lenny's Newsletter, "How Duolingo reignited user growth", 2023-02-28](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
  - In Duolingo's growth model, Current User Retention Rate (CURR) had 5x the DAU impact of the next-best lever.
  - Leaderboards (leagues) raised total learning time 17% and tripled highly engaged learners (1+ hour a day, 5 days a week).
  - The first big streak win was a "streak saver" notification sent before a streak is lost.
  - A 10-day streak "substantially" reduced drop-off; he notes possible selection bias.
  - A "moves counter" gamification test was "completely neutral".
  - Notification volume was capped to "protect the channel".
  - Over four years: CURR +21%, daily churn of best users down 40%+, DAU 4.5x, and the share of DAU on a 7+ day streak almost 3x, to over half of DAU.
- Jackson Shuttleworth (Group PM, Retention), interview [older] — [Lenny's Podcast, "Behind the product: Duolingo streaks", 2024-12-15](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks):
  - 600+ streak experiments.
  - Changing the button from "Continue" to "Commit to my goal" "significantly increased retention".
  - Letting users choose a streak-goal length also increased it.
  - Retention is most fragile in the first 7 days.
  - No percentages are given.
- A third-party summary of the same interview says moving to one lesson a day was a major driver, while a test requiring only ONE EXERCISE did not raise DAU. This is a secondary source, so use it with caution. — [recall.it summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- Streak widget [older] — [Duolingo blog, "Widget feature", 2023-08-29](https://blog.duolingo.com/widget-feature):
  - It shows only the streak, whether it is at risk today, and Duo's mood, which grows panicked toward midnight. "Extra stats were unnecessary."
  - Launched on iOS in July 2022 and on Android in March 2023.
  - Half of widget users have a streak of 6+ months.
  - Widget users had "far better retention" even after controlling for commitment; no percentage given.
- Friend streaks, low confidence: a search summary of Duolingo's Q2 2024 shareholder letter says "one out of every five of our DAUs has a streak longer than 365 days" and introduces Friend Streaks (up to 5 friends) [older]. I could not open the letter itself to verify. — [Duolingo Investor Relations](https://investors.duolingo.com/)

**Peer-reviewed evidence on streaks**
- Silverman & Barasch, *Journal of Consumer Research* (2023), seven studies:
  - Highlighting an intact streak in a behavior log raises later engagement compared with highlighting a broken one.
  - The demotivating effect of a broken streak holds regardless of streak length.
  - A search summary reports the penalty is weaker when the streak can be repaired; I saw only the abstract.
  — [Silverman & Barasch, "On or Off Track: How (Broken) Streaks Affect Consumer Decisions" (UDSpace)](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb/full)

### Inferences
- Aimtrue's weekly streak (3 days a week) is in good company with Khan Academy (weekly), Mathletics (weekly certificate) and DreamBox (weekly count).
  - It also builds in what Duolingo's Weekend Amulet does: a slack day.
  - Duolingo's evidence suggests showing the streak on home as a small persistent element, with an explicit at-risk state ("1 more day this week to keep your streak").
  - At-risk is the one thing Duolingo's widget research found worth showing.
- Keep the bar to count a day low: one Daily set or one tile. Duolingo found one lesson worked better than tying the streak to an XP goal, and that one exercise was too little to move DAU.
- Highlight intact streaks (Silverman & Barasch). After a break, show a fresh start or a repair offer rather than the broken count.
- Leagues are the second-best documented lever (+17% learning time). They are social, though, which matters for under-13s (see Q4).
- A Daily set is a natural primary card for self-directed learners, but Duolingo and Brilliant both put the streak and the path, not a "daily problem", at the center. I found no published retention number for a "daily challenge" on its own.

### Gaps
- No published numbers specifically for streak freezes; Duolingo talks about them but has published no figures.
- No numbers for Brilliant's leagues or home.
- No numbers for Khan Academy's missions or gems, or for Prodigy's daily goals.
- No retention data on "continue where you left off" cards in kids' math apps.

## 4. How do the apps treat young children (under 13) differently from teens on the home?

### Takeaway
For young children, the home shrinks to one big play button plus a library (Khan Academy Kids), or to an avatar-led, simpler environment (DreamBox K-2). Social and competitive features are removed: Duolingo's child accounts have no leaderboards or friends. Adults set the goals (Prodigy's parent Goals, Beast Academy's lock schemes). Teen and adult products (Quizlet 13+, Brilliant, standard Duolingo) add feeds, leagues and self-chosen goals.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Academy Kids (ages 2-8): one big green play button in the center opens the Learning Path, and a book icon in the top right opens the Library. The home has almost no text. — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/app/khan-academy-kids); [CSU Thinkspace](https://thinkspace.csu.edu.au/thelibrarykingdom/?p=185)
- DreamBox: K-2 students reach their home view through their avatar (top-left), while grades 3-8 use a tab on the right edge. These are different environments for different grade bands [2023]. — [Discovery Education FAQ 2023](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/Getting%20Started%20FAQs%202023.pdf?version=0)
- Duolingo:
  - Under-13s give a parent's email at sign-up and get a child account with "no access to social features such as leaderboards or adding friends".
  - Age is not verified.
  - These are third-party sources; another source disagrees on whether child profiles are public by default.
  — [Internet Matters](https://www.internetmatters.org/advice/apps-and-platforms/skills-building/duolingo); [Bark review](https://www.bark.us/app-reviews/apps/duolingo-app-review/); [ExpressVPN (conflicting)](https://expressvpn.com/blog/is-duolingo-safe/)

### Inferences
- For Aimtrue's younger children (grades 1-4 and anyone under 13):
  - one giant "Play" or "Continue" control;
  - an icon-first view with fewer tiles;
  - a mascot or visual streak rather than numbers;
  - no public leaderboards;
  - multiplayer only by code within the space, which Aimtrue already does.
- For teens: allow self-chosen goals, the "commit" framing, and comparative or social elements.
- Aimtrue already keeps a teen's personal space closed to code-joins and lets a teen add nobody. A home that hides any social or comparative element for under-13s would match that and Duolingo's practice.

### Gaps
- No primary Duolingo help-center page was retrieved for child-account restrictions; only third parties.
- Found no published retention comparison of kid vs. teen home layouts.
- Prodigy's under-13 social features (chat, friends) were not covered by retrieved sources.

## 5. On the home, how do apps treat a learner assigned work by a teacher or tutor versus a self-directed learner?

### Takeaway
Assigned work goes first.
- **Khan Academy Reimagined:** the Daily Mission, built from assignments (due today or tomorrow, or overdue), sits at the top of the Learning Queue, and the Weekly Mission appears only after it.
- **Zearn:** "Next Up" is the teacher-assigned lesson.
- **Mathletics:** a "To-do" list.
- **SplashLearn:** a "From Your Teacher" tab.
- **IXL:** students are told to start from the teacher's list, with the Recommendations wall for early finishers.

Self-directed learners get a path or recommendations instead: Khan's "Next Up", IXL's wall, Duolingo's path. Khan's older Learner Home kept "courses you chose" and "work you were assigned" visibly separate.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Academy Reimagined (snippet) — [Khan Academy Help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/articles/46056261189773); [Khan Academy Help, student dashboard](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/46935588089997-How-do-I-use-the-student-dashboard-in-Khan-Academy)
  - The Daily Mission (due today, due tomorrow, overdue) is at the top of the Learning Queue.
  - The Weekly Mission is revealed once the Daily is done.
  - Overdue work rolls into the missions.
  - "View all assignments" is the secondary path.
- Khan Academy Learner Home (older): the left navigation "makes it easier for you to distinguish between courses you're taking independently, and any assignments or mastery goals you've been assigned by a teacher or coach"; assignments have Active and Past tabs (snippet) [older]. — [Khan Academy Help community, "Refreshed Learner Home"](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/fr/community/posts/360048340591/comments/360007021311); [Khan Academy Help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360030629852-What-is-my-Learner-Home-page-and-what-can-I-do-there)
- Zearn: login lands on the Student Feed, whose "Next Up" is the currently assigned Independent Digital Lesson, with a lesson tracker (snippet). — [Zearn Help](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/360058908394)
- Mathletics: all teacher-assigned activities appear in the To-do list on the student console. — [Mathletics KB](https://knowledgebase.mathletics.com/2-get-familiar-with-the-student-environment)
- SplashLearn: teacher assignments sit under "From Your Teacher", and teachers can override the adaptive path. — [SplashLearn Support](https://support.splashlearn.com/hc/en-us/articles/12274860294418-Math-Facts-on-SplashLearn); [Educators Technology](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2022/01/splashlearn-free-math-games-for.html)
- IXL: schools tell students to start from the list of skills their teacher assigned. The Recommendations wall is suggested for early finishers. — [IXL sign-in page](https://www.ixl.com/signin/wallhs); [IXL for Personalized Practice](https://ca.ixl.com/materials/i_guides/IXL_for_Personalized_Practice.pdf)
- Khan Academy Gems are earned by completing assigned work, with mission and unit bonuses. This rewards doing the assignment, not free browsing (snippet). — [Khan Academy Help, gems](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/38505636598413-How-do-I-earn-gems-in-Khan-Academy)

### Inferences
- For an Aimtrue student with a tutor:
  1. **Top card:** what the tutor set, such as an Assessment waiting, or a reviewed Assessment ready to read. This is "Waiting for you", which Aimtrue's Play home already has.
  2. **Then:** "Continue" or today's Daily set.
  3. **Then:** tiles.
- For a self-directed learner: lead with Continue (the last tile or level) or the Daily set, then the streak strip, then tiles.
- Borrow Khan's reveal: once assigned work is done, the "more" (Daily, tiles) moves up. That keeps one primary action without hiding the rest.
- Reward the assigned action, as Khan's gems do. Aimtrue already counts Daily and game answers toward `activity_days`. Counting a submitted Assessment as a streak day would align the tutor's goal with the student's habit; that is a design suggestion, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No published data comparing retention when assignments lead the home vs. when the path leads it.
- Found no primary source on how Duolingo for Schools or Brilliant surface teacher-assigned work on the learner home.
- Prodigy's teacher "assignments" view on the student home was not covered by retrieved sources.

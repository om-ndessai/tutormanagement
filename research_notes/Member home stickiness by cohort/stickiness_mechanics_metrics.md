# Stickiness mechanics and metrics for a children's math practice home (Aimtrue, grades 1-8)

Researched 2026-11-03. Intended destination: `research_notes/Member home stickiness by cohort/stickiness_mechanics_metrics.md` (could not be written there: plan mode was active, so this plan file was the only writable path). "Vendor" = a company's own marketing or benchmark number; "company-reported" = a firm's own experiment, not independently verified.

## 1. Which home-screen mechanics raise retention (and by how much), which backfire, and what children's-design rules say

### Takeaway
The best-measured retention gains come from making the habit easier to keep (one small unit counts, slack such as freezes or reserve days, a reward for coming back after a miss), not from adding pressure. Duolingo's published effects are single-digit percentages per change (+3.3% D14 retention) that compound. Leaderboards and tangible or expected rewards have the clearest evidence of backfiring, especially for low performers and children. Regulators (the UK ICO, the FTC, 5Rights) name reward loops, notifications and features that make it hard to stop as risks, and want wellbeing nudges (breaks, pause without losing progress).

### Cited Findings
**Streaks: what Duolingo measured (company-reported)**
- Separating the streak (1 lesson a day extends it) from the daily XP goal gave +3.3% Day-14 retention, +1% daily active learners, +10.5% share of daily learners on a streak (over 20 days), and +19% share of new learners on a streak. Before, learners with higher goals were *less* likely to keep a streak, so the goal itself was the barrier. Downside: fewer learners reached their daily goals. Learners on a 7-day streak were 2.4x more likely to come back the next day (correlational). The share of daily learners with a 7+ day streak rose from about a third to just over half in a year (Anton Yu, Nov 2020, older) — [Duolingo blog, "Improving the streak"](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- A 7-day streak is linked to being 3.6x more likely to complete a course (correlational; seen in a search summary, page not fetched). Streak Freeze "hit pause on your streak for a day"; up to two can be equipped at once. No retention figure for freezes is published — [Duolingo blog, "How the Duolingo streak builds habit"](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Streak Wager (a store item): Day-7 retention +14%, with significant D1 and D14 gains too. This was attributed to Duolingo in search results but I could not trace the original page, so treat it as unverified — [Lazyweb summary of Duolingo retention experiments (third party)](https://lazyweb.com/research/duolingo-retention-experiments)
- Former CPO Jorge Mazal (Feb 2023): the share of DAU with a 7+ day streak "nearly tripled" to more than half of DAU. A streak-saver notification was the "first big win", and freezes and streak rewards improved retention (no figures given). DAU grew 4.5x over four years. A moves-counter gamification test had "no change" to retention or DAU — [Lenny's Newsletter, "How Duolingo reignited user growth"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- Duolingo retention PM (Dec 2024): more than 600 streak experiments. Changing "continue" to "commit to my goal" and letting users choose their streak goal both "significantly" raised retention (no figures). Stated principles: streaks cannot compensate for a weak product; keep the mechanic understandable at a glance; the first 7 days are the most fragile — [Lenny's Podcast, "Behind the product: Duolingo streaks" (Jackson Shuttleworth)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)
- Q2 2026: a one-time "Streak Revival" event (opt in plus 3 lessons restores your longest-ever streak) revived 15.4M learners' streaks, including nearly 8M with no active streak. DAU was 58.7M, +23% YoY — [Duolingo Q2 2026 shareholder letter (SEC 8-K)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)

**Broken streaks, slack and repair (peer-reviewed)**
- Silverman & Barasch (J. Consumer Research, 2023, 7 studies): people are more likely to repeat a behaviour when it extends an intact streak in their log than after a broken one, even with past behaviour held constant (only the display differed). The effect is stronger when people blame themselves, weaker when the streak can be repaired, and fades with time since the break. People often switch activity after a break, e.g. starting a new language rather than rebuilding. The authors say post-break messaging "typically doesn't work" — [CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [paper record, UDel](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb)
- Sharif & Shu (OBHDP, 2019/21), "emergency reserves": a goal framed as 7 days with 2 emergency skips beats a plain 5-day goal for persistence after a failure. In a 5-week step-count field study, reserves gave up to 20% more steps and up to 40% more days meeting the goal. The mechanism is a sense of ongoing progress after a miss. A small student-thesis replication in diet found no effect — [CHIBE/UPenn](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/nudging-persistence-after-failure-through-emergency-reserves/); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/); [Utrecht thesis](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/44466)
- StepUp megastudy (Milkman et al., Nature 2021; ~61,000 24 Hour Fitness members, 54 four-week programmes): about 45% of programmes raised weekly gym visits by 9-27%. The best arm (+27%) gave a tiny bonus (125 points, about 9 cents) for returning after a *missed* workout. Few effects persisted after the four weeks — [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits); [Nature record (IDEAS)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v600y2021i7889d10.1038_s41586-021-04128-4.html)
- Beshears, Lee, Milkman, Mislavsky & Wisdom (Management Science, 2021; 2,508 Google employees): paying for gym visits within a fixed daily 2-hour window ("routine") produced *fewer* visits than paying for a visit at any time ("flexible"), both during the 4 weeks and after. The routine group dropped off more afterwards even where rates had been matched — [INFORMS/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v67y2021i7p4139-4171.html); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/unexpected-exercise-advice-for-the-super-busy-ditch-the-rigid-routine)
- Fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis, Management Science 2014): goal pursuit (diet searches, gym visits, goal commitments) rises after temporal landmarks such as the start of a week, month or semester, or a birthday. A secondary summary says resets can demotivate people who were already succeeding (unverified) — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)

**Points, leaderboards, badges, gamification overall**
- Duolingo leaderboards (leagues): +17% overall learning time, and highly engaged learners (1+ hour a day, 5 days a week) roughly tripled. D1 and D7 improved significantly (company-reported, 2023) — [Lenny's Newsletter (Mazal)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- Duolingo later raised XP for path lessons so that climbing the leaderboard tracks learning, adding about 1.1M (partial) to 1.8M (full) learning minutes a day. This implies the earlier XP rewarded the wrong behaviour — [Duolingo blog, "Time spent learning well"](https://blog.duolingo.com/time-spent-learning-well)
- Leaderboards in education: in several small studies, low-ranked students lose motivation or feel stressed while top performers gain. Showing only nearby ranks has been proposed to soften this. Samples are small (e.g. n=45) — [JOTSE 2026 physics study](https://jotse.org/index.php/jotse/article/view/3321); [NSF-hosted math-game paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10510520); [ESPOL engineering study](https://www.cti.espol.edu.ec/sites/default/files/docs_pdf/Gamification%20through%20leaderboards_An%20empirical%20study%20in%20engineering%20education.pdf)
- Badges (Denny, PeerWise randomised badges on/off): the badges-on group submitted 22% more answers and was active on more distinct days — [Denny, "Do badges work?" (PeerWise)](https://peerwise.cs.auckland.ac.nz/docs/community/do_badges_work)
- Meta-analysis (Sailer & Homner, Educational Psychology Review 2020): small positive effects of gamification on cognitive (g = .49, k = 19), motivational (g = .36) and behavioural (g = .25) learning outcomes. Only the cognitive effect held in high-rigour studies. Game fiction and social interaction moderated the behavioural effects. Likely underpowered, and publication bias was not assessed — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)
- Hanus & Fox (Computers & Education, 2015; 80 university students, one semester): the gamified course (badges, leaderboard, coins together) produced *lower* intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and final-exam scores, mediated by intrinsic motivation. The authors suspect the overjustification effect — [DOI 10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019) (details via secondary summaries)
- Deci, Koestner & Ryan (Psychological Bulletin, 1999; 128 experiments): expected tangible rewards undermine free-choice intrinsic motivation. Engagement-contingent d = -0.40 and performance-contingent d = -0.28; the completion-contingent figure was reported as -0.36 in one summary (my recollection is -0.44; check the paper). The finding is still contested by Cameron and Pierce's line of meta-analyses — [PDF copy (Colorado)](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/deci%201999.pdf); [2001 follow-up](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)

**Harm to children**
- Radesky et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2022): 113 of 133 apps (about 80%) used by 160 children aged 3-5 had at least one manipulative design feature; 98.8% of children were exposed. Among features to prolong play: navigation constraints 45.9%, lures 45.1%, parasocial relationship pressure 24.8%, time pressure 17.3% of apps. Children of parents without a college degree scored higher on these features (exploratory) — [PMC9206186](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/)
- Common Sense Media / U. Michigan, "Constant Companion" (2023; 203 teens tracked): median 237 notifications a day (some over 4,500), about 25% during school hours and about 5% at night; median 43 minutes of phone use during the school day — [report PDF](https://www.tcatitans.org/cms/lib/CO50010872/Centricity/Domain/63/2023%20-%20Common%20Sense%20Media%20-%20Constant%20Companion.pdf)
- Snapchat streaks and young people: the evidence is weak and mostly cross-sectional. There is a small association with problematic smartphone use (van Essen et al., 2023); Bits of Freedom (Mar 2026) found peer pressure to keep streaks, though most participants did not notice an effect on screen time; and an Egyptian teen survey (2025) reported distress when a streak is lost and putting streaks before sleep or study — [EDRi on Bits of Freedom study](https://edri.org/our-work/new-study-reveals-how-young-people-are-influenced-by-gamification-features-on-snapchat/); [Egyptian study](https://mjsm.journals.ekb.eg/article_422365.html); [UBC summary of van Essen](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec523/2024/06/02/snapchat-streaks/)
- 5Rights "Disrupted Childhood" (2018; updated 2023) lists streaks, with notifications, likes and auto-play, among persuasive-design "hooks", and says the burden should not fall on children and parents — [5Rights 2023](https://5rightsfoundation.com/in-action/disrupted-childhood-the-cost-of-persuasive-design-2023.html); [5Rights 2018](https://5rightsfoundation.com/in-action/disrupted-childhood-the-cost-of-persuasive-technology.html)

**Regulation and guidance**
- UK Children's Code, verbatim. Standard 5: "Do not use children's personal data in ways that have been shown to be detrimental to their wellbeing". Standard 13: "Do not use nudge techniques to lead or encourage children to provide unnecessary personal data or turn off privacy protections". Standard 7: settings "high privacy" by default — [Children's Code standards text (mirror)](https://edlaw.eduverse.com/docs/jurisdiction/nat/gb/childrens-code/STANDARDS.md) (the ICO pages themselves returned 403)
- Guidance under Standard 5, as summarised by law firms (I could not open the ICO text): engagement-extending strategies ("reward loops, continuous scrolling, features that encourage continuous play, notifications") are examples of detrimental use; "sticky features" need caution; features that make it hard to disengage or exploit peer pressure are "unlikely to be fair"; provide "pause buttons which allow children to take breaks without losing progress"; nudge *towards* wellbeing, e.g. taking breaks — [WSGR summary](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2020/01/update-uks-age-appropriate-design-code/); [TIGA summary](https://tiga.org/?p=8283)
- ICO "Top tips for games designers" (Feb 2023, not binding) covers helping players disengage from long sessions and parental alerts. It is "under review" after the Data (Use and Access) Act (law 19 June 2025). I found no ICO wording on streaks specifically — [ICO page](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/childrens-code-hub/top-tips-for-games-designers-how-to-comply-with-the-children-s-code); [Bristows](https://inquisitiveminds.bristows.com/post/102i85n/ico-boosts-childrens-code-with-walkthrough-for-game-developers)
- FTC COPPA amendments (final Jan 2025, Federal Register Apr 2025, effective 23 June 2025, compliance by 22 Apr 2026): the proposal to require parental consent for using persistent identifiers to send push notifications that prompt engagement was *not adopted* (too broad). The FTC restated concern about "push notifications and other engagement techniques to keep kids online in ways that could harm their mental health" — [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/ftc-finalizes-long-awaited-coppa-rule-updates-to-strengthen-childrens-online-privacy-protections/); [Wiley](https://wiley.law/alert-FTC-Adopts-Amended-Childrens-Online-Protection-Act-Rule); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1623166/ftc-finalizes-amendments-to-the-childrens-online-privacy-protection-act-rule)

### Inferences
- Aimtrue's existing weekly streak (3 days a week) already has the shape the evidence favours: a low bar per unit (like Duolingo's one lesson), built-in slack (in effect 4 "reserve" days a week, as in Sharif & Shu), and flexible timing (Beshears). Rather than adding a daily streak, keep the weekly one and make it legible: a 7-dot week with 3 targets.
- The biggest lever after a miss is a low-guilt "come back" moment, not a loss message: StepUp's best arm rewarded the return, and Silverman & Barasch show repairability blunts the post-break drop. A weekly streak gives a natural fresh start each Monday. A one-time "revive" (like Duolingo's) is defensible; a paid freeze or a wager is not, for children.
- Avoid public or global leaderboards for under-13s. If competition is wanted, multiplayer games (already built), team scores or "beat your own best" fit both the evidence and the Children's Code better. Duolingo's own leaderboard needed an XP fix because it rewarded the wrong behaviour.
- Badges derived from counts are low-risk if they are unexpected or informational (milestones), not promised as payment for time spent. Engagement-contingent rewards ("play 20 minutes, get X") carry the largest undermining effect.
- For children: no time-pressure lures, no parasocial guilt (a mascot that "is sad"), no notifications in school hours or at night. Pause without losing progress fits the ICO code.

### Gaps
- No published retention effect for Duolingo's streak freeze, streak repair or weekend amulet, and none for Brilliant, Headspace, Strava, Peloton or Apple Fitness rings. I found no peer-reviewed or company data on their streak or ring mechanics, only reviews and opinion (e.g. the Apple activity streak allows no rest days).
- No study of weekly versus daily streaks head to head. The support for weekly comes indirectly from the flexibility, reserves and dosage studies.
- No controlled study of streak-related harm in children under 13. The Snapchat evidence concerns teens and is cross-sectional.
- Could not verify the exact ICO wording on reward loops or pause buttons (403), nor Deci et al.'s children-versus-adults moderator.

## 2. Which personal metrics on a home help (learners, parents, tutors) and which overwhelm

### Takeaway
Monitoring progress reliably helps people reach goals (d ≈ 0.40), more so when it is recorded and reported. Nearness to a goal accelerates effort (goal gradient, endowed progress). For learning, weekly dosage (minutes or days) predicts gains better than raw counts. Comparing a learner with peers is where dashboards backfire for low performers. Parents respond to short, specific, action-oriented weekly messages, which have cut course failure by about 27-41% in RCTs.

### Cited Findings
- Harkin et al. (Psychological Bulletin, 2016; 138 RCTs, N = 19,951): prompting progress monitoring raised monitoring (d = 1.98) and goal attainment (d = 0.40, 95% CI 0.32-0.48). The effect was larger when progress was physically recorded and when it was reported to others or made public — [White Rose abstract](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/); [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2015/10/progress-goals)
- Goal gradient (Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, J. Marketing Research 2006): in a "buy 10, get 1 free" café card, the time between purchases fell by about 20% from the first stamp to the last. A 12-stamp card with 2 pre-filled stamps was completed faster than a 10-stamp card (illusory progress). Motivation can drop after the reward is reached (a "reset") — [Columbia PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf)
- Endowed progress (Nunes & Drèze, J. Consumer Research 2006): a car-wash card with 2 of 10 stamps pre-filled versus a blank 8-stamp card (same 8 washes needed). The control redeemed at 19%; the widely cited 34% for the endowed card is unverified here — [UX Collective summary](https://uxdesign.cc/endowed-progress-effect-give-your-users-a-head-start-97d52d8b0396); [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=5630)
- Implementation intentions (if-then plans: "when X, I will do Y"): Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, 94 tests, d = 0.65. A 2024 meta-analysis of 642 tests finds the effect depends on plan format and existing motivation — [Konstanz repository](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f); [summary of 2024 meta-analysis](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/)
- Khan Academy dosage (company research, observational): 30+ minutes a week (about 18 hours a year) is associated with about 20% greater-than-expected MAP Growth gains (effect size 0.36), but only about 9% of students reached that dose. A blog-reported PNAS paper (2026; not verified) estimates +0.09 to 0.18 SD for each added 30 minutes a week or 60 skills a year — [Khan Academy efficacy, Nov 2024](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18194); [Khan blog, multiple studies](https://blog.khanacademy.org/multiple-studies-show-khan-academy-drives-learning-gains-evidence-for-our-platforms-effectiveness)
- Learning-analytics dashboards (Saqr et al., LAK 2024; review of 38 studies): only modest, rarely significant effects on motivation and attitudes. Peer comparison, a common frame, is not perceived positively by all learners. In one thesis, low-mastery learners in an "Ability Group" comparison showed high demotivation — [arXiv 2312.15042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15042); [SFU thesis](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/2023-11/etd22727.pdf)
- Parents, Bergman & Chan (J. Human Resources 2021, RCT, West Virginia): automated weekly texts on missed assignments and absences plus monthly low-grade alerts cut course failures by 27% (0.97 to 0.71 per student) and raised attendance, but not standardized test scores. The effect was concentrated in high school; 32,000 texts cost $63 — [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/2353); [JHR](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/56/1/125)
- Parents, Kraft & Rogers (Economics of Education Review 2015; 435 parents): weekly one-sentence teacher-to-parent messages cut the share failing to earn credit from 15.8% to 9.3%, mainly by preventing drop-out. Messages about *what to improve* had larger effects than praise — [Brown Annenberg](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/underutilized-potential-teacher-parent-communication-evidence-field-experiment)
- Habit-formation time: Lally et al. 2010, median 66 days to automaticity (range 18-254; observation stopped at 84 days). Singh et al. 2024 review: medians 59-66 days, range 4-335 — [secondary summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-habit-systematic-review-59-66-335-days/); [Lenz fact check](https://lenz.io/q/how-long-does-it-take-to-form-habit)

### Inferences
- The learner home should show one primary self-referenced metric with a visible finish line: "2 of 3 days this week" as dots, then the next milestone ("8 more questions to your 500th"). Goal gradient and endowed progress argue for showing *partial* progress, e.g. today's Daily set as "2/5 done".
- Prefer "you versus your past" (personal best, this week versus last, skills mastered this month) to peer ranks. Accuracy can be shown per skill as mastery, not as a headline percentage that punishes a hard day.
- Weekly time or days practised is the metric linked to learning outcomes (Khan's dosage). A tutor and parent view could show "weeks at 3+ days" and skills moved, which is what predicts gains.
- Parents: a weekly digest with one concrete next step ("Maya missed practice this week. Her next skill is fractions: try the Daily set together") fits the RCTs. Aimtrue's email rules (no scores or notes in mail) are compatible: an email can say *that* there is something to see and link to the app.
- If-then plans can be prompted on the home: "When do you practise? After school on Tue/Thu/Sat", anchored to lesson days where a tutoring schedule exists.
- Rough budget for "overwhelm": the reviewed sources give no threshold. Practitioner convention (not evidenced here) is one primary metric plus 2-3 secondary.

### Gaps
- No direct study of how many metrics overwhelm on a child's home screen, nor of age-specific comprehension of percentages or charts by grade (1-8).
- No RCT evidence on tutor-facing practice dashboards. The parent evidence is from secondary schools (middle-school effects were not significant in Bergman & Chan).
- The endowed-progress 34% figure and the Khan PNAS figures were not verified against the primary sources.

## 3. Habit frameworks and trigger design (notifications, email, calendar anchors)

### Takeaway
Fogg (B = MAP) says behaviour needs a prompt at a moment of enough motivation and ability, and that making the behaviour easier is more sustainable than raising motivation. Eyal's Hook loop (trigger, action, variable reward, investment) is effective but ethically fraught for children. Notifications measurably add retention (Duolingo's optimised reminders: about +0.5% DAU, +2% new-user retention), but children already receive hundreds a day and regulators flag engagement pushes. Calendar-anchored prompts and if-then plans are the low-risk route.

### Cited Findings
- Fogg Behavior Model: behaviour happens when Motivation, Ability and a Prompt converge. Prompts are Sparks (low motivation), Facilitators (low ability) or Signals (both high). Fogg's advice: ensure a prompt, then make it easier, and only then add motivation. "Triggers" were renamed "prompts" in 2019 — [The Behavioral Scientist](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/?p=3058); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/fogg-behavior-model)
- Hook Model (Eyal, *Hooked*, 2013/14): an external or internal trigger, then a simple action, then a variable reward (tribe, hunt, self), then investment that loads the next trigger. It is widely critiqued for manipulation risk — [Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/hooked/); [Amplitude blog](https://amplitude.com/blog/the-hook-model.md)
- Duolingo's notification bandit (Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020): optimising which reminder template to send (the "recovering" bandit penalises repeats) gave about +0.5% total DAU and +2% new-user retention over a strong baseline (verify against the PDF). The replication data covers about 200M notifications — [paper PDF](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Papers with Code](https://paperswithcode.com/paper/a-sleeping-recovering-bandit-algorithm-for)
- Duolingo capped notification volume unless the CEO approved an increase. The streak-saver notification was a "first big win" — [Lenny's (Mazal)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- Teens: a median of 237 notifications a day, about a quarter in school hours — [Common Sense 2023](https://www.tcatitans.org/cms/lib/CO50010872/Centricity/Domain/63/2023%20-%20Common%20Sense%20Media%20-%20Constant%20Companion.pdf). The FTC dropped, but "reiterated concern" about, push notifications that keep kids online — [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/ftc-finalizes-long-awaited-coppa-rule-updates-to-strengthen-childrens-online-privacy-protections/). The ICO code treats notifications among engagement-extending features — [WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2020/01/update-uks-age-appropriate-design-code/)
- Timing: temporal landmarks such as Mondays and new months raise goal pursuit — [Dai et al. 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf); [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/why-monday-is-the-best-day-for-setting-new-goals.html). If-then plans, d = 0.65 — [Konstanz](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f). Flexible timing beat fixed windows — [Beshears et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v67y2021i7p4139-4171.html)

### Inferences
- For Aimtrue, the home itself is the main "facilitator" prompt: one obvious next action (Continue, or today's Daily set) cuts ability cost, which is Fogg's first lever. Tiles already do this.
- Use triggers that do not target children directly where possible: the parent's weekly digest, tutor lesson days (a "practise before Thursday's lesson" card), and the Monday fresh start. Any reminder to a child should be opt-in, capped (say at most one per day of the weekly target), never in school hours or at night, and silenced once the week's 3 days are met.
- Avoid variable-reward slot mechanics (random prizes or chests) for children. Fixed, predictable, informational milestones carry less risk under both self-determination theory and ICO Standard 5.

### Gaps
- No published effect sizes for in-app (non-push) home cards versus push notifications in kids' apps.
- No primary data on calendar-anchored practice prompts (lesson day minus N) in tutoring products.
- I did not reach Fogg's own site or Eyal's book; the framework descriptions are from secondary summaries.

## 4. Stickiness metrics and benchmarks (edtech and consumer learning)

### Takeaway
Track a weekly-native set: WAU/MAU (or weeks active out of the last 4), a weekly L-histogram (days active out of 7), current-user retention (CURR), new/reactivated/resurrected flows, and cohort W1/W4/W8 retention, alongside dosage (share of learners meeting 3 days a week). Public benchmarks for education apps are poor and conflicting: D30 about 2-8%, DAU/MAU about 13% median. Duolingo (about 37-42% DAU/MAU, CURR 84%) is the ceiling, not the norm.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo DAU/MAU, computed from letter figures: Q3 2024 37.2M/113.1M ≈ 32.9%; Q3 2025 50.5M/135.3M ≈ 37.3% — [Q3 2025 letter (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828025049514/q3fy25duolingo9-30x25share.htm). Q2 2025 47.7M/128.3M ≈ 37.2%; Q2 2026 58.7M/140.6M ≈ 41.7% — [Q2 2026 letter (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)
- Duolingo's CURR (2026 definition) is "the proportion of 'current user DAUs' who come back the next day", where a current user was "also active another time in the previous seven days". It was 84% in Q2 2026, an all-time high, about +1 point YoY, and "one of our most important leading indicators" — [Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)
- Duolingo growth model (Mazal, 2023). User states: New (first day ever); Current (active today and at least once in the prior 6 days); Reactivated (back after 7-29 days); Resurrected (back after 30+ days); At-risk WAU (inactive today, active in the prior 6 days); At-risk MAU (inactive 7 days, active in the prior 23); Dormant (31+ days). In simulation, CURR moved DAU about 5x more than the next-best lever; CURR rose 21% over 4 years and cut daily churn among the best users by over 40%. CURR was originally Zynga's weekly measure (the chance of returning this week after being active in each of the past two weeks) — [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- Returning users: more than half of Duolingo's top-of-funnel growth in the year to Q3 2024 came from users inactive for over a month — [Q3 2024 letter (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208824000248/q3fy24duolingo09-30x24shar.htm). An older Duolingo post says resurrected users are about 20% less likely than new users to be retained at D7/D14 — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/back-from-the-brink-what-duolingo-learned-about-its-resurrected-users)
- Power User Curve (Andrew Chen / a16z, 2018): a histogram of days active in the last 30 (L30) or 7 (L7), a naming from Facebook's growth team. A "smile" (a cluster of near-daily users) signals health, and cohort-over-cohort shifts show whether engagement deepens. It reveals what a single DAU/MAU figure hides; not every category supports daily use — [andrewchen.com](https://andrewchen.com/power-user-curve/); [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-power-user-curve-the-best-way-to-understand-your-most-engaged-users/)
- Category DAU/MAU (vendor): "Education & Knowledge" median 12.6%, top decile 25.4% (2026, US iOS catalog estimate) — [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/glossary/dau-mau). The Mixpanel figures found (about 37% "all-industry average", 2024; B2B SaaS about 31%, e-commerce about 20%, 2026) come only via secondary summaries, with no education row — [PM Toolkit](https://pmtoolkit.ai/calculators/dau-mau)
- Amplitude (vendor, 2025 Product Benchmark Report, 2,600+ companies): a "7% Day-7 retention" North Star threshold across products; 7-day activation is the strongest predictor of 3-month retention. Education's D1 activation is 21% below the all-company average and month-1 retention about 1% below — [Amplitude 7% rule](https://www.amplitude.com/blog/7-percent-retention-rule); [Amplitude education benchmarks](https://amplitude.com/benchmarks/education)
- Mobile classic retention (vendor, conflicting): education D30 about 2% (AppsFlyer via Business of Apps); Adjust education D7 10%, then 8%, and D30 about 4% (2022-23, older); a OneSignal-based table gives education D1 27.5%, D7 17.8%, D30 8.0%. All-app averages are commonly cited as D1 25-26%, D7 11-13%, D30 5-7%. Rolling and classic definitions differ — [Getstream guide](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide); [Passion.io](https://passion.io/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks-for-creators-course-coaching-apps); [Adjust education](https://www.adjust.com/blog/educational-app-trends-insights-strategies/)

### Inferences
- Because Aimtrue's habit target is weekly (3 of 7 days), daily metrics will look "bad" by design. A suggested metric set (my synthesis): (1) weekly active members / monthly active members; (2) the L7 histogram (0-7 days played) per week, watching the 3+ bucket; (3) weekly CURR: of members who met the weekly streak last week, the share who meet it again this week; (4) streak-week share: members with 3+ days / weekly active; (5) user-state flows weekly (new, current, reactivated after 2-4 weeks, resurrected after 5+ weeks, dormant); (6) cohort retention at W1, W4, W8 and W12 by sign-up week, split by role (student, parent, tutor, staff) and by space type (personal or self-serve versus a tutoring space); (7) dosage, minutes or questions a week, against a 30-minutes-a-week reference; (8) the Daily set completion rate; (9) post-break return within 7 days, the resurrection lever. These can all be derived from Aimtrue's existing `activity_days` rollup without new per-person tracking, and from anonymous counts for guests.
- Benchmarks to set expectations: for a consumer education app, a DAU/MAU above about 13% is above median and above about 25% is top decile (vendor). A D7 above 7% clears Amplitude's bar. A school or tutoring-linked product should do far better than consumer D30 of 2-8%, because a tutor and lesson days supply external structure.
- Report metrics to platform staff in aggregate. Learners, parents and tutors see only their own or their pairing's metrics, consistent with the reader-own rules.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025-26 education-specific D1/D7/D30 table from AppsFlyer or Adjust was retrievable; all category figures are vendor and conflicting.
- No published WAU/MAU or L7 benchmarks for K-8 learning apps; no public data from Khan Academy, IXL, Prodigy or Brilliant on DAU/MAU.
- Duolingo's Q3 2026 letter (due about 5 Nov 2026) was not yet available.

## 5. Color-coded level/category tiles: categorisation, wayfinding, colorblind safety, visual variety

### Takeaway
Colour coding speeds visual search when colours map to stable categories and the set is small (≤ about 8 distinguishable hues). It must never be the only cue (WCAG 1.4.1); text labels and icons or shapes are needed too, because about 8% of men of Northern European descent have red-green deficiency and dichromats search about 2x slower when colour is the cue.

### Cited Findings
- Colour coding improved search time over no coding (Swan et al., HFES 1993). A 1994 follow-up found colour, texture and other non-blank codes about equally effective once colours were equal in saturation and brightness — [Swan 1993](https://web.cse.msstate.edu/~swan/publications/b2hd-HFES93-win.html); [Swan 1994](https://web.cse.msstate.edu/~swan/publications/b2hd-HFES94-bar.html)
- Colour helps most when tasks involve more complex, multiple stimulus formats, and practice with the code matters (US Air Force review) — [DTIC ADA031105](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA031105.pdf)
- Search time depends on the target's and distractors' colour *categories*, not raw colour difference (so pick hues that name differently: blue, orange, green and so on) — [Optical Review 2012](https://annex.jsap.or.jp/photonics/opticalreview/TOC-Lists/vol19/19d0276tx.htm)
- Dichromats' mean search times were twice those of people with normal colour vision in a colour-cued search task — [U. Minho 2015](https://repositorium.sdum.uminho.pt/handle/1822/39759)
- Red-green colour vision deficiency: up to 8% of males and 0.5% of females of Northern European descent, lower in Asian and African populations — [PMC12385717](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12385717/)
- WCAG 2 SC 1.4.1 Use of Color (Level A): colour is not the only means of conveying information. Use text or shape too. Hues differing in lightness pass only at 3:1 contrast, and anything that relies on identifying a particular colour needs another visual indicator — [W3C Understanding 1.4.1](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/use-of-color); [W3C G182](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Techniques/general/G182.html)
- Okabe-Ito / Wong (Nature Methods 2011) categorical palette, 8 colours: #E69F00, #56B4E9, #009E73, #F0E442, #0072B2, #D55E00, #CC79A7, #000000. Above 8 categories, add shape or pattern or use Paul Tol's palettes (up to 12) — [Conceptviz reference](https://conceptviz.app/fr/blog/okabe-ito-palette-hex-codes-complete-reference); [The Node](https://thenode.biologists.com/data-visualization-with-flying-colors/research/)
- NN/g: colours should work together, not overwhelm, and "communicate the same kind of information everywhere in the interface" — [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/using-color-enhance-design/). Redundant coding: colour enhances, but does not carry, the key information — [Wilke, Fundamentals of Data Visualization](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/redundant-coding.html)

### Inferences
- Map one colour per *stable family* (e.g. curriculum or grade band: Beast Academy levels, NC grade bands, Kangaroo), not per tile, and keep it the same everywhere (tiles, progress, badges). Pair it with the level code text (`G4`, `BA3`) and an icon or shape, so the tiles pass WCAG 1.4.1 and work for colorblind children.
- Limit hues to about 6-8 drawn from a CVD-safe set (Okabe-Ito-like), adapted to the brand tokens with light and dark variants. Check 3:1 non-text contrast for tile edges and 4.5:1 for labels. The project's rule (tokens, never hex literals in components) means the palette belongs in `index.css` as category tokens.
- Visual variety probably helps young children find "their" tile through recognition, but the evidence found is for categorical coding, not decorative variety; random colours per tile would cost the categorisation benefit.

### Gaps
- No study found specifically on children (grades 1-8) and colour-coded navigation tiles, or on whether decorative variety (not categorical) improves findability.
- NN/g's full colour guidelines and dashboard articles were not retrievable (only a video page); no primary NEI page was reached for colour-vision-deficiency prevalence.

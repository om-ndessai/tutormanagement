# Live and asynchronous classroom quiz/game platforms for math practice and competitions (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-21. Scope: products a math club, tutoring center or teacher uses to run a quiz, game show, timed challenge or tournament where the same questions go to all participants and are scored. US focus. Many third-party pricing pages disagree with each other; official sources are preferred and conflicts are flagged. Vendor-reported figures are marked as such.

## Q1. Formats, scoring, player limits, question types, math rendering, and whether everyone gets the same set

### Takeaway
Two families dominate: (a) host-paced or student-paced "same set for everyone" quiz shows (Kahoot!, Wayground/Quizizz, Quizlet Live, Socrative, Nearpod Time to Climb, Factile), whose default scoring rewards speed and which only recently added accuracy-only options (Kahoot! Accuracy mode, May 2025); and (b) "game-economy" products (Blooket, Gimkit) where questions earn currency that is then spent or gambled, so luck and strategy decide the winner. Math-specific practice products (Sumdog, Prodigy, Live Mathletics, 99math) mostly ADAPT the questions per child, so participants do NOT get the same set. Math notation is weak almost everywhere: Kahoot! has a palette-style math symbol editor, not LaTeX, and typed answers are plain text.

### Cited Findings

**Kahoot! (Kahoot! 360 / EDU)**
- Official teacher plans page (fetched Oct 2026) lists game experiences "Classic live, unplugged, quiz and true/false, accuracy, and assign kahoots" on the free Go plan; assign (self-paced "challenge" homework) and team mode are on all plans; team mode allows 3 teams (Go), 5 (Plus Bronze), 10 (Silver/Gold), 25 (Kahoot! One) — [Kahoot! plans for schools](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/)
- Participants per game by plan: Go 40 (the page's FAQ says 10 — internal conflict), Plus Bronze 50, Plus Silver 100, Plus Gold 200, Kahoot! One 800 — [Kahoot! plans for schools](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/)
- Question types gated by plan: free Go has only quiz and true/false with max 4 answer options; puzzle, multi-select, poll from Bronze; slider, type answer, pin answer, open-ended only on Gold and One — [Kahoot! plans for schools](https://kahoot.com/schools/plans/)
- Classic scoring: correct answers earn more the faster they are submitted, wrong answers earn nothing; an answer-streak bonus has existed (one event rulebook: 100 bonus points per streak step, up to 500, reset by a wrong answer), though a Kahoot community post says streak points were removed in 2020 — conflicting, status unclear — [UC Davis trivia rules (PDF)](https://campusrecreation.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk6556/files/files/event/Aggies%20at%20Home%20Trivia%20Rules.pdf); [Kahoot community: Disable Answer Streak Bonus](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360033686653-Disable-Answer-Streak-Bonus)
- Accuracy mode (announced May 2025 in a "Teacher Takeover" blog; later called the "Accuracy experience"): everyone gets the same time, each correct answer = 1 point, no double points, partially correct multi-select and nearly-correct slider answers count as correct, and the host can enable unlimited time as an accessibility option — [Kahoot! blog, 6 May 2025](https://kahoot.com/blog/2025/05/06/teacher-takeover-accuracy-mode/); [Kahoot! support: Accuracy experience](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/articles/39818967108627); [Kahoot! blog, Sept 2025](https://kahoot.com/blog/2025/09/04/back-to-school-season-with-kahoot/)
- Teachers had long requested a no-speed option; the older "No points" setting gave zero points rather than one per correct answer — [Kahoot community: Score without being the fastest](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360037566554-Score-without-being-the-fastest); [Kahoot community: remove points scaling with answer speed](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/38794659972755-Add-option-to-remove-points-awarded-scaling-with-answer-speed)
- Confidence experience (Silver and up): players "boost" answers; Level 1 gives +50 for a boosted correct answer and −50 for a boosted wrong one — [Kahoot! support: Confidence experience](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/articles/32200674639261)
- Math notation: Kahoot! added a "math symbol editor" (Oct 2020) via an f(x) button for fractions, roots, logarithms, negative integers, Greek letters, in questions, answers and slides, free for all; no evidence it accepts LaTeX — [Kahoot! blog, 13 Oct 2020](https://kahoot.com/blog/2020/10/13/math-symbol-editor-kahoot/); [Dawson College: Kahoot features for math teachers](https://dawsonite.dawsoncollege.qc.ca/2021/03/important-kahoot-features-for-math-teachers/)
- A teacher reported that students answering "Type answer" questions can enter only letters and numerals (no exponents or special characters) — user report, not confirmed — [Kahoot community: special characters in answer types](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/44325420738323-SPECIAL-CHARACTERS-IN-ANSWER-TYPES)
- Same set: in live Classic, all players get the same questions in the same order, host-paced (implicit in the live format; no source states otherwise).

**Wayground (formerly Quizizz) — rebrand confirmed**
- Quizizz rebranded as Wayground on 24 June 2025 (quizizz.com moved to wayground.com); CEO Ankit Gupta said the product had outgrown "quiz" and is now a "supplemental learning platform"; company launched in 2015 — [THE Journal, 24 June 2025](https://thejournal.com/Articles/2025/06/24/Quizizz-Rebrands-as-Wayground-Announces-New-AI-Features.aspx?p=1); [Teesside University LTE blog, 26 June 2025](https://blogs.tees.ac.uk/lteonline/2025/06/26/quizizz-is-now-wayground-what-this-means-for-teesside-university-staff/)
- Rebrand-era features: curriculum alignment, lesson bundles, AI generation hub (one file into many formats), AI accommodations (translation, difficulty adjustment, dyslexia font, read-aloud), and VoyageMath, a middle-school individualized math program — [THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/Articles/2025/06/24/Quizizz-Rebrands-as-Wayground-Announces-New-AI-Features.aspx?p=1)
- Modes: instructor-paced live ("all your students tackle each question together") and student-paced Classic live; power-ups (Streak Saver, Time Freeze, Immunity, Double Jeopardy = double points for a correct answer), on by default; redemption questions let students retry missed questions — [Wayground live quizzes page](https://wayground.com/home/solutions/live-quizzes?lng=en); [Quizizz "make learning fun" page](https://quizizz.com/home/solutions/make-learning-fun-with-quizizz)
- Exact free-plan caps and math/LaTeX editor behaviour in Wayground could not be confirmed from an official page (see Gaps).

**Blooket**
- Gold Quest (official help center): players answer questions, open treasure chests and "hope luck is on your side"; chests hold gold, multipliers, nothing, steal, swap, or a dragon that takes gold; "every chest is random" — [Blooket help: Gold Quest](https://help.blooket.com/hc/en-us/articles/42100784373655-Game-Overview-Gold-Quest)
- Free hosts: 60 players per live game; Plus: 300 (third-party, consistent across several sources) — [Nibble: Blooket pricing](https://nibble-app.com/blog/blooket-pricing); [Rigorous Themes: Is Blooket Plus worth it](https://rigorousthemes.com/blog/is-blooket-plus-worth-it-free-vs-paid/)
- Founder story: Ben Stewart was "tired of the same kids always winning" in other quiz games — i.e., luck is a deliberate design choice to level the field — [UBC ETEC522 blog](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec522/?p=5820)

**Gimkit (Live/Kit, Assignments, KitCollab)**
- Hard cap 500 students in a live game; 60 for all 2D modes; Pro-exclusive modes limited to 5 players for free (Basic) members; featured and Pro-exclusive modes rotate every few weeks — [Gimkit help: Player maximums](https://help.gimkit.com/en/article/player-maximums-18mbcz0/)
- Scoring is an economy: correct answers earn in-game cash spent on upgrades and power-ups; Gimkit changed its streak bonus to stack (reset to zero by a wrong answer) explicitly to reward accuracy over speed, and its simulations showed a more accurate student with worse upgrades still won "in quite a few scenarios" — [Gimkit blog: An upgrade gets an upgrade](https://blog.gimkit.com/blog/an-upgrade-gets-an-upgrade)
- Common Sense Education: power-ups make play more random; teachers can switch them off — [Common Sense Education: Gimkit review](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/gimkit)
- Assignments: teacher sets a due date and students work through the kit at their own pace to a goal (a Pro feature); KitCollab: students submit questions that the teacher approves before play; modes include Classic, Team, Fishtopia, Trust No One — [Nibble: Gimkit vs Kahoot](https://nibble-app.com/blog/gimkit-vs-kahoot); [Educators Technology: Gimkit](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/03/gimkit-live-game-show-for-classroom.html)

**Quizlet Live**
- Built on a flashcard set (term/definition); team mode puts students in random animal-named teams where each question's correct answer is on only one teammate's screen, forcing discussion; game ends when a team answers every question in the set; an individual mode also exists; paid teacher accounts can set custom teams. Sources are 2023 guides and unofficial pages; minimum set size (6 vs 12 terms) and the "wrong answer resets the team to zero" rule are unconfirmed — [Educators Technology, May 2023](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/05/what-is-quizlet-live-and-how-to-use-it.html); [NGPF: Tech tool review Quizlet Live](https://dev6.ngpf.org/blog/ngpf-fellows/tech-tool-review-quizlet-live/)

**Live Mathletics (3P Learning)**
- Real-time 60-second head-to-head fluency race; World Challenge matches students with others online worldwide "only with those on the same ranking"; Class Challenge (up to four classmates), School Challenge, Computer Challenge, Shadow Mode (race a classmate's high score asynchronously); ten skill-based (not grade-based) levels; 1 point per correct answer, bonus level doubles points; ranks from "Raging Rookie" to "Human Calculator" (from older 3P handbooks; may differ now) — [Live Mathletics user guide (PDF)](https://marketing-cdn.3plearning.com/uploads/docs/user-guides/Live-Mathletics_User-Guide_A4.pdf); [3P Learning support: Mathematics fluency](https://support.3plearning.com/mathletics/for-home/student-mathematics-fluency)

**99math**
- Live multiplayer math game; students join at join.99math.com with a game code and nickname, no account needed; teachers can set up a game in about a minute and challenge another class in the same school or elsewhere in the world; detailed per-student results after each game — [Educators Technology: 99math review, June 2023](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/06/a-teachers-review-of-99math.html); [99math blog, 25 Sept 2020](https://blog.99math.com/post/en-teen-entrepreneur-revolutionizes-learning-math-through-competition-and-games)
- Grade range disputed across reviews (K-6, 1-8, 1-12); company materials say K-12 — [99math review (ailistingtool)](https://ailistingtool.com/blog/99math-review); [Makerstations: Is 99math worth using?](https://www.makerstations.io/99math/)

**Sumdog**
- Questions adapt to each child's level, and contest ranking is by accuracy, not speed — i.e., NOT the same set for everyone — [Sumdog: MAT-wide contests](https://learn.sumdog.com/en-gb/schools/maths/contests/how-mat-contests-work); [Bishopton PS (Glow Scotland) blog](https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk/re/bishopton/?p=21589)

**Prodigy Math**
- Role-playing game in which players solve math problems to win battles and cast spells (math questions gate gameplay) — [Wikipedia: Prodigy Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodigy_Education)

**Socrative (Showbie)**
- Free plan: 5 quizzes, 1 room, 50 students per activity, 30-day report history; Pro: 30 rooms, 300 students per activity — [Socrative plans](https://socrative.com/plans/)
- Space Race (team race where a correct answer moves a team's rocket) is a long-standing Socrative activity, but this pass found no current official documentation of it (see Gaps).

**Nearpod Time to Climb**
- Students "race to the top of the mountain against the clock" answering multiple-choice questions; teacher sets time per question; 2-4 answer options, exactly one correct; "scores are calculated based on speed and accuracy" (weighting undisclosed) — [Nearpod Time to Climb FAQ (ISTE launch PDF)](https://news.nearpod.com/pdf/Time-to-Climb-FAQs-ISTE-Launch.pdf); [Dearborn Schools tech coaches, May 2022](https://techcoaches.dearbornschools.org/2022/05/11/new-features-in-nearpods-time-to-climb)

**Factile (Jeopardy-style)**
- Jeopardy board game; optional Buzzer Mode turns devices into buzzers (paid); players join by 4-digit PIN or QR; up to 100 teams / 500 players; host chooses what each device shows; "Buzz-In Shuffle" randomizes who may buzz each round — [Factile plans](https://www.playfactile.com/accountFeatures); [Factile blog: why we built buzzer mode](https://www.playfactile.com/fr/blog/why-we-built-buzzer-mode/)

**Desmos / Amplify Classroom; Mathigon / Polypad**
- Desmos Classroom merged into Amplify Classroom (classroom.amplify.com) in June 2025; free library of 1,000+ lessons and the activity builder remain free; Amplify Desmos Math is the separate paid curriculum — [Amplify: Introducing Amplify Classroom](https://amplify.com/blog/problem-based-learning/introducing-amplify-classroom/); [Amplify and Desmos](https://amplify.com/amplify-and-desmos/)
- Amplify acquired Mathigon (Polypad virtual manipulatives) on 13 Oct 2021 (not 2023); Polypad stays free; ~600,000 monthly students and teachers at the time — [Amplify press release, 13 Oct 2021](https://amplify.com/news/amplify-acquires-k-12-mathematics-education-innovator-mathigon/)
- These are rich, notation-native activity tools rather than scored quiz games; no evidence found of a competitive scoring or tournament mode.

### Inferences
- "Same set to everyone, scored on accuracy" is a minority default: only Kahoot!'s optional Accuracy mode (since May 2025), Factile (host-judged), and Sumdog contests (accuracy ranking, but adaptive questions) come close. A product that defaults to a fixed, checked set scored on correctness, with speed only as a tie-breaker, would be differentiated.
- Every mainstream quiz game treats math as plain text plus a symbol palette; none found renders LaTeX in a live game or accepts structured math answers (exponents, fractions) from students. A math-native renderer and answer checker (exact-fraction evaluation, as Aimtrue's bank already does) is a real gap.
- Adaptive math products (Sumdog, Prodigy, Mathletics, 99math) cannot claim "everyone answered the same paper", which is what a competition or a math club's selection round needs.

### Gaps
- Wayground's current free-tier caps, max live players, and whether its equation editor accepts LaTeX: no official page retrieved (help center not reachable in this pass). A Quizalize (different product) help page describes a LaTeX-preview editor — not evidence for Wayground.
- Kahoot!'s exact speed-points formula (support.kahoot.com returned 403).
- Socrative Space Race and Quizlet Live current rules: no official 2026 documentation retrieved.
- 99math's scoring formula and whether everyone in a game gets the same questions: not documented in sources found.
- Prodigy and Sumdog "battle"/multiplayer modes in detail: not researched in depth.

## Q2. Pricing, business models and scale

### Takeaway
Classroom quiz games are freemium per-teacher SaaS ($36-$228/yr per teacher at Kahoot!, ~$60/yr at Blooket and Gimkit, $120/yr at Socrative Essentials, $48/yr at Factile) with quote-based school/district licences; the math-practice games (Prodigy, Mathletics, Sumdog) are school-subscription or consumer-freemium. Kahoot! is the giant (taken private for ~$1.7B, delisted Jan 2024); most others are private with little public financial data.

Cumulative 1 billion players and 40M MAUs (undated article) — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/brief/kahoot-1-billion-players/). No public figures since going private.

### Inferences
- The per-teacher price point for a "game show" tool clusters at ~$36-$150/yr; a tutoring-center feature would compete against near-free tools on price, so differentiation must be on math quality, fairness and cross-organization competition, not on the quiz mechanic itself.
- Player caps on free tiers (Kahoot! 10-40, Gimkit Pro modes 5, Socrative 50, Blooket 60) are the main upsell lever; a tutoring center's groups are small, so caps rarely bind for them but would bind for a multi-center tournament.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025-2026 user or revenue figures for Kahoot! (private), Blooket, Gimkit, Wayground, Sumdog or Prodigy.
- Kahoot!'s 2021 acquisition of Clever and its ownership of math apps (DragonBox) were not re-verified in this pass.
- Official current Prodigy membership prices and Blooket Plus prices were not retrieved from the vendors' own pages.

## Q3. Known criticisms from teachers and research

### Takeaway
The recurring criticisms are: speed scoring rewards the fastest rather than the most correct (enough that Kahoot! shipped an Accuracy mode in 2025); luck and economy mechanics (Blooket chests, Gimkit upgrades) decide winners independent of knowledge; content tends to recall-level multiple choice; math notation and typed math answers are poor; and Prodigy's in-game upsells to children drew a 2021 FTC complaint. Research finds low-stakes ungraded quiz games lower anxiety overall, but speed scoring specifically is a reported stressor.

### Cited Findings
- Adkins-Jablonsky et al. (CBE—Life Sciences Education, 2021; n=694 and n=60 intro biology): students rated ungraded Kahoot! play as less anxiety-provoking than group work or textbook reading; lower-performing students showed greater engagement and lower anxiety — [CBE-LSE](https://lifescied.org/doi/10.1187/cbe.20-08-0187); vendor summary at [Kahoot! blog, Sept 2026](https://kahoot.com/blog/2026/09/14/kahoot-impact-high-stakes-lecture-courses-benefit-from-low-stakes-game-based-review/)
- A study of a Kahoot-style quiz tool notes that because faster answers score higher, students "may feel anxiety and stress and may risk too much at the expense of spending enough time to find the right answer"; another reports anxiety in 25% of students tied to limited time in ~27% (methodology not verified) — [i-JEP article (PDF)](https://online-journals.org/index.php/i-jep/article/download/21359/9529/75849); [Swansea University study (PDF)](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa60505/Download/60505__24931__6b3f4277a44040b1a76e74b8604d8e7c.pdf)
- Kahoot! itself framed Accuracy mode as answering teachers who "wanted a way to slow down the pace and focus on what matters most—accuracy" — [Kahoot! blog, Sept 2025](https://kahoot.com/blog/2025/09/04/back-to-school-season-with-kahoot/); long-running teacher requests in [Kahoot community thread](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360037566554-Score-without-being-the-fastest)
- Blooket's Gold Quest is explicitly random ("every chest is random"; steal, swap, dragon) — [Blooket help](https://help.blooket.com/hc/en-us/articles/42100784373655-Game-Overview-Gold-Quest); players complain of losing gold despite correct answers and that stealing "feels like cheating" (anonymous reviews) — [Trustpilot: Blooket](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/blooket.com)
- Gimkit: teacher reviews say power-ups give students "too much power" and don't always reinforce learning; some students chase in-game cash more than content; harder for younger students than Kahoot! — [Common Sense Education teacher reviews](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/5029045/teacher-reviews); [Eastern Herald: cons of Gimkit, Aug 2025](https://easternherald.com/2025/08/23/cons-of-gimkit/)
- A high-school science teacher's action research questioned whether Blooket "detracts from the lesson objective" after observing student behaviour — [SUNY SOAR thesis](https://soar.suny.edu/items/ec58b91f-2e00-48ee-b555-68164d73fa27); a Minnesota high school's proposed Blooket ban prompted a student petition — [Change.org: Stop the Blooket ban](https://www.change.org/p/stop-the-blooket-ban)
- Math notation: Kahoot! offers only a symbol palette, and typed answers reportedly accept only letters and numerals — [Kahoot! blog 2020](https://kahoot.com/blog/2020/10/13/math-symbol-editor-kahoot/); [Kahoot community post](https://support.kahoot.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/44325420738323-SPECIAL-CHARACTERS-IN-ANSWER-TYPES). Nearpod Time to Climb allows only 2-4 multiple-choice options — [Nearpod FAQ PDF](https://news.nearpod.com/pdf/Time-to-Climb-FAQs-ISTE-Launch.pdf)
- Prodigy: Feb 2021 FTC complaint by 22 groups led by the Campaign for a Commercial-Free Childhood (now Fairplay) alleged "bait-and-switch" — marketed to schools as free while pushing children to buy memberships for items and faster levelling; argued it creates classroom inequality and lacks substantiation for learning claims; no FTC action found — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/prodigy-math-game-ftc-complaint-d693af67-019a-4ff5-9bff-8c73d1277342.html); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/360719/ftc-urged-to-investigate-prodigy-math-game-over-al.html). Reviews still describe aggressive in-game upsell (one group counted 13 offers in 20 minutes; user reports) — [HomeschoolFox](https://homeschoolfox.com/curriculum/prodigy-math)
- Cheating/answer-sharing: a large ecosystem of SEO "cheat"/bot/flooder pages targets Kahoot!, Blooket and Gimkit (search results dominated by such pages; claims of vendor anti-cheat detection could not be verified from official sources) — e.g. [Cal Poly-hosted page "5 ways to cheat Kahoot"](https://arlevent.ime.calpoly.edu/cheating-in-kahoot)

### Inferences
- A checked bank (Aimtrue's ~18,500 questions with code-verified keys and blind AI solve) directly answers the "shallow, teacher-typed, sometimes wrong" content critique; accuracy-first scoring answers the speed critique; no luck mechanics answers the Blooket/Gimkit critique.
- Because live quiz games are known targets for answer bots and flooders, a competition feature should assume adversarial clients: server-side timing, signed-in participants rather than anonymous nicknames, and answers checked server-side.

### Gaps
- No EdWeek or EdSurge article directly on "shallow learning" in Blooket/Gimkit was retrieved; no r/Teachers threads were fetched (Reddit not searched in this pass).
- No controlled study comparing speed-weighted vs accuracy-only scoring for slower students was found.

## Q4. How they handle under-13 users (accounts vs nicknames, COPPA/FERPA, school consent)

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is "join with a PIN and a nickname, no account" for live play (Kahoot!, Gimkit, 99math, Quizlet Live), which keeps data near zero but makes persistent identity, tracking over time and fair competition impossible; accounts are needed for assignments/progress, where vendors rely on the COPPA school-consent route (the teacher/school consents as the parent's agent). Prodigy is the outlier for marketing to children at home.

### Cited Findings
- Kahoot!: students join with PIN and nickname, no login; the student privacy policy says only an anonymous nickname "not linked to a persistent identifier" is collected from a player; optional Player ID (select plans) requires an email/identifier so the teacher's report links results over time; under-13 account holders give name and email only — [Kahoot! Trust: For teachers](https://trust.kahoot.com/teachers/); [SDPC district agreement exhibit, July 2024 (PDF)](https://sdpc.a4l.org/agreements/2024-07-18_11423_53_exhibita.pdf); [Common Sense Privacy: Kahoot](https://privacy.commonsense.org/privacy-report/kahoot)
- Kahoot! recommends keeping Player ID off and using the nickname generator; Durham College advises students "should not be directed to create an account" — [Kahoot! Trust](https://trust.kahoot.com/teachers/); [Durham College](https://durhamcollege.ca/ctl/educational-tech/kahoot/)
- Blooket: students invited to play/homework only create a username; under-13 users need verifiable parental consent; ad-free, does not sell or rent personal information; teachers and students can message each other and the policy does not say whether interactions are moderated (Common Sense evaluation) — [Common Sense Privacy: Blooket evaluation](https://privacy.commonsense.org/evaluation/Blooket); [Blooket privacy (via district notice)](https://governance.grasslands.ab.ca/api/documents/SYS-004_Parent_Notice/blob)
- Gimkit: students need no account to play or contribute to KitCollab; accounts needed for homework assignments and progress tracking; states adherence to FERPA and COPPA; terms say the educator may act as the parent's agent to consent for children under the minimum age; Google Workspace for Education admins must mark Gimkit "trusted" for under-18 school accounts — [Gimkit help: mark as trusted](https://help.gimkit.com/en/article/mark-gimkit-as-trusted-in-google-workspace-for-education-1r3kqms/); [Rockford (MN) district Gimkit page](https://www.rockford.k12.mn.us/district/departments/technology/digital-tools/gimkit); [Green Bay APS data privacy agreement (PDF)](https://sdpc.A4L.org/agreements/Gimkit_GreenBayAreaPublicSchools.pdf)
- 99math: students join with game code and nickname, no individual accounts — [Educators Technology](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/06/a-teachers-review-of-99math.html)
- Socrative via Showbie: only student display name and email (if present) are shared — [Showbie support](https://support.showbie.com/en/articles/7043349-how-does-showbie-integrate-with-socrative)
- Quizlet Live: students enter their real names on joining (2023 guide) — [Educators Technology](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2023/05/what-is-quizlet-live-and-how-to-use-it.html)
- Prodigy: children who play at school are shown membership ads when they play at home — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodigy_Education); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/prodigy-math-game-ftc-complaint-d693af67-019a-4ff5-9bff-8c73d1277342.html)
- Districts govern these tools through Student Data Privacy Consortium agreements and parent notices (opt-out, view, correct, delete) — [SDPC agreements](https://sdpc.A4L.org/agreements/Gimkit_GreenBayAreaPublicSchools.pdf); [Grasslands parent notice](https://governance.grasslands.ab.ca/api/documents/SYS-004_Parent_Notice/blob)

### Inferences
- The nickname model is privacy-light but it is exactly why classroom games cannot run trustworthy leaderboards or cross-school tournaments: there is no verified participant. A tutoring platform whose students are already enrolled members of an organization, with guardians on file, can run identified competitions without collecting anything new — a structural advantage.
- Showing a child's name on a cross-organization leaderboard would be a new disclosure; nickname or initials display, organization-level opt-in and guardian visibility would be the expected pattern given the school-consent norms above.

### Gaps
- Wayground's, Sumdog's and Mathletics' current COPPA/FERPA statements were not fetched.
- Common Sense privacy grades (letter/percent) for each product were not retrieved.

## Q5. Cross-school or public tournaments, leagues and events

### Takeaway
Organised cross-school competition exists mainly in the math-practice products, not the general quiz games: Sumdog runs free week-long contests ranked by accuracy (national, regional, trust-wide), 3P Learning runs World Maths Day (free, global, 20 x 60-second live challenges), and 99math runs global "Math Game Days" and seasonal leagues. All three adapt questions to the child, so they are fluency/effort contests rather than same-paper competitions. Kahoot!, Blooket, Gimkit and Wayground are class-scoped; no recurring public tournament for them was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Sumdog contests:** free to enter with a teacher account (US page: "Register for a free teacher account to enter our math contests!"); pupils play normal Sumdog games in class or at home and all answers count; ranked by accuracy not speed, questions adapt to each child so "any class... can win"; typically a week (one school: 8am Friday to 8pm the following Thursday); up to 1,000 questions per pupil (~1 hour) — [Sumdog US: math games contests](https://www.sumdog.com/us/math-games-contests/); [Sumdog: MAT-wide contests](https://learn.sumdog.com/en-gb/schools/maths/contests/how-mat-contests-work); [US Sumdog contest letter to parents (PDF)](https://content.sumdog.com/hubfs/Marketing/Contests/US_Sumdog_Contest_Letter-To_Parents_V1.pdf); [Bishopton PS blog](https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk/re/bishopton/?p=21589)
- Sumdog's Maths Week Scotland contest has run every year since 2017; 2026 edition ran 21-25 Sept 2026 with trophies for top classes; every Scottish class may enter whether or not the school subscribes; Sumdog also runs trust-wide (multi-academy trust) and regional contests — [Sumdog: Maths Week Scotland 2026](https://learn.sumdog.com/en-gb/maths-week-scotland-2026); [Sumdog MAT contests](https://learn.sumdog.com/en-gb/schools/maths/contests/how-mat-contests-work)
- **World Maths Day (3P Learning/Mathletics):** free, open to schools and home learners; non-subscribers get Mathletics access for the event; students compete worldwide in 20 x 60-second live challenges; 2026 edition held around 25 March 2026 per search results; historic claim of 10 million students answering over 1 billion questions (press release, date unclear); Wikipedia's "third Friday of September" date conflicts with the March dates in 3P sources — [3P Learning: World Maths Day FAQ](https://www.3plearning.com/world-maths-day/faq/); [EWA press release](https://ewa.org/members-news/press-releases/world-math-day-starts-march-25-in-sydney-australia-registration-is-free-and-open-now-at-mathletics-com); [Mathletics KB: What is World Maths Day](https://knowledgebase.mathletics.com/what-is-world-maths-day); [Wikipedia: World Maths Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Maths_Day)
- **99math:** "Math Game Days" bring thousands of students together for live online math battles; first year (c. 2019-20) >30,000 students from >22 countries; teachers can challenge a class anywhere; 2025 posts reference "Spring Speedsters", "Math Madness" (March 2025 competition) and a "Grand Finale and Yearly League Champions" — [99math blog](https://blog.99math.com/post/en-teen-entrepreneur-revolutionizes-learning-math-through-competition-and-games); [99math: Fun math challenges for distance learning](https://blog.99math.com/post/fun-math-challenges-for-distance-learning)
- **Live Mathletics:** always-on World Challenge matchmaking against online students at the same ranking (asynchronous "Shadow Mode" too) — [Live Mathletics user guide (PDF)](https://marketing-cdn.3plearning.com/uploads/docs/user-guides/Live-Mathletics_User-Guide_A4.pdf)
- World Education Games (the older 3P global event that included World Maths Day) — [Wikipedia: World Education Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Education_Games)
- **Blooket:** "early event access" is listed as a Plus perk (in-game seasonal events, not inter-school competitions) — [Nibble: Blooket pricing](https://nibble-app.com/blog/blooket-pricing)

### Inferences
- The live competition market splits into (1) class-scoped quiz shows with no identity and no cross-school play, and (2) adaptive fluency contests that rank effort/accuracy on different questions. Nobody found offers a recurring, cross-organization competition on a fixed, verified, standards-tagged math paper (MATHCOUNTS/AMC-style but run in-platform), where tutoring centers or clubs can enter teams and see a fair league table. That is the clearest gap for Aimtrue: its bank is already standards-tagged (NC 2026, grades 3-8 and Math 1), its keys are code-checked, and its participants are identified members of organizations.
- Sumdog's design choices are a useful template for fairness at scale: a multi-day window rather than one live slot (time zones, schedules), accuracy over speed, and a cap on questions so that time spent does not dominate.

### Gaps
- Prodigy's events/tournaments: none found in this pass.
- Kahoot! global/public events (e.g., world-record or brand-run live games): not found in this pass.
- 99math 2026 event calendar, entry rules and prize structure: official site returned only a title; details not retrieved.
- Formal competition products (MATHCOUNTS, AMC, Math Kangaroo, Math League) are outside this scope and not covered here.

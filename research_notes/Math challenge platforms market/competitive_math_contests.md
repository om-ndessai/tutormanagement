# Competitive Math Communities, Contests and Online Competition Platforms (grades 3-12), as of October 2026

Researched 2026-10-21. Every figure carries the date it applies to; "older" marks information from before the 2025-26 season. Secondary sources (tutoring-company blogs, student newspapers) are flagged where an official source was not reached. Several official MAA PDFs returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher, so some AMC facts rest on search-result excerpts of those PDFs or on secondary coverage.

## 1. Art of Problem Solving: For The Win! (FTW), Alcumus, Beast Academy Online, and AoPS's role in contest prep

### Takeaway
FTW is still live in October 2026 at artofproblemsolving.com/ftw. It is AoPS's "Countdown Arena": everyone in a game answers the same problem, and the score counts both correctness and speed. Ranked games move a player rating that can go up or down, and leaderboards track it. Its live traffic is small (a snapshot showed 14 players online). Alcumus is AoPS's free adaptive practice tool. It is driven by a per-topic 0-100 rating, defined as the probability of solving an average problem in that topic. AoPS also builds the official MATHCOUNTS Trainer.

### Cited Findings
- FTW is "the Art of Problem Solving competition arena for MATHCOUNTS-style problems". Problems come from MATHCOUNTS, MOEMS, the American Mathematics Competitions and CEMC (Waterloo) "among others". The page heading says "Over 15,000 Problems", but its live stats panel showed 3,662 total problems; the page does not explain the difference (fetched 2026-10-21) -- [AoPS FTW](https://artofproblemsolving.com/ftw)
- Scoring and play (fetched 2026-10-21):
  - In group play "all players have an opportunity to answer, and will be scored based on both the correctness and speed with which they respond".
  - A creator can "include multiple contenders in your battle", and games "can also be customized by the match creator".
  - Ranked games move a player ranking, and "what goes up may also come down!"
  - Leaderboards show the highest ranking, most games played and most wins over several time periods.
  - A "Play as guest" option exists.
  - Source: [AoPS FTW](https://artofproblemsolving.com/ftw)
- Live counters on 2026-10-21: 488 games played, 915 total players and 14 players live. The window these counts cover is not stated, and may be a rolling day. -- [AoPS FTW](https://artofproblemsolving.com/ftw)
- Older (AoPS wiki, describing the earlier Flash-era FTW):
  - There were two game types: a normal game against many players, and a head-to-head "Countdown" game modelled on the MATHCOUNTS Countdown Round.
  - The wiki says that version "has been deactivated" in favour of a new one.
  - Source: [AoPS Wiki: FTW](https://wiki.artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/FTW) (search excerpt; the page itself refused connection on 2026-10-21)
- Alcumus (AoPS blog):
  - It is a free adaptive tool. An engine over a large problem database "rates" students per topic, on a scale of 0-100.
  - A correct answer raises the rating and a wrong answer lowers it.
  - The rating is the probability of answering an average problem in that topic correctly, so 75 means three in four are expected right.
  - Students may retry after a mistake.
  - Source: [AoPS blog: Alcumus, a peek under the hood](https://artofproblemsolving.com/blog/articles/alcumus-a-peek-under-the-hood-of-our-adaptive-learning-tool)
- The official MATHCOUNTS competition page (2026-27) links "MATHCOUNTS Trainer (Art of Problem Solving)" among its practice tools, alongside the OPLET problem database, the Problem of the Week archive and "Brilliant for Educators" -- [MATHCOUNTS Competition Series](https://www.mathcounts.org/comp)

### Inferences
- FTW shows that the "same problem to everyone, scored on correctness plus speed, rated" loop works for MATHCOUNTS-age students. Its small live population (14 online at one sampling) suggests the space is under-served, not saturated. That is one snapshot, not a trend.
- Alcumus's rating is a calibrated probability, not an Elo number. That makes it explainable to parents ("75 = gets 3 of 4 right"), which is a useful model for a tutoring platform's per-topic mastery score next to a competitive rating.

### Gaps
- FTW's current mode list could not be confirmed from an official page: blitz, team games, private or custom rooms, problems per game, time per problem, the speed-scoring formula, the rating formula and its starting value. The AoPS wiki was unreachable and the FTW page gives no formula.
- FTW's cost (free vs. AoPS-account-gated) and its relaunch date are not stated on the page.
- No source was found on Beast Academy Online's gamified elements (stars, badges, leaderboards) or on Alcumus badges or leaderboards.
- AoPS online contests or club programs (for example AoPS-run mock contests, or the "MATHCOUNTS Trainer" feature set) were not researched in depth.

## 2. MATHCOUNTS: Competition Series, Countdown Round, fees, Math Club and online tools

### Takeaway
MATHCOUNTS (grades 6-8) is the template for "challenges and clashes":
- written timed rounds that every competitor gets in the same form:
  - Sprint: 30 problems in 40 minutes, no calculator.
  - Target: 4 pairs of problems, 6 minutes per pair, calculator allowed.
  - Team: 10 problems in 20 minutes, 4 students together.
- an oral head-to-head Countdown Round: 45 seconds per problem, first correct answer scores, bracketed.

2026-27 registration costs $42-$53 per school competitor, or $75-$85 for a non-school competitor.

### Cited Findings
- Round formats (official, 2026-27):
  - Sprint: "40 minutes to complete 30 math problems without a calculator".
  - Target: 4 pairs of problems, 6 minutes per pair, calculator assumed.
  - Team: "20 minutes to complete 10 math problems, assuming the use of a calculator"; only a school's 4 team members take it officially.
  - Countdown: "a maximum of 45 seconds per problem without a calculator"; optional at school, chapter and state.
  - All rounds together take about 3 hours.
  - Source: [MATHCOUNTS Competition Series](https://www.mathcounts.org/comp)
- Structure (official, 2026-27):
  - School practice runs Aug 2026-Jan 2027, and coaches give the School Competition from November.
  - Chapter competitions run Feb 1-28, 2027, at about 500 chapter competitions. State runs Mar 1-31, 2027. The RTX MATHCOUNTS National Competition is May 9-10, 2027.
  - A school may send up to 14 competitors: 4 on the team, who also compete individually, and 10 individuals.
  - Non-school competitors (NSCs) compete as individuals.
  - The top 4 individuals per state go to nationals as that state's team.
  - Source: [MATHCOUNTS Competition Series](https://www.mathcounts.org/comp)
- Scoring (secondary sources: a Think Academy guide and the AoPS wiki):
  - Individual score = correct Sprint answers + 2 x correct Target answers, for a maximum of 46.
  - Team score = average of the members' individual scores + 2 per correct Team answer, for a maximum of 66.
  - Sources: [Think Academy 2026 MATHCOUNTS guide](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/?p=16296); [AoPS Wiki: MathCounts](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/MathCounts)
- Countdown bracket (AoPS wiki, community-maintained; not the official rulebook):
  - At nationals the top 12 written finishers enter a single-elimination bracket, and the top 4 get byes.
  - In early rounds a problem is shown and both players have 45 s. The first correct answer scores 1 point, and whoever has the most correct out of 3 questions wins the match.
  - From the top four onward, the first to 3 correct answers wins.
  - When chapter or state make it official, the bracket starts with 10th place against 9th place.
  - Source: [AoPS Wiki: MathCounts](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/MathCounts)
- 2026-27 fees and dates:
  - The early-bird rate is $42 per chapter competitor, by Nov 3, 2026 (official).
  - Regular, by Dec 15, 2026: $48 per school competitor and $75 per NSC (secondary).
  - Late: $53 per school competitor and $85 per NSC (secondary).
  - Registration opened Aug 17, 2026. NSC registration opens Nov 5, 2026, online only (secondary).
  - Nationals has 224 competitors (secondary).
  - Sources: [MATHCOUNTS registration announcement](https://www.mathcounts.org/about/mathcounts-competition-series-registration-now-open); [Oriel Admissions](https://orieladmissions.com/?p=6253); [Cuemath](https://cuemath.com/blog/mathcounts-explained/)
- Online tools linked officially (2026-27): MATHCOUNTS Trainer (built by AoPS), the OPLET Problem Database, the Problem of the Week archive, past competitions and practice plans, and Brilliant for Educators. The page also lists a separate national Math Club program, without details. -- [MATHCOUNTS Competition Series](https://www.mathcounts.org/comp)

### Inferences
- The Sprint, Target and Countdown trio maps directly onto platform features:
  - Sprint: a timed tournament where every participant gets the same set.
  - Target: paired problems with a short timer per pair.
  - Team: a collaborative round.
  - Countdown: a live head-to-head where the fastest correct answer scores.
- Weighting Target problems 2x shows the convention of rewarding harder, slower problems more than speed items.
- A "Countdown" clash in a tutoring app would be instantly familiar to MATHCOUNTS families. A 45-second cap and a best-of-3 or first-to-3 match are the native conventions.

### Gaps
- The virtual or online formats MATHCOUNTS used from 2020 (for example a virtual 2021 season) were not found in this session's sources.
- The current Math Club program rules (activities, any online component) are not described on the page fetched.
- Official tiebreak rules for written rounds were not retrieved. They are in the official Competition Rules & FAQ ([mathcounts.org/competition-rules-faq](https://www.mathcounts.org/competition-rules-faq)), which was not fetched.
- The Title I half-fee claim appears in only one third-party blog and is unverified.

## 3. MAA AMC 8/10/12: administration, timing, scoring, integrity, and the 2023-2026 leak crisis and response

### Takeaway
The AMC is the largest US school contest, taken by about 300,000 students a year in 2025-26. It is scored 6 per correct answer, 1.5 per blank and 0 per wrong answer, for a maximum of 150. It may be administered on paper or digitally on site, and remote proctoring is banned.

Organised leaks became a paid industry:
- In the 2025 cycle, Discord servers sold solutions at $200-$400 per test, often solved with generative AI. One AMC 12A file was posted a full day early.
- Perfect scores on the AMC 12A rose from fewer than 30 (2022) to "well over 300" (2025 cycle).

The MAA responded on Aug 26, 2026, for the 2026-27 cycle:
- AIME qualification moved to fixed AMC cutoffs: 100 on the AMC 10 and 85 on the AMC 12.
- The AIME itself moves to computer-based testing at Pearson centres on Feb 5-6, 2027, with a $85 fee and two 90-minute parts.
- Students under 13 are excluded.
- USAMO/USAJMO selection is shifting toward the AIME alone.

### Cited Findings
- Format and dates:
  - The 2025 AMC 10A/12A was on Nov 5, 2025 and the 10B/12B on Nov 13, 2025.
  - Scoring is 6 per correct, 1.5 per blank and 0 per wrong, for a maximum of 150.
  - Each test has 25 questions.
  - One secondary guide lists the 12B on Nov 11, which conflicts with the other sources.
  - Sources: [Math Prep Pro AMC 10 2025](https://www.mathpreppro.com/blog/amc-10-2025-guide); [Math Prep Pro AMC 12 2025](https://www.mathpreppro.com/blog/amc-12-2025-guide); [A-One Institute](https://blog.a-oneinstitute.com/article/146)
- 2026 AMC 10/12 dates are Nov 5 and Nov 13, 2026 (Harker Aquila, published Sep 17, 2026) -- [Harker Aquila](https://harkeraquila.com/99024/science-and-technology/maa-overhauls-aime-rules-over-cheating-concerns/)
- Administration rules (2025-26 manuals; search excerpts, because the PDFs returned 403):
  - "Remote proctoring is not allowed", including as an alternative when a school is closed. Students found to have tested remotely have their scores cancelled.
  - No online or public discussion is allowed until 8 a.m. ET the day after the competition window ends.
  - Spreading problems "via phone, email, or digital media of any type" means disqualification.
  - Sources: [2025-26 AMC 8 Teachers Manual](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-26-AMC-8-Teachers-Manual.pdf); [2025 AMC 10/12 Teachers Manual](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-AMC-10AMC-12-Teachers-Manual.pdf)
- Digital delivery (2025 AMC 10/12 Competition Manager guide):
  - The portal is split into "Digital administration" and "Print and scan administration", and a manager may run the test digitally, on paper, or both.
  - Print-and-scan sheets that show no scores have been "flagged for manual review".
  - Sources: [Guide du responsable AMC 10/12 (2025)](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Guide-du-responsable-AMC-10-AMC-12.pdf); [2025-26 AMC 8 manual](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-26-AMC-8-Teachers-Manual.pdf)
- Older (2022-23): proctoring "only ... in person with an approved Competition Manager unrelated to the student" -- [MAA 2022-23 AMC online policies](https://www.maa.org/math-competitions/2021-2022-amc-online-policies)
- The leak market, from an investigation published Feb 10, 2026:
  - The Discord tip came in May 2025 and concerned a ring of about 300 people.
  - Reporters reviewed 300+ messages, and three sellers ran 10+ servers with 30-600 members each.
  - At least 60 high-school and college students regularly sell AMC material.
  - Pre-orders open in late September and run through October.
  - In November 2025, one AMC test's solutions sold for $200-$400. AIME, USAMO and USAJMO material sold for $500 up to "tens of thousands".
  - An AMC 12A file posted "a full day before administration" was verified, as were two files posted before the contest began.
  - Sellers bribe or pose as officials, or register fake "independent math circles" with large member counts to receive papers 1-2 days early instead of hours.
  - Former USAMO/IMO qualifiers are hired to solve the 25 problems, "often with generative AI".
  - Source: [Lakeside Tatler, "Solutions for Sale"](https://tatler.lakesideschool.org/8567/showcase/solutions-for-sale-inside-the-discord-marketplaces-undermining-americas-top-math-competitions/)
- Score inflation (same source, citing The Free Press, 2026):
  - The AMC 12A had "fewer than 30 perfect scores nationwide" in 2022 against "well over 300" in the 2025 cycle, so the top-1% honour roll required a perfect 150.
  - First 2025 administration: AMC 10, 31,608 students at 1,956 schools; AMC 12, 20,438 students at 1,840 schools.
  - About 300,000 students take the AMC 8, 10 and 12 each year.
  - Source: [Lakeside Tatler](https://tatler.lakesideschool.org/8567/showcase/solutions-for-sale-inside-the-discord-marketplaces-undermining-americas-top-math-competitions/)
- Last year's AMC 12A/12B Distinguished Honor Roll needed 150 and 145.5 out of 150; in 2022, 126 and 129 met the cut (Harker Aquila, Sep 17, 2026) -- [Harker Aquila](https://harkeraquila.com/99024/science-and-technology/maa-overhauls-aime-rules-over-cheating-concerns/)
- 2025 AIME cutoffs were AMC 10A 105, 10B 99, 12A 96 and 12B 100.5 -- [A-One Institute](https://blog.a-oneinstitute.com/article/146)
- The MAA's interim response (Dec 23, 2025): "protecting the integrity of our competitions is an ongoing priority"; "strengthened security practices across exam development, administration, and review" for 2025-26; "some efforts are not shared publicly". -- [Lakeside Tatler](https://tatler.lakesideschool.org/8567/showcase/solutions-for-sale-inside-the-discord-marketplaces-undermining-americas-top-math-competitions/)
- The 2026-27 overhaul (official MAA news item):
  - AIME eligibility requires 100+ on the AMC 10 or 85+ on the AMC 12.
  - The AIME is offered Feb 5 and 6, 2027, at Pearson testing centres. The registration fee is $85.
  - The MAA cites community concerns about exam integrity.
  - Source: [MAA: Official Testing Centers for the 2027 AIME](https://maa.org/news/maa-announces-official-testing-centers-for-the-2027-american-invitational-mathematics-examination/)
- Overhaul details from secondary sources:
  - The rules were announced Aug 26, 2026.
  - The AIME is computer-based in English at 200+ Pearson Professional Centers in the US and Canada.
  - It has two 90-minute parts, questions 1-8 then 9-15, and students cannot return to Part 1.
  - The centre supplies laminated graph paper and wet-erase markers, and compasses and rulers are barred.
  - Students must be at least 13 on test day.
  - Sources: [Harker Aquila](https://harkeraquila.com/99024/science-and-technology/maa-overhauls-aime-rules-over-cheating-concerns/); [Think Academy AIME 2027](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/all-about-aime-qualification-competition-difficulty-and-key-knowledge-points/); [AoPS Wiki: AIME](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/American_Invitational_Mathematics_Examination); [Math Prep Pro AIME 2027 changes](https://www.mathpreppro.com/blog/aime-2027-changes-new-qualifying-scores-format-and-rules); [CompeteMap](https://competemap.com/blog/maths-competitions/aime-changes-pearson-testing-fixed-amc-cutoffs-usajmo-usamo)
- USAMO/USAJMO index (MAA-sourced reporting, as of Sep 16, 2026):
  - 2025: AMC + 10 x AIME.
  - 2026: AMC + 20 x AIME.
  - 2027: AIME only, so a leak-inflated AMC no longer carries a student to the olympiad.
  - Source: [A-One Institute](https://blog.a-oneinstitute.com/article/146)
- 2025 AMC 8: 162 perfect scores worldwide, and 57 students on the Distinguished Honor Roll (top 1%) (reported Feb 14, 2025) -- [Ivy League Center](https://ivyleaguecenter.org/2025/02/14/2025-amc-8-results-just-announced-19-students-received-perfect-scores/)
- Older (2023): the AMC 10A/12A leaked (widely in China, per Tatler).
  - The MAA disqualified and permanently banned the source site.
  - It said it uses "human resources and artificial intelligence" to spot statistical anomalies.
  - Stuyvesant cancelled its 10A/12A.
  - Sources: [Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/stuyvesant-cancels-administration-of-the-amc-10a-and-amc-12a); [Saratoga Falcon](https://saratogafalcon.org/19217/features/amc-12a-test-leak-underscores-new-era-of-low-risk-high-reward-cheating/)

### Inferences
- The MAA's answer to cheating is structural rather than technical. It did not lock down the AMC itself. Instead it:
  - decoupled advancement from relative rank, using fixed cutoffs so cheaters cannot push honest students out;
  - moved the stakes to a secured, centre-proctored, computer-based later round (the AIME at Pearson);
  - is phasing the leakable round out of olympiad selection.

  This "low-stakes open round, high-stakes secured round" pattern is a usable model for a tutoring platform's challenges.
- The 1.5-point blank credit is the standard way contests discourage guessing. It is worth copying for any multiple-choice challenge where guessing would distort rankings.
- Any answer-only contest whose paper leaves the organiser's hands before the start can now be solved by AI within minutes. Secrecy windows of hours, not days, are no longer enough.

### Gaps
- AMC 10/12 time limit: the commonly stated 75 minutes for the AMC 10/12, and 40 minutes for the 25-question AMC 8, were not re-verified against an official 2025-26 document in this session, because the MAA PDFs returned 403.
- Official 2025/2026 AMC fee schedule: secondary sources disagree (one lists $33 early / $76 regular / $116 late, apparently per school bundle), so it is unverified.
- Whether the MAA randomised question order in 2025: one search excerpt said so but could not be tied to a specific source, so it is unverified.
- Whether the AMC 10/12 or the AMC 8 also move to test centres: the sources found say the change applies to the AIME only.
- 2026 AMC 8 statistics: one unattributed search excerpt reported 80,977 takers, an average of 12.42/25, a top 5% at 21 and a top 1% at 24. It could not be tied to a URL, so it is excluded from the findings.
- Tiebreaks: AMC awards and honour rolls use score thresholds, and no tiebreak rule was found.

## 4. Other school contests: Math Kangaroo, Purple Comet, mathleague.org, MOEMS, Noetic, Continental Math League, Online Math League

### Takeaway
Most other grade 2-12 contests are cheap ($18-$130), short (20-45 problems in 45-90 minutes), and multiple-choice or integer-answer.

They now deliver online in three integrity tiers:
- proctored at a centre;
- proctored remotely, by camera and an adult chaperone (mathleague.org on Zoom; Math Kangaroo's "Remote Proctored");
- unproctored and unofficial (Math Kangaroo "Virtual Self-Proctored", Noetic "At-Home"), which earns only a score or percentile and no awards.

Purple Comet, a free-form online team contest with a 10-day window, now explicitly bans generative AI.

### Cited Findings
- Math Kangaroo, 2026 season (secondary, plus the organiser memo):
  - It is one 75-minute test of five-option multiple-choice questions, on paper or online.
  - Grades 1-4 answer 24 questions for a maximum of 96. Grades 5-12 answer 30 for a maximum of 120.
  - A third of the questions are worth 3 points, a third 4 points and a third 5 points. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, and a blank scores 0.
  - Online delivery comes in three modes:
    - "Online at Center": proctored, with awards, an official score and a ranking.
    - "Remote (Home, Proctored)": an official score and ranking.
    - "Virtual (Self-Proctored)": unproctored, no gifts, and only a ranking range.
  - The 2026 fee was $18 at educational centres and $28 at remote centres, where gifts are shipped.
  - US participation was 60,504 in 2026, up from 53,562 in 2025.
  - The 2027 contest is on Mar 18, 2027, and registration opened Sep 15, 2026.
  - Sources: [Think Academy 2027 Math Kangaroo](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/?p=5573); [Math Kangaroo Memo 2026](https://mathkangaroo.org/mks/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Memo-2026-v.2.pdf); [Think Academy 2026 results](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/?p=21324)
- Purple Comet! Math Meet, 2026 (official):
  - It is an online team contest. The window ran Tuesday Apr 14 to Thursday Apr 23, 2026 (UTC), and the adult supervisor chooses when to start each team's clock.
  - Teams have 1-6 students. Middle school teams get 20 problems in 60 minutes, and high school teams 30 problems in 90 minutes.
  - Every answer is a non-negative integer, and teams may keep submitting until their clock expires.
  - Calculators and computers may be used for computation. Books and notes may be used only if they are physically at hand.
  - Students "may not query their Internet browser about how to solve a particular problem". They may not use "generative artificial intelligence tools (AI) including but not limited to ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or Claude" to produce, explain or verify answers.
  - For 2026 the site was rebuilt, the 150-mile team distance rule was dropped, and a MIXED TEAMS category covers multi-school teams.
  - Sources: [Purple Comet home](https://www.purplecomet.org/); [Purple Comet: Changes for 2026](https://purplecomet.org/changes)
- Purple Comet participation figures conflict: the official homepage showed 690 registered teams from 48 countries (an apparently early snapshot), while a third-party listing claimed over 4,800 teams from 86 countries -- [Purple Comet](https://www.purplecomet.org/); [Internshala listing](https://internshala.com/competitions/purple-comet-math-meet-2026/)
- mathleague.org online contests (official):
  - The contests run in a Zoom room. Cameras are mandatory and microphones muted.
  - An adult chaperone must be present but may not interact with the student.
  - Each student needs a separate computer with a keyboard; tablets and phones may not be used to access questions.
  - A director-announced password unlocks the questions, and students may not click outside the testing window.
  - Answers must be submitted the moment the timer hits zero, and late tests are refused.
  - Students may not share questions until every test with that "release number" has finished.
  - Results are posted within about 24 hours, and negative scores are not published.
  - Rounds are individual written tests plus a collaborative "Team, Power, or Relay" test. An optional Countdown Round runs at most contests with over 50 participants.
  - There are also a Number Sense Challenge and a Relay Challenge.
  - Source: [mathleague.org online contests](https://mathleague.org/onlinecontests.php)
- MOEMS (Math Olympiad for Elementary and Middle Schools):
  - It covers grades 4-8, with five monthly contests from November to March.
  - Each contest has a multi-week window, for example Contest 1 on Nov 10-Dec 1, 2025 and Contest 5 on Mar 2-23, 2026.
  - It is given in school, and results are sent to MOEMS for scoring.
  - Source: [RSM competitions calendar 2025-26](https://www.mathschool.com/blog/competitions/math-competitions-calendar-for-2025-2026)
- Noetic Learning Math Contest (secondary):
  - Grades 2-8, held twice a year, with 20 problems in 45 minutes on grade-specific papers.
  - Fall 2026: school registration by Oct 22, 2026, late registration Oct 23-Nov 5 for an extra $25, and a contest window of Nov 12-25, 2026.
  - A US school team costs $129 per contest for up to 30 students of one grade, and $159 outside the US.
  - "At-Home" costs $29 per student, is unofficial, and gives a percentile but no medals.
  - Sources: [Math Explorers Club: Noetic guide](https://www.mathexplorersclub.com/noetic-learning-math-contest-prep-guide); [Math Explorers Club: Noetic at home](https://www.mathexplorersclub.com/noetic-math-contest-at-home); [WuKong 2026-27 Noetic guide](https://www.wukongsch.com/blog/noetic-learning-math-contest-post-59548/)
- Continental Mathematics League (CML):
  - Grades 2-9 plus an AP Calculus League.
  - Grades 2-3 have 3 meets of 6 questions each. Grades 4-9 have 5 meets of 6 questions each. Calculus has 4 meets of 8 questions each.
  - Schools proctor and grade the meets.
  - Fees for the whole year are $90 per team for grades 2-3, and $100 for the first team and $85 for each additional team for grades 4-9 and Calculus.
  - Sources: [CML official](https://www.cmleague.com/product-category/cml/); [Gonit CML guide](https://gonit.app/continental-math-league/)

### Inferences
- The three-tier integrity model (centre, remote-proctored, self-proctored) is the industry's compromise: only proctored modes earn official ranks and awards. A tutoring platform's open "challenges" fit the self-proctored tier. Ranked "clashes" with prizes would need live supervision (a tutor on video) or a format where integrity matters less, such as fast live head-to-head play.
- Purple Comet's "start your own clock within a window" model is the standard way to run one problem set across time zones. It is also where leaks happen, which is why mathleague.org gates on "release numbers".
- Integer-only and multiple-choice answers dominate because they are machine-gradable. Math Kangaroo's 3/4/5 difficulty weights are a simple, transparent alternative to rating-weighted scoring.

### Gaps
- Online Math League: no 2026 information was found (grades, fees, format). Its site was not reached.
- MOEMS time limits and problem count per contest, and any online option, are not in the sources found. From memory, MOEMS has 5 problems per contest with some individually timed, but this is unverified.
- Purple Comet's fee (historically free) and its tie-break and scoring rules are not stated on the pages fetched.
- mathleague.org fees, problem counts, time limits and tiebreaks are on sub-pages that were not fetched.

## 5. Platforms for running and playing math competitions online: ContestDojo, MathDash, Brilliant, duel sites, and competitive-programming analogues

### Takeaway
The "rated real-time math" niche is real but small:
- MathDash, founded by two contest alumni, runs Kahoot-style synchronous contests with chess-style Elo and divisions, plus rated proof olympiads with partial credit. In mid-2024 it reported about 500 daily and 1,500 weekly active users.
- FTW is the incumbent, with a modest live population.
- Generic 1v1 Elo "math duel" sites exist for arithmetic speed.

Other players fill other roles:
- ContestDojo is the registration and seating back-office for student-run tournaments such as BMT.
- Brilliant's "leagues" are Duolingo-style weekly XP leagues: 30 people, promotion and relegation, 10 tiers. They reward effort, not head-to-head performance.
- Codeforces and AtCoder supply the mature model for rated contests:
  - the same problem set for everyone;
  - score and time penalties for wrong submissions;
  - per-round rules announcements;
  - "virtual participation" in past contests, unrated, ranked ICPC-style.

### Cited Findings
- MathDash (MIT Solve application; page last updated Jul 2, 2024):
  - Founders and company:
    - Founded by Daniel Sun (team lead) and Akshaj Kadaveru (co-founder), who met at 11 through an online math game.
    - The company is Breez Solutions, Inc., New York, a for-profit, at "Pilot" stage on mathdash.live.
  - Product:
    - "Live competitions" are synchronous and likened to Kahoot.
    - An initial evaluation assigns an "ELO rating and a division", in a system adapted from chess.
    - Students can also do solo practice, and post-contest discussion rooms, leaderboards and user-contributed problems are planned.
  - Problems and partners:
    - A customised ChatGPT helps brainstorm, check and tag problems, and a committee screens and approves every problem.
    - It has run contests with CMIMC, the Stanford Mathematics Tournament, AlphaStar Math Academy and the Minnesota Math Circle.
    - It reports working with Singapore Math and AoPS to bring their curricula to the platform.
  - Metrics: 500 daily and 1,500 weekly active users, 40% one-month retention and 20% week-over-week organic growth.
  - Business model: "currently everything is free", with freemium planned.
  - Target users: Pre-K-8 learners, mostly contest participants.
  - No anti-cheating measures are described.
  - Source: [MIT Solve: MathDash](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/90601)
- MathDash olympiads:
  - It ran "Live MathDash Internet Math Olympiad" rounds announced on Codeforces from 2024: rated, with prizes, on MathDash's own platform and its own rating system. Live Dash 1 drew 100+ participants.
  - In the third round, proof problems were worth 500, 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2500 points, each scaled by the proof's score out of 7. It introduced a new "Proof rating".
  - A rating change depends on the participant's score, their current rating and the problem's rating, which is set by the ratings of those who solved it. Only problems the participant opened count.
  - Grading was live in round 1, with unlimited resubmissions, and after the round in round 3.
  - Disputes are settled at the organisers' discretion in the post-contest chat.
  - Sources: [Codeforces: First Live MathDash Olympiad](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/131496); [Codeforces: Third MathDash Olympiad](https://codeforces.com/blog/Nsah159)
- mathdash.com now presents itself as "MathDash — Structured Math Training for AMC, MATHCOUNTS & AIME" (fetched 2026-10-21; only the title rendered). The domain differs from 2024's mathdash.live. -- [mathdash.com](https://mathdash.com)
- Generic 1v1 Elo math duels exist, mostly for arithmetic speed:
  - erudia.org runs ranked 60-second mental-math matches.
  - duel-games.net offers 1-, 3- and 5-minute matches with seven rank tiers.
  - Sources: [Erudia 1v1 Math](https://erudia.org/1v1-math/); [Duel Games: Math Duel](https://duel-games.net/games/math-duel)
- ContestDojo, as Berkeley Math Tournament uses it:
  - An adult creates a coach account, creates an "organization" (school, club or family), registers it for an event, and receives a join code.
  - Students create their own accounts and join with the code.
  - The coach buys seats and assigns students to teams. Seats belong to the organization, so rosters can be swapped without contacting the tournament.
  - Any adult (parent, tutor) may register as a coach.
  - Source: [BMT FAQs](https://berkeley.mt/faqs/)
- Brilliant leagues (help centre):
  - Leagues are weekly XP competitions of 30 learners. Learners join automatically on their first XP of the week, and weeks reset Sunday at 8 PM PT.
  - There are 10 levels, from Hydrogen to Einsteinium.
  - In the first five levels the top 15 are promoted, the middle 10 stay and the bottom 5 drop.
  - XP "corresponds to the time and effort required", and repeating a lesson earns none.
  - Daily Challenges also earn XP.
  - Sources: [Brilliant: Leagues FAQ](https://help.brilliant.org/en/articles/6399120-leagues-faq); [Brilliant: Leagues and leaderboards](https://brilliant.org/help/features/what-are-leagues-and-leaderboards)
- Codeforces (community and official blog posts):
  - A submission that fails pretests, or a resubmission, costs 50 points. First-test failures and similar verdicts are exempt.
  - Penalties apply only during the contest window.
  - Organisers may modify the default rules per round, in the round announcement.
  - Past contests are open as "virtual contests" under ACM-ICPC rules: ranked by problems solved, then total time with a penalty per wrong attempt, and no hacking in virtual mode. Participants are asked not to enter if they have already seen the tasks.
  - Multiple accounts are a rule violation.
  - Sources: [Codeforces blog 65949](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/65949); [Codeforces blog 4088 (rules)](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/4088); [Codeforces blog 21225 (virtual)](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/21225); [Codeforces blog 2607](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/2607?)
- AtCoder (rules page for one 2026 contest): the highest score wins, ties are broken by time, each wrong answer adds penalty time, and compile errors are not penalised -- [AtCoder contest rules](https://atcoder.jp/contests/ajo2026heuristic-qual/rules)
- A contrast with olympiad-style scoring: IOI ranks on score only and ignores wrong submissions, whereas most online contests penalise them -- [Codeforces blog 54033](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/54033)

### Inferences
- The competitive-programming model carries over to math almost one for one:
  - same set, fixed window: a "challenge" or tournament;
  - score, then time, then penalty per wrong answer: the ranking;
  - per-round announcement of rule tweaks;
  - rated vs. unrated rounds;
  - "virtual participation", replaying a past challenge unranked against its historical leaderboard.

  Virtual participation is especially useful to a tutoring platform: a student who missed a live challenge can still take it, without affecting the rating.
- MathDash's problem rating ("set by the ratings of those who solved it") is the standard Elo-on-items approach. It also gives automatic difficulty calibration of a question bank, which is valuable where questions are AI-written.
- Brilliant's leagues reward volume (XP) and are cheat-tolerant, because nothing is head-to-head. Head-to-head ratings (FTW, MathDash) reward skill but invite cheating and smurfing. A children's platform may want both: an effort league for everyone, and rated clashes for opt-in competitors.
- ContestDojo's coach, organization, join-code and seats model is the de facto workflow student-run tournaments use. A tutoring platform's "organization", with a tutor as coach, maps onto it naturally.

### Gaps
- ContestDojo's test-delivery features (whether it hosts timed online rounds, its proctoring, its pricing) were not found; only its registration role (BMT) is confirmed.
- MathDash's current (2026) status was not confirmed: users, pricing, whether it has 1v1 duels, and whether mathdash.com is the same company as mathdash.live. Only the title of mathdash.com rendered.
- Project Euler-style formats, other "chess.com for math" startups and Brilliant streak mechanics were not researched in this session.
- AtCoder's standard algorithm-contest rules (for example the usual 5-minute penalty) and its rating system were not fetched; only one heuristic contest's rules page was seen.

## 6. Integrity, AI risk, ties and partial credit in online math contests

### Takeaway
In 2025-26, integrity rests on four levers:
- where and how the test is taken: in person, centre-proctored, camera and chaperone, or self-proctored and unofficial;
- the secrecy window: papers released hours before, a release-number embargo, no discussion until the morning after the window;
- rule bans: no remote proctoring, no internet lookup, no generative AI;
- structural design: fixed cutoffs, high stakes moved to a secured round, statistical anomaly detection.

Frontier AI reached IMO gold level in July 2025, and top models score near 100% on AIME-level problems. Any answer-only contest taken unsupervised must therefore be treated as AI-solvable.

### Cited Findings
- AI capability:
  - Google DeepMind's Deep Think and an experimental OpenAI model each solved 5 of 6 problems at IMO 2025, scoring 35/42 (gold level), under contest conditions: two 4.5-hour sessions, no tools.
  - These are the labs' own evaluations, and some observers questioned them.
  - Sources: [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-openai-model-math-olympiad/); [Zvi Mowshowitz](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/google-and-openai-get-2025-imo-gold)
- AIME 2025 is now a saturated AI benchmark. Reported scores include Gemini 2.5 Pro at 86.7%, o3 at 88.9% and Grok 4 at 100%, and a leaderboard lists GPT-5.4 at 98 -- [BenchLM AIME 2025](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/aime2025)
- Leaked AMC papers are solved by hired former olympians "often with generative AI". The Free Press (2026): "Now with AI tools, if an exam is stolen, it can be instantly solved — and sold along with the answers." -- [Lakeside Tatler](https://tatler.lakesideschool.org/8567/showcase/solutions-for-sale-inside-the-discord-marketplaces-undermining-americas-top-math-competitions/)
- Explicit AI ban: Purple Comet forbids ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Claude to produce, explain or verify answers -- [Purple Comet: Changes](https://purplecomet.org/changes)
- Remote supervision: mathleague.org requires a camera, an adult chaperone, a separate keyboard computer, no clicking outside the test window, a password release and hard timer submission -- [mathleague.org online contests](https://mathleague.org/onlinecontests.php)
- MAA:
  - Remote proctoring is banned, with scores cancelled.
  - There is a discussion embargo until 8 a.m. ET the day after the window.
  - Unmatched print-and-scan sheets go to manual review.
  - Sources: [2025-26 AMC 8 Teachers Manual](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-26-AMC-8-Teachers-Manual.pdf); [2025 AMC 10/12 Teachers Manual](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-AMC-10AMC-12-Teachers-Manual.pdf)
  - 2023: "human resources and artificial intelligence" anomaly detection, plus a site ban -- [Saratoga Falcon](https://saratogafalcon.org/19217/features/amc-12a-test-leak-underscores-new-era-of-low-risk-high-reward-cheating/)
  - 2026-27: the AIME at Pearson centres on test-centre computers, with fixed AMC cutoffs -- [MAA news](https://maa.org/news/maa-announces-official-testing-centers-for-the-2027-american-invitational-mathematics-examination/)
- The MAA's December 2025 statement says it keeps some security measures secret: "some efforts are not shared publicly, as doing so would compromise their effectiveness" -- [Lakeside Tatler](https://tatler.lakesideschool.org/8567/showcase/solutions-for-sale-inside-the-discord-marketplaces-undermining-americas-top-math-competitions/)
- Staggered testing times "have enabled cheating for years", which motivated the single-window AIME at centres (Harker Aquila, Sep 2026) -- [Harker Aquila](https://harkeraquila.com/99024/science-and-technology/maa-overhauls-aime-rules-over-cheating-concerns/)
- Partial credit and ties:
  - AMC: 1.5 points per blank ([Math Prep Pro](https://www.mathpreppro.com/blog/amc-10-2025-guide)).
  - Math Kangaroo: no negative marking, with questions weighted 3/4/5 by difficulty ([Think Academy](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/?p=5573)).
  - MATHCOUNTS: Target answers count double, and the Countdown bracket decides final placings among the top written finishers ([AoPS Wiki](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/MathCounts)).
  - MathDash olympiads: a problem's value is scaled by the proof's score out of 7 ([Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/blog/Nsah159)).
  - AtCoder: ties broken by time, with penalty time for wrong answers ([AtCoder](https://atcoder.jp/contests/ajo2026heuristic-qual/rules)).
  - Codeforces: 50 points per failed or re-submission ([Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/65949)).
- Unofficial tiers: unproctored modes get no official rank. Math Kangaroo's "Virtual (Self-Proctored)" gives only a ranking range ([Think Academy](https://www.thethinkacademy.com/blog/?p=5573)), and Noetic "At-Home" scores are unofficial ([Math Explorers Club](https://www.mathexplorersclub.com/noetic-math-contest-at-home)).

### Inferences
For a tutoring platform's "challenges and clashes" in 2026:
- Live head-to-head play with 30-45 s per problem is the most AI-resistant online format. The answer window is too short to copy into a chatbot comfortably, though not impossible. It is no accident that MATHCOUNTS Countdown and FTW use it.
- Same-set timed tournaments taken unsupervised should be treated like Math Kangaroo's self-proctored tier: a score and percentile or ranking range, no prizes. Alternatively, run them under a tutor's live supervision, as mathleague.org does with a chaperone and camera.
- Per-participant problem variants and a randomised order reduce answer-sharing within a window. Question banks with an exact-answer checker make variants cheap. No contest in this research was confirmed to use per-student variants; this is an inference, not an observed practice.
- Fixed thresholds ("100 qualifies") rather than relative ranks keep a cheater from displacing honest students. This is directly copyable for badges and qualifying levels.
- Speed-based scoring needs a tiebreak rule: earlier time, fewer wrong attempts, or a sudden-death Countdown. Every platform surveyed uses one of these three.

### Gaps
- No source found describes per-student problem variants or randomisation at any major K-12 math contest (the AMC 2025 randomised-order claim is unverified).
- No published policy on AI was found for the AMC, MATHCOUNTS or Math Kangaroo, beyond general cheating rules.
- Detection methods (statistical anomaly detection, answer-pattern analysis) are not described publicly by any organiser; the MAA explicitly keeps them confidential.
- Lockdown browsers: no K-12 math contest was found requiring one. mathleague.org relies on camera plus chaperone, and the MAA on in-person proctors and, for the 2027 AIME, test-centre devices.
